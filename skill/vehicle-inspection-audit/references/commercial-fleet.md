# Commercial, Fleet & Specialty Vehicle Inspections (US)

As of 2026-10-20. Regulations change; re-check primary sources before relying on a number. [UNVERIFIED] = not confirmed from a primary source (research search budget ran out; eCFR/FMCSA blocked automated fetch, so Part 396 text came from Cornell LII). Paid standards (CVSA OOS handbook, TMC RP Manual, NFPA 1910/1900, CAAS GVS) are summarized only from public descriptions.

**Why this file exists:** a commercial or for-hire report is checked against a *regulatory floor* — specific numbers, required items, a qualified inspector, retention periods. Flag errors in **both directions**: a "fail/replace" on a component that meets the federal minimum presented as if regulation required it, and a "pass" on one that doesn't meet it.

**Naming:** the FMCSA periodic inspection standard is **Appendix A to Part 396** (formerly "Appendix G to Subchapter B"; redesignated 2021–2022, 86 FR 57068/57077, 86 FR 62111, 87 FR 59037). Reports citing "Appendix G" mean the same standard.

## 1. Recognize the vehicle and inspection type

### Is it a federally regulated CMV? (49 CFR 390.5 / 390.5T, interstate)
Any one of:
1. GVWR, GCWR or actual gross weight **≥10,001 lb** (whichever greater) — check door-jamb label
2. Designed/used to carry **>8 passengers incl. driver for compensation** (9+)
3. Designed/used to carry **>15 passengers incl. driver without compensation** (16+)
4. Carrying **placardable hazmat**

- [UNVERIFIED: Cornell shows §390.5 suspended, temporary §390.5T in effect; definitions believed identical — confirm section number before citing.]
- Intrastate-only → state law. Many states adopt FMCSRs with changes; some use **26,001 lb** instead of 10,001 (e.g., Texas, CA BIT (k)).
- Part 396 exemptions: some provisions for private nonbusiness passenger carriers; DVIRs not required for driveaway-towaway or single-CMV carriers (396.11).
- Government-operated emergency vehicles are generally outside Part 396 → expect NFPA 1910 / state EMS checklists.
- A ~9,000 lb GVWR pickup used intrastate is **not** a CMV — "failed DOT inspection" is wrong framing (state rules may still apply).

### Which inspection is this? (each has its own scope and standard)

| Type | Who / rule | Judge against |
|---|---|---|
| DVIR (post-trip) / pre-trip | Driver; 396.11 / 396.13 | 11 DVIR items; defect closeout |
| Systematic PM | Carrier; 396.3 | Carrier schedule, safe operating condition, fleet PM policy |
| Annual / periodic | Carrier or agent; 396.17 + Appendix A, or state equivalent (Texas, CA BIT 90-day) | Appendix A reject limits |
| Roadside | Enforcement; CVSA Level I–VIII | CVSA OOS criteria (imminent hazard) |
| For-hire | CPUC 19-point (CA TNC/TCP), NYC TLC, city TNC forms | That checklist |
| Emissions | CARB Clean Truck Check | OBD or opacity + visual |
| Fire apparatus | NFPA 1910 | Paid standard / dept policy |
| School bus | CHP 13-month cert; carrier 45-day/3,000 mi; NSTSP-based state programs | State checklist / NSTSP OOS |

**Don't** judge a DVIR against Appendix A, or call a PM a "DOT inspection."

### Which standard does each finding cite? (different thresholds, most to least strict)
Fleet PM pull point / TMC RP / OEM spec  ≥  **Appendix A** (annual reject)  ≥  **§393** operational limits  ≥  **CVSA OOS** (imminent hazard).
A vehicle can pass OOS roadside, fail the annual standard, and both sit below the fleet's pull points. Reports should name the standard.

## 2. Part 396 rules

### 396.3 Systematic inspection, repair, maintenance
- Carriers and intermodal equipment providers (IEPs) must systematically inspect/repair/maintain all controlled vehicles (or have it done); parts in safe operating condition at all times — Part 393 equipment plus frames, suspension, axles, wheels, rims, steering.
- **Buses:** pushout windows, emergency doors, emergency door marking lights inspected at least **every 90 days**.
- Records for each vehicle controlled ≥30 consecutive days: ID (company number, make, serial, year, tire size, owner if not carrier); schedule of inspection/maintenance type and due date; dates and nature of inspections/repairs/maintenance; bus emergency-exit tests.

### 396.11 / 396.13 DVIR and pre-trip
- Driver prepares written post-trip report at end of each day's work, per vehicle.
- **11 required items:** service brakes incl. trailer brake connections; parking brake; steering mechanism; lighting devices and reflectors; tires; horn; windshield wipers; rear vision mirrors; coupling devices; wheels and rims; emergency equipment.
- **No-defect reports not required:** property carriers since **2014-12-18**; passenger carriers since **2020-09-17** (85 FR 50787).
- **Corrective action:** before next use, carrier repairs any listed defect likely to affect safety and **certifies on the DVIR** it was repaired or repair unnecessary. Next driver reviews and signs (396.13).
- Electronic DVIRs allowed (390.32).
- Roadside inspection reports (396.9): certify corrections and return within 15 days. [UNVERIFIED; re-check 396.9(d)]

### 396.17 / 396.21 / 396.23 Annual (periodic) inspection
- Every CMV and **each unit of a combination** (tractor, semitrailer, full trailer, converter dolly) must have passed all Appendix A items within the **preceding 12 months**.
- Proof on vehicle: the report, or a decal showing date, name/address of report keeper, vehicle ID if not marked, and pass certification.
- Performed by carrier or an agent (commercial garage, leasing company, truck stop); inspector must meet 396.19; **carrier stays responsible**.
- State periodic program meeting Appendix A counts 12 months from **end of month** inspected; a mandatory state program FMCSA finds as effective replaces 396.17 (396.23).
- **Report contents (396.21):** (1) inspector identity; (2) carrier/IEP; (3) date; (4) vehicle ID; (5) components inspected and results, incl. any failing Appendix A; (6) certification that it is accurate and complete. Retain **14 months**; carrier must get a copy if someone else inspected.
- A form without component-by-component results, or a decal with no traceable report, does not meet 396.21.

## 4. Appendix A numeric reject criteria (annual inspection)

Paraphrased reject thresholds. Source: 49 CFR Appendix A to Part 396 [AppA].

### Brakes
**Pushrod stroke at or beyond readjustment limit = defect.** Measure engine off, reservoir **80–90 psi**, brakes fully applied.

| Chamber | Standard stroke limit | Long stroke limit |
|---|---|---|
| Clamp Type 6 | 1-1/4 in | — |
| Clamp Type 30 | 2 in | 2-1/2 in |
| Bendix DD-3 Type 30 | 2-1/4 in | — |
| Rotochamber Type 50 | 3 in | — |

Other chambers have their own limits (pull full table from eCFR). Unlisted actuators fail above 80% of rated stroke or marked limit.

**Lining/pad thickness — reject if less than:**

| Axle | Brake type | Appendix A reject | §393.47 roadside/operational (for contrast) |
|---|---|---|---|
| Steering | Air drum | 1/4 in at shoe center | **3/16 in** (continuous-strip lining) |
| Steering | Air disc | 1/8 in | 1/8 in |
| Steering | Hydraulic disc / electric | 1/16 in or less | — |
| Non-steering | Air drum | 1/4 in at shoe center | — |
| Non-steering | Air disc | 1/8 in | — |
| Non-steering | Hydraulic / electric drum | 1/16 in or less | — |

Don't confuse Appendix A with §393.47 [JJK-393.47].

**Other brake criteria**
- Wedge brakes: scribe-mark movement >1/16 in
- Low-air warning must activate at ≤55 psi or half governor cut-out, whichever less
- Hydraulic master cylinder <1/4 full
- Parking brake won't apply; cracked drums/rotors; hose damage exposing outer reinforcement ply; missing/broken shoes, linings, pads, springs, anchor pins, spiders, cam rollers, pushrods, chamber bolts; audible air leaks at chambers; insufficient vacuum reserve; ABS lamp and automatic adjuster requirements

### Coupling — reject if more than

| Item | Limit |
|---|---|
| Fifth wheel horizontal movement upper/lower halves | 1/2 in |
| Fifth wheel pivot bracket pin movement | 3/8 in |
| Fifth wheel slider movement | 3/8 in |
| Pintle hook horn section / drawbar eye wear | 20% |
| Safety chains | Any measurable link cross-section reduction |
| Saddle-mount horizontal movement | 1/4 in |
| Drawbar tongue slider/subframe movement | 1/4 in |

### Steering
**Steering-wheel lash — reject if more than** (more diameters exist; pull full table):

| Wheel diameter | Manual | Power |
|---|---|---|
| 16 in | 2 in | 4-1/2 in |
| 18 in | 2-1/4 in | 4-3/4 in |
| 20 in | 2-1/2 in | 5-1/4 in |
| 22 in | 2-3/4 in | 5-3/4 in |

- Ball/socket joints: >1/4 in non-rotational movement at any linkage point
- Loose column, gearbox mounting, U-joint wear [UNVERIFIED in detail]

### Tires

| Position | Reject below |
|---|---|
| Steering-axle tires on power units | **4/32 in** in any major groove |
| All other tires | **2/32 in** |

Also reject: exposed ply/belt; tread or sidewall separation; cuts to ply; overloading; flat or audible leak; "Not for highway use"; bias/radial mixed on one axle; regrooved steer tires (urban/suburban bus exceptions); boot or blowout patch; recaps/retreads on bus front wheels.

### Other categories

| Category | Reject when |
|---|---|
| Exhaust | Leak forward of/below driver or sleeper; bus outlet (gasoline) >6 in forward of rearmost point; (other fuels) >15 in forward of rearmost point or forward of any openable door/window (emergency exits excepted) |
| Fuel | Visible leak; missing filler cap; tank not secure |
| Lighting | Any Part 393-required lamp/reflector inoperative |
| Safe loading | Spare/cargo could fall; missing required front-end structure; defective intermodal container securement |
| Suspension | Broken/missing leaves; broken main leaf; broken coil; missing rubber spring; deflated air suspension; axle out of position (cracked/broken U-bolt, spring hanger) |
| Frame | Cracked/broken/loose/sagging members; loose/missing fasteners; body/frame contacting tire or wheel |
| Wheels/rims | Cracks/breaks; elongated stud holes; loose/missing fasteners; cracked welds; welded repairs on aluminum steer wheels |
| Windshield | Excluded zones: 2 in top, 1 in sides, below top of steering wheel. Crack OK only if ≤1/4 in wide and not intersected by another crack. Damage (chip) OK only if ≤3/4 in diameter and ≥3 in from other damage |
| Wipers | Inoperative/ineffective on power unit |
| Motorcoach seats | Any passenger seat not securely fastened |
| Rear impact guard | Trailers/semis ≥10,001 lb GVWR built on/after 1998-01-26: within 4 in of each side, ≤22 in above ground, ≤12 in forward of rear, ≥4 in vertical section. Older (post-1952): ≤30 in above ground, within 18 in of sides, ≤24 in forward of rear |

**Audit relevance:** recommending replacement "per DOT" of steer tires at 5/32", drive tires at 4/32", or air-drum linings at 3/8" is maintenance *above* the federal floor. That may be sound preventive practice (fleet pull points, TMC), but it is **not legally required** and the report must not call the vehicle "failed" or "out of service" on that basis → typically **Supported – optional**.

## 6. CVSA levels, decals, OOS

| Level | Name | Covers |
|---|---|---|
| I | North American Standard | Full driver credentials/HOS + full vehicle incl. under-vehicle |
| II | Walk-Around | Driver + vehicle items checkable without getting under (secondary source) |
| III | Driver-Only | Credentials, HOS; no mechanical |
| IV | Special | One-time look at a particular item (study/trend) |
| V | Vehicle-Only | Level I vehicle items, no driver; any location (e.g., terminal) |
| VI | Enhanced NAS | Transuranic / highway-route-controlled radioactive shipments |
| VII | Jurisdictional Mandated | State programs: school buses, limos, taxis, shared-ride, hotel shuttles, intrastate. **No CVSA decal** (jurisdiction decal possible) |
| VIII | Electronic | Wireless while moving, no officer interaction; defined 2017; still in operational test |

**CVSA decal:** only Level I or V with **no critical violations** (Level VI special decal); valid up to **3 consecutive months**; vehicle condition only; **not** the 396.17 annual inspection.

**OOS criteria:** paid CVSA publication; updated annually, effective **April 1**; 2026 edition had **17 changes** (CDL/licensing restrictions, alcohol thresholds, ELD tampering, brakes, cargo securement, placarding). OOS = imminent-hazard thresholds, deliberately more lenient than Appendix A (e.g., commonly cited "20% of service brakes defective" rule [UNVERIFIED; exact wording in paid handbook]).

## 8. State programs

### California BIT (CVC 34501.12, 34505.5)
- **Covered (34500):** (a) 3+ axle motortrucks >10,000 lb GVWR; (b) truck tractors; (c) buses, school buses, SPABs, youth buses, farm labor vehicles, modified limousines, general public paratransit; (e) trailers/dollies used with them; (f) motortruck combos >40 ft; (g) hazmat vehicles; (k) CMVs ≥26,001 lb or towing trailer >10,000 lb; others.
- **AB 3278 (eff. 2025-01-01)** removed subdivision **(j)** ("other motortrucks regulated by DMV, DCA, or USDOT") from BIT and the 90-day rule. **Conflict:** one secondary summary describes (j) as hazmat trucks; the statute lists hazmat under (g). Read the CHP bulletin.
- **Terminal inspections:** since 2016 selected by performance (FMCSA SMS BASIC percentiles); unsatisfactory → reinspect within **120 days**; review request within 5 business days, CHP decides within 10. Sample by fleet size: 1–2 = all; 3–8 = 3; 9–15 = 4; 16–25 = 6; 26–50 = 9; 51–90 = 14; 91+ = 20. Sunsets **2031-01-01** unless extended.
- **90-day self-inspection (34505.5):** vehicles in (a), (b), (e), (f), (g), (k); at least **every 90 days** as part of maintenance/lube program. Minimum: brake adjustment; brake components and leaks; steering and suspension; tires and wheels; connecting devices. Defective vehicle driven only to repair; carrier rep signs off before return. Records: vehicle ID, date and nature of each inspection/repair, signature; keep **2 years** at terminal.

### California school buses
- **Only CHP** inspects/certifies school buses, SPABs, youth buses; certificate dated **within 13 months** (CVC 2807, 2807.1, 2807.3).
- Carrier inspects each bus every **3,000 miles or 45 days**, whichever first; minimum brake adjustment, tires, steering (13 CCR 1232).

### Texas (post-2025)
- HB 3297 ended **non-commercial** safety inspections 2025-01-01. **All commercial vehicles in all counties still need a passing safety inspection** (plus emissions where applicable); exempt from $7.50 replacement fee.
- **Texas commercial vehicle (DPS page dated 2026-03-19):** GVWR >26,000 lb; farm vehicle ≥48,000 lb; 15+ passengers [note: source wording; compare federal 16+ unpaid / 9+ paid]; placarded hazmat; GVWR >10,000 lb interstate; school bus operating at 60 mph outside an urban district.
- **Items (if originally equipped):** backup lamps; beam indicator; coupling devices; emission/exhaust; frame; fuel system and gas cap; headlamps; horn; insurance; license plate lamp; mirrors; parking and service brakes; rear reflector; seat belts; steering; stop/tail/turn lamps; suspension; tires; wheel assembly; window tint; windshield and wipers. >80 in wide adds clearance/ID/side marker lamps and reflectors, rear impact guard, splash guards. School buses add crossover mirrors, fire extinguisher, red warning lamps, signage.
- Treated as **equivalent** to the federal annual inspection, except intrastate farm vehicles <48,000 lb, covered farm vehicles, government/emergency vehicles, out-of-state IRP vehicles with valid federal annual. [UNVERIFIED — J.J. Keller summary only]

### NSTSP (school transportation)
- **2025 edition** (May 2025 National Congress, Des Moines), first update since 2015; has "School Bus Inspections" section and **recommended school bus OOS criteria**.
- Adoption examples: **Minnesota** (169.451) — bus with an NSTSP OOS defect is unsafe to transport students; **South Dakota** cites it for daily driver inspections; **Florida** publishes its own 2025 School Bus Safety Inspection Manual.

## 9. Rideshare / TNC

### California (CPUC) — TNC and TCP (incl. limousines)
- **19-point inspection at a BAR-licensed facility** before first service and every **12 months or 50,000 miles**, whichever first (D.13-09-045, D.16-04-041, GO 157-E §4.05). Records kept **3 years**.
- **19 items:** 1 foot brakes; 2 emergency brake; 3 steering mechanism; 4 windshield; 5 rear window/other glass; 6 wipers; 7 headlights; 8 taillights; 9 turn indicators; 10 stop lights; 11 front seat adjustment; 12 doors (open/close/lock); 13 horn; 14 speedometer; 15 bumpers; 16 muffler/exhaust; 17 tire condition incl. tread depth; 18 interior/exterior mirrors; 19 safety belts driver and passengers.
- No numeric tread limit on the CPUC form [UNVERIFIED whether CPUC sets one].
- Enforcement: Resolution TEB-001 (2026-02-09) approved **$3.5M** Uber consent order; Uber admitted 1,270 annual-inspection violations (2019–2022). Uber petition to change the 50,000-mile trigger pending as of Feb 2026.

## 13. Audit checks and red flags

### Annual inspection report completeness (396.21) — checklist
- [ ] Inspector named; carrier/IEP identified
- [ ] Date within past 12 months at time of use (state programs: from end of month)
- [ ] Vehicle ID (VIN/unit); **each unit of a combination has its own report**
- [ ] **Every Appendix A category** has a result: brakes, coupling, exhaust, fuel, lighting, safe loading, steering, suspension, frame, tires, wheels/rims, windshield, wipers (+ motorcoach seats, rear impact guard where applicable). Missing categories → incomplete
- [ ] **Measured values** where a numeric limit exists: stroke by chamber type/size, lining thickness, tread per position, steering lash. Bare "OK" = weak evidence
- [ ] Certification statement and signature
- [ ] Decal or report on vehicle; decal details match report
- [ ] Retained 14 months

### Qualified inspector checks
- [ ] Annual inspector 396.19 evidence on file (except state program inspections)
- [ ] Brake tech 396.25 evidence on file
- [ ] CA TNC/TCP: BAR license number on form
- [ ] Clean Truck Check: CARB tester ID
- Inspector signature = person who sold the repairs, with no qualification evidence → not illegal, but note as conflict of interest

### Accuracy red flags (suggested status in parentheses)
- **"DOT requires replacement"** above the regulatory limit — steer tires ≥5/32", drive/trailer ≥3/32", air-drum linings ≥5/16" — unless a fleet policy or OEM spec is cited (**Supported – optional** if reasonable PM; flag the "required" framing)
- **Steer-axle lining limit misstated** — Appendix A 1/4" drum vs §393.47 3/16" drum; check which the report claims (**Inconsistent**)
- **"Out of adjustment" brake** with no stroke value, no chamber size, or not measured at 80–90 psi fully applied (**Evidence missing**; still **Verify in person – safety** if credible)
- **Manual readjustment of an automatic slack adjuster** as the "fix" for over-stroke with no root-cause diagnosis (cam, bushings, adjuster) — improper practice per industry guidance
- **Steering lash** "excessive" without wheel diameter or power/manual (**Needs OEM spec**/**Evidence missing**)
- **Wheel-end bearing play** "excessive" without dial-indicator end-play value (RP 618 target 0.001–0.005 in)
- **Windshield crack** failed though in excluded border zones, or ≤1/4" and not intersecting (**Inconsistent**)
- **"Failed DOT inspection"** on a non-CMV (**Not applicable to this vehicle** for federal framing; check state rules)
- **Decal with no matching report**; backdated report; every component "pass" with zero findings on a high-mileage unit while the same work order sells repairs on those components (**Inconsistent**)
- **CVSA misused** — "out of service" for a non-OOS defect; CVSA decal presented as annual inspection
- **School bus** — a shop "certifying" a CA school bus (only CHP issues the annual certificate; shop does 45-day/3,000-mi carrier inspections)
- **TNC (CA)** — non-BAR facility; >12 months or >50,000 mi since last; any of the 19 points missing
- **Clean Truck Check** — uncredentialed tester; OBD-eligible vehicle smoke-tested instead; frequency misrepresented to justify extra tests
- **Invoice vs findings** — repairs billed on passed items; failed items neither repaired nor documented as declined

### Fleet manager
- Completeness and defensibility: every Appendix A item with measurements on annuals.
- DVIR defects closed with signed repair certification before dispatch.
- Inspector and brake-tech qualification files current; retention clocks met (3 mo / 14 mo / 1 yr / +1 yr).
- Check above-floor recommendations against your PM policy (often TMC-style pull points) rather than accepting them as "required."

### Shop owner (auditing a technician)
- If doing carrier annuals or brake work: confirm tech meets 396.19 and 396.25.
- Report lists measured values, cited standard, photos.
- Every "fail" maps to a specific Appendix A or §393 criterion; every advisory labeled **preventive, not regulatory**.
- Invoice matches findings; labor consistent with inspection scope.

### Consumer (driver / owner-operator / rideshare driver)
- Know your checklist: 11-item DVIR; CPUC 19 points; your city's TNC form.
- Ask which standard each failure uses, and for the measurement.
- A tire/brake "fail" above the legal minimum is a recommendation, not a legal requirement.
- For TNC, what the platform/regulator needs is a pass at a properly licensed facility (BAR in CA). Don't let the TNC visit become an upsell of unrelated maintenance.

# Federal Law for Repair Inspections, Estimates & Invoices

As of 2026-10-20 (bill status, EPA tampering/converter policy, TCPA revocation and ADAS items re-checked against primary sources 2026-10-21). Federal law moved a lot in 2025–2026 (EPA tampering enforcement, right-to-repair bills). Treat [UNVERIFIED] items as leads. Not legal advice.

**Key framing:** There is **no general federal auto-repair-fraud statute and no federal written-estimate rule.** State repair acts (see `state-repair-laws.md`, `california-bar.md`) are the main consumer law for shops. Federal law matters for emissions parts, A/C refrigerant, safety systems, recalls, tires, odometers, warranties, e-signatures and texting.

## 2. Emissions tampering / defeat devices

**Rule — CAA §203(a)(3), 42 U.S.C. 7522(a)(3)** [7522]
- (A) No removing or making inoperative an emission-control device/element of design (after first sale: knowing conduct).
- (B) No making, selling, offering or installing a part whose principal effect is to bypass/defeat/disable emission controls, where the person knows or should know it is used that way.
- Repair carve-out: owners need not use OEM parts; temporarily disabling a device to repair/replace and then restoring it is not tampering. The exception is the flush text at the end of 7522(a); EPA's July 1, 2026 letter (IACD-2026-08) cites 7522(a) and 40 CFR 1068.101(b)(1)(i)–(ii) for it [7522, EPA-IACD].
- **Customer consent and "off-road use" are not defenses.** Each vehicle or part is a separate violation.

**Penalties (42 U.S.C. 7524; 40 CFR 19.4)**

| Violator | As enacted | Inflation-adjusted (assessed on/after 2025-01-08) |
|---|---|---|
| Non-manufacturer/dealer, (3)(A), per vehicle | $2,500 | **$5,911** |
| Any person, (3)(B) defeat device, per part | $2,500 | **$5,911** |
| Manufacturer/dealer, (3)(A), per vehicle | $25,000 | **$59,114** |

**EPA Tampering Policy (Nov 23, 2020):** not tampering if a *reasonable basis* exists to believe emissions are unaffected (restoring certified configuration, parts-maker equivalence claim, testing, EPA certification, CARB EO). Civil enforcement focus: defeat-device makers/sellers, fleet tamperers, and **service shops that routinely delete** emissions equipment. The reasonable-basis statement **does not apply to OBD-system conduct** (enforceable regardless of emissions effect). A shop that restores an already-tampered system to certified configuration, or declines the job, generally faces no action. Federal only; state laws still apply. **Still in effect as of 2026-10-21:** EPA's enforcement-policy list shows no withdrawal, and nothing in the 2026 memo or EPA letters changed the OBD carve-out [EPA-TP, EPA-list].

**2025–2026 enforcement climate**

| Date | Event |
|---|---|
| Dec 2025 | EPA OECA "Compliance First" approach [Hunton] |
| Jan 2026 | DOJ drops criminal CAA tampering charges; pending OBD criminal cases dismissed. **Civil enforcement continues**; 5-year limitations period; states still enforce [Hunton, A&P] |
| 2025–26 | Presidential pardons for several convicted diesel tuners [The Drive] |
| Jun 29, 2026 | "Freedom to Fix" memo: EPA to clarify policy, speed aftermarket conformity testing, *consider* deprioritizing civil enforcement against **individuals restoring their own vehicle** to original configuration (§4; a direction to consider, not a binding change). **Does not cover shop-performed deletes.** No EPA document formally adopting that deprioritization found as of 2026-10-21 [WH, EPA-F2F] |
| Jul 1, 2026 | EPA letter IACD-2026-08 (OAR + OECA): repairs lawful if certified configuration restored before return to service; OEMs must give owners and independents the same emission service info/tools/training as dealers. Letter has no force of law. Separately, SEMA SC-E program recognized as an alternative to CARB for aftermarket-part testing; non-certified parts may lack warranty protection [EPA-IACD, EPA, RDN] |

**Bottom line:** the statute is unchanged (only Congress can change it). Criminal risk low now; civil and state risk remain, especially for shops.

## 3. Catalytic converter replacement

Source: EPA aftermarket converter policy, 51 FR 28114 (Aug 5, 1986) and fact sheet [EPA-cat].

**Aftermarket (non-OE) converter allowed only if:**
1. Converter is missing; or
2. A state/local I/M program found it damaged/needing replacement; or
3. Vehicle is past 5 yr/50k mi (**8 yr/80k mi for MY1995+**) **and** a legitimate need is documented (e.g., plugged, unrepairable exhaust leaks).

Otherwise use OE or a certified equivalent.

**Aftermarket not allowed when:** existing converter is present and working; replacement is covered by recall or warranty; vehicle returning from overseas.

**Installer duties**

| Requirement | Detail |
|---|---|
| Signed statement | If no I/M program required it, customer **and** installer sign a statement of the reason |
| Invoice content | Customer name and full address, make, model year, mileage, reason for replacement |
| Retention | Invoices + statements **6 months**; removed converter tagged to customer **15 days** |
| Installation | Same type (OC/TWC/TWC+OC), original location, connect any air injection, replace all original converters unless part rated to replace several; correct catalog application; fill out warranty card |
| Labeling | New: label N/XX/YYYY/ZZZZ; 5 yr/50k shell and 25k performance warranty. Used: must be tested and labeled U/… |
| Prohibited | Untested salvage converter, or one the customer brings in |

- OE converter on MY1995+ is under the **federal emissions warranty 8 yr/80k mi** → send to dealer at no cost.
- 2020–21: EPA requested information on updating/withdrawing the 1986 policy, and state air agencies asked EPA to replace it. **Not revised or withdrawn as of 2026-10-21**: EPA still lists it (plus a 2004 policy for OBD-II vehicles); the RFI page announces no decision; SEMA SC-E recognition (July 2026) doesn't mention converters. The 2020 Tampering Policy would govern catalysts only if EPA withdraws the 1986 policy [EPA-list, EPA-TP].
- California and some other states require CARB-approved converters (state files).

## 4. A/C refrigerant

**Section 609 (CAA §609; 40 CFR 82 Subpart B)**
- Anyone servicing MVAC **for consideration** must use EPA-approved recover/recycle equipment and be certified by an EPA-approved program (e.g., ASE, MACS, ESCO) (82.34(a)).
- Shop must certify to EPA it owns/uses approved equipment (make, model, serial) and techs are certified (82.42(a)).
- Equipment must meet 82.36; recovered refrigerant must be reclaimed/recycled before reuse (82.34(d)–(e)).
- R-134a and R-1234yf need separate dedicated equipment and fittings. [UNVERIFIED: SAE J2788/J2843 citations not re-checked.]

**AIM Act Technology Transitions (40 CFR 84; final rule Oct 24, 2023)**

| Vehicle class | GWP ≤150 required from |
|---|---|
| New light-duty passenger vehicles | **MY2025** (no earlier than 2024-10-24) — effectively ends R-134a (GWP ~1,430) → R-1234yf |
| Medium-duty passenger, HD pickups, complete HD vans | MY2028 |
| Listed nonroad equipment | 2028-01-01 |

- May 26, 2026 reconsideration rule (91 FR 31284) does **not** appear to change MVAC dates. [H&K]
- Applies to manufacturing new vehicles; servicing existing R-134a vehicles with R-134a remains lawful. [UNVERIFIED: no servicing restriction confirmed in rule text.]
- SNAP (CAA §612) governs acceptable substitutes. Retrofitting a yf system to 134a or an unlisted refrigerant is a red flag. [UNVERIFIED: SNAP listings not re-fetched.]

## 5. State I/M programs (EPA role)

40 CFR 51 Subpart S [51.350–.369]
- Required in certain ozone/CO nonattainment areas; EPA sets minimum design, states run them.
- MY1996+ LDVs: OBD check (40 CFR 85.2222) may replace tailpipe test. Unset readiness monitors or missing/leaking exhaust can cause rejection from testing (51.357).
- Retest requires a repair form listing **repairs performed, repairs recommended but not performed, and the facility** (51.369(b)). Enhanced programs publish repair-facility performance.
- Waivers require a "recognized repair technician"; **tampering-related repair spending does not count toward the waiver minimum** (51.360).

## 6. NHTSA: make-inoperative and FMVSS-required systems

**49 U.S.C. 30122:** manufacturers, distributors, dealers, rental companies and **motor vehicle repair businesses** (anyone repairing for pay) may not knowingly make inoperative any part of a device/element installed to comply with an FMVSS — except when reasonably believing the vehicle won't be driven while inoperative (other than testing during repair).
- Exemption: 49 CFR 595 disability modifications — modifier labels vehicle, gives owner a document, keeps copy **5 years**; only listed FMVSS may be affected (595.7).
- FMVSS set **manufacturing** performance standards; they generally don't set in-service wear limits for cars.

| System | FMVSS-required? | Note |
|---|---|---|
| Air bags, seat belts | Yes — FMVSS 208/209 | Resistor "fixes", bypassed pretensioners = risk |
| Rear-visibility camera | Yes — FMVSS 111 | Left disconnected after body/glass work = risk |
| TPMS | Yes — FMVSS 138 | "TPMS delete"/programming off = risk |
| ESC | Yes — FMVSS 126 | |
| Lighting | Yes — FMVSS 108 | |
| AEB | **Not yet** — FMVSS 127 compliance date **2029-09-01**, under industry challenge; no 2026 change found | |
| Lane-keeping, adaptive cruise, most ADAS | No | Skipped calibration = state-law/negligence/OEM-procedure issue, not a clear §30122 violation [UNVERIFIED: no NHTSA interpretation found in nhtsa.gov interpretation searches, 2026-10-21. A 2015 repair-shop letter (seen only via search summary; page 403) says shops violate if they should have known a modification disables a required system, and should return systems working at least as well as received [NHTSA-2015]] |

- No federal ADAS calibration standard. H.R. 7389 §221 (not enacted) would order an NHTSA study.

## 7. Counterfeit air bags

- No federal statute specifically on installing counterfeit air bags found. [UNVERIFIED; 18 U.S.C. 2320 and §30122 may apply.] States filling the gap (e.g., Idaho 2026). [RDN]
- NHTSA warnings (2012, 2024, Jan 2026): counterfeit/substandard Chinese replacement inflators, notably **DTN**, linked to deaths. Inspect any vehicle with prior deployment; prioritize salvage/rebuilt titles. **Scan tools cannot reliably detect counterfeits.** [NHTSA]

## 8. Recalls

49 U.S.C. 30118–30120
- Dealers may not sell/lease **new** vehicles/equipment in inventory under recall until remedied (30120(i)). Rental companies ground covered vehicles within 24/48 hours.
- No one may sell recalled replacement **equipment, including tires,** for installation unless remedied (30120(j)).
- Free remedy does not apply if vehicle first sold **>15 years** before recall, tire **>5 years** (30120(g)).
- **No federal duty for independent shops (or used-vehicle dealers) to check for or perform open recalls.** Remedies are done free by OEM-authorized dealers. [UNVERIFIED as a negative.]
- FTC acted against deceptive recall-themed service mailers (2018) and used-car "inspection" ads not disclosing open recalls (2016).
- H.R. 7389 would allow electronic recall notices (not enacted).

## 9. Tire registration and mis-cited tread numbers

**49 CFR 574.8:** selling new tires, independent dealers must either give the buyer a registration form pre-filled with full TIN and dealer identity, or register the tires themselves (paper/electronic). Manufacturer-owned dealers record and submit TINs.
- Missing TINs on a new-tire invoice = documentation gap that also blocks recall notification.

**Mis-cited numbers:** 49 CFR 393.75 (FMCSA, **commercial vehicles only**): 4/32" steer tires on trucks/buses, 2/32" elsewhere; no exposed ply, separation, or audible leak. "Below 4/32" on a passenger car is **not** a federal violation — passenger-car limits come from state inspection law and OEM specs. Tire-age guidance is OEM/industry, not federal.

## 10. Odometer after cluster work

49 U.S.C. 32701 et seq.; 49 CFR 580
- During service/repair/replacement of an odometer or cluster, the reading must stay the same. If impossible, set to **zero** and attach a written notice to the **left door frame** with prior mileage and date of service. Removing/altering that notice with intent to defraud is prohibited (32704).
- Rollback/alteration separately prohibited (32703). Transfer disclosures under Part 580 [not re-fetched].

## 11. FTC

**FTC Act §5 (15 U.S.C. 45(a)):** misrepresenting repair need, billing for work not done, false "inspection" claims are classic deception theories. In practice, repair fraud is enforced mainly by **state AGs and repair bureaus**; no recent FTC unnecessary-repair case found. [UNVERIFIED as a negative.]

FTC consumer guidance (2012) [FTC-basics]: written estimate and approval before exceeding it; itemized RO with odometer in/out; ask for old parts; compare to owner's manual schedule; second opinions incl. diagnostic-only shops; rule out simple causes (connections, terminals) before replacing alternator/battery/starter; "tune-ups" may not apply to modern vehicles.

**Magnuson-Moss anti-tying (15 U.S.C. 2302(c); 16 CFR 700.10)**
- Warrantor can't condition coverage on branded parts or authorized service unless provided free. "Void if serviced by anyone other than an authorized dealer" / "use only [brand] parts" = prohibited and deceptive.
- Warrantor may deny a claim only by showing the non-OEM part/service **caused** the damage.
- CAA (7522) separately confirms owners needn't use OEM parts; EPA July 2026 repeats this for emission parts (caveat: non-certified parts).
- Enforcement: FTC final orders vs Harley-Davidson and MWE (2022-10-27); 8 warning letters July 2024; 2021 repair-restrictions policy statement. No 2025–2026 auto MMWA actions found. [UNVERIFIED]

**Used Car Rule (16 CFR 455):** Buyers Guide required; deceptive to misrepresent mechanical condition/warranty. Relevant when a dealer markets a pre-sale "multi-point inspection." Last major revision 2016. [UNVERIFIED whether amended since.]

**CARS Rule:** **Vacated** by 5th Cir. 2025-01-27 (NADA v. FTC). Never in effect; covered dealer sales/F&I, not repair.

## 12. OSHA and used oil (fee line items)

- **Asbestos, 29 CFR 1910.1001(f)(3) & App. F:** brake/clutch work requires negative-pressure HEPA enclosure or low-pressure/wet method (or equivalent); ≤5 brake/clutch jobs per week may use wet-wipe. No compressed-air blowing without capture. Does **not** justify a brake replacement.
- **Lifts:** no lift-specific OSHA standard; General Duty Clause + ANSI/ALI ALOIM. [UNVERIFIED]
- **Used oil, 40 CFR 279.22:** containers in good condition, labeled "Used Oil," clean up releases. Federal law sets **no fee amount** for hazmat/disposal/shop-supply charges; such a line is legitimate but shouldn't be disguised labor.

## 14. E-SIGN and TCPA basics

**E-SIGN (15 U.S.C. 7001)**
- E-signature/record can't be denied effect *solely* for being electronic → app/text approvals valid in principle.
- Where a law requires info be given to a consumer **in writing**, electronic delivery satisfies it only after E-SIGN consumer consent (paper-copy, withdrawal, hardware/software disclosures; demonstrated ability to access). Records must be retainable/reproducible (7001(e)).
- Many state written-estimate/authorization rules are such "in writing" requirements; state UETA also applies (see `consumer-protection.md`).

**TCPA (47 U.S.C. 227; 47 CFR 64.1200)**
- Autodialed/prerecorded calls and texts need prior express consent; **written** consent for marketing.
- Current rule (since 2025-04-11): revocation by **any reasonable method**; "stop," "quit," "end," "revoke," "opt out," "cancel," "unsubscribe" count per se; honor within **10 business days** (64.1200(a)(10)–(11)).
- **"Revoke-all" never took effect:** waived to 2026-04-11, then to **2027-01-31** (FCC CGB DA 26-12, 2026-01-06) [FCC-DA26-12].
- **FCC 26-67 (adopted 2026-09-30, released 2026-10-01)** rewrites (a)(10)–(12): a revocation of an *informational* message may be applied only to that category (a revocation of marketing still stops all marketing); callers may designate and disclose an **exclusive** opt-out method (automated key-press, reply-text keyword, or designated website/phone), otherwise any reasonable method still counts; 10 business days unchanged. Effective 30 days after Federal Register publication (not yet published as of 2026-10-21); replaces the 2027-01-31 revoke-all date. FNPRM on shorter deadline, two-way texting, optional revoke-all [FCC-26-67].
- Sending the customer's own inspection link for a car they dropped off is generally informational; upsell/"service due" campaigns are marketing.
- FCC one-to-one consent rule was vacated by the 11th Circuit, *Insurance Marketing Coalition v. FCC* (2025-01-24); STOP-revocation rules effective 2025-04-11 — sourced in `consumer-protection.md`.

## 15. Audit checks

### Both audiences

| Area | Request / check | Red flags → suggested status |
|---|---|---|
| Emissions work | Any delete, "tune off," straight pipe, test pipe, O2 spacer/defouler, EGR block-off, DPF/DEF delete, "off-road tune" | Federal tampering red flag regardless of customer request (~$5,911 per vehicle/part civil). O2 simulator/spacer to clear P0420/P0430 = defeat-device pattern. Describe factually; don't accuse |
| Catalytic converter | Documented need (failed efficiency test with fuel trims, O2/AFR data, misfire history ruled out; backpressure test; rattle/plug); age/mileage; 8 yr/80k emissions warranty or recall; EPA/CARB label of installed part; signed reason statement | Aftermarket cat under 8 yr/80k with no I/M order; "universal" part without application match; no customer signature; removed cat not kept/tagged; upstream cause (misfire, oil, coolant) not fixed → **Evidence missing** / **Inconsistent** |
| A/C | §609 cert number/program; recovery machine for refrigerant type; refrigerant matches underhood label; charge weight vs OEM spec; recovery/evacuation/vacuum and leak test listed | "Converted yf to 134a"; sealant top-off undisclosed; charging "by feel"; uncertified tech; recharge-only with no leak test on a low system (leak ignored) |
| Safety systems | SRS scan before/after; part numbers and source for air bag/inflator | SRS light "cleared" with no part; resistor across air-bag connector; pretensioner bypassed; TPMS removed/disabled; backup camera disconnected after body/glass. Unknown/online air bag source or salvage/rebuilt title with prior deployment → **Verify in person – safety** (DTN concern) |
| ADAS | After windshield, bumper, alignment, ride-height work on camera/radar vehicle: OEM-required calibration or documented "not required," with before/after reports | "Calibration performed" with no report. Not a federal mandate — frame as OEM procedure/state law/negligence |
| Recalls | Check VIN at nhtsa.gov/recalls | Paid repair of a component under open recall or extended warranty/service campaign = significant finding (free at dealer unless vehicle >15 yrs) |
| Tires | Full DOT TINs on new-tire invoice; registration handled; DOT date code if "too old" | Missing TINs. "Unsafe below 4/32" on a car is a recommendation, not federal law |
| Odometer/cluster | Cluster line matches "mileage preserved" note or door-frame notice (prior mileage + date); odometer in/out consistent with prior ROs | Large discrepancy = §32703/32704 red flag → **Inconsistent** |
| Warranty claims | "Must do at dealer," "OEM parts or warranty void," "aftermarket filter voids warranty" | False under MMWA unless the item is free under the warranty. Dealer denials must show causation in writing |
| Schedule inflation | Compare to owner's manual schedule for mileage/conditions | Flushes at a fraction of OEM interval; "tune-ups" on coil-on-plug engines → **Beyond OEM schedule** unless a finding justifies it |
| Digital approvals/texting | Each approval: who, when, which lines, amount, channel; E-SIGN consent where state requires writing; new approval for revised amounts | Missing approval for overage; marketing texts after STOP |
| I/M repairs | Repair form lists done **and** recommended-not-done repairs | "You need $X more to qualify for a waiver" — check state waiver limit; tampering repairs don't count |

### Consumer
- Ask for: DVI photos/measurements behind each red item, codes and freeze-frame, refrigerant/tech certification, converter paperwork, tire TINs, old parts (no federal return right; many states require it).
- Free alternatives first: VIN recall check; emissions warranty (8 yr/80k on cat and ECM, MY1995+); warranty work can be done at an independent shop.
- Don't accept "delete"/"off-road" emissions work — exposure for the shop, registration/inspection problems for you.
- Where to complain (point only; don't file): state AG/repair bureau (primary); FTC reportfraud.ftc.gov (warranty tying, deception); EPA "report a violation" (tampering); NHTSA (safety defects, counterfeit air bags).

### Shop owner
- **Keep on file:** §609 certs per A/C tech + equipment certification to EPA (82.42); converter invoices/signed statements 6 months, removed converters tagged 15 days; tire TIN registration records; odometer notices; SRS/ADAS pre/post scans and calibration reports; OSHA brake-dust method; E-SIGN consent records; TCPA consent and opt-out logs (STOP honored ≤10 business days).
- **Hard stops:** any tech recommendation in delete/defeat-device or make-inoperative categories.
- **Policy violations:** converters without documented diagnostics; "warranty void" scripting at the counter (CAA 7524 per-vehicle/part civil exposure; FTC §5/MMWA).
- **Advisor scripts:** never say independent/aftermarket parts void warranties or that recalls must be paid for; disclose open recalls found; tag OEM-required vs shop-recommended vs condition-based items separately (makes "beyond need" auditable).
- **Climate:** don't relax policy because of pardons or the DOJ memo — civil enforcement continues, 5-year limitations period runs, states active, and Freedom to Fix relief targets individual owners, not shops.

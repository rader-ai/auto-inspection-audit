## 2. Recurring practice patterns

1. **Quotas and commissions/bonuses tied to parts and labor sold** (Sears; quick-lube ex-employees) — the textbook upsell driver.
2. **Scare tactics** — false/exaggerated condition or safety statements (Midas injunction; PA Mavis).
3. **Low-price entry offer → unneeded add-ons** (brake specials, free checks, oil changes: Midas, Firestone, Pep Boys, Big O).
4. **Charging for parts/services not performed or not installed** (Firestone TPMS, Midas, Mavis, Big O).
5. **Maintenance earlier than OEM schedule** (CBC dealers; quick-lube flushes).
6. **Weak documentation** (EZ Lube remedy required documenting every service).

## 3. Red-flag checklist

Each flag is a **signal to ask questions, not proof of fraud.**

**A. Evidence quality**
- No photo/video, or photo doesn't show the claimed defect (wrong part, wrong side, not clearly this vehicle).
- No measurement where expected: pad mm, rotor thickness vs stamped minimum, tread in 32nds, battery test printout, coolant/brake-fluid strip result.
- Measurements that don't fit: pads "2 mm" but rotor not measured; identical readings on all four tires; contradicts previous visit.
- "Fluid is dark" with no test result (color alone is weak).
- Photos are a sales tool too: vendors report more photos correlate with bigger tickets; some shops set minimum-photo quotas. Anecdote: DVI videos flagging two ball joints on a 30k-mile car.

**B. Scope and necessity**
- Not in OEM schedule for that mileage/duty cycle, or well before interval (fuel-system cleaning, multiple flushes, "induction service").
- Bundles (pads + rotors + calipers + flush) when only one component measured worn.
- "Red / unsafe" on items whose own measurement is within spec.
- Same recommendations every visit regardless of mileage.

**C. Paperwork and authorization**
- Work started/billed before documented authorization (direct violation in CA).
- Invoice exceeds estimate with no recorded re-authorization.
- Vague lines ("shop supplies," "misc"), acronyms instead of plain descriptions (barred in CA), generic supply charges.
- Parts not identified new/used/rebuilt; no part numbers; no tech name/cert number where required (MI).
- DVI shows "approved" items the customer doesn't recall, with no timestamp, approver name, or contact method.

**D. Sales environment**
- Pressure language ("unsafe to drive" without evidence; "price good today only").
- Advisor pay tied to sales.
- Refusal to return or show replaced parts.

## 5. Records to request (CA / FL / NY / MI)

| Record | CA (BAR) | FL | NY | MI |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| Written estimate before work | Required for a specific job; no work/charges before authorization (BPC 9884.9) | Required above **$150** (2024 text) before diagnosis/repair (559.905) | Required **on request**; reasonable fee allowed (GBL 398-d) | Required, itemized; waivable only via specific 14-pt bold form |
| Re-authorization over estimate | Required before extra work; date, time, name, phone/email recorded | Notify if over greater of $10 or 10%, capped at $50 (559.909) | Can't exceed without consent | Knowing consent |
| Itemized invoice | Each part new/used/rebuilt; labor and parts subtotals; no acronyms | Required (559.911) | All work/parts described; used parts flagged | Includes **mechanic name and certification number** |
| Return of replaced parts | If requested at authorization; cores/warranty parts excepted but offered for inspection | Inspection on request at authorization; return if no warranty/exchange arrangement | On timely written demand; exchange/warranty parts excepted | Entitled; shop keeps 2 business days if not addressed |
| Record retention | **3 years**, incl. authorization texts/emails, uniquely identified | [UNVERIFIED] | [UNVERIFIED] | [UNVERIFIED] |

**Ask-for list:** authorized estimate + authorization record (timestamp, approver, contact method); every revised estimate; final invoice; full DVI report incl. **original-resolution timestamped photos/video** (a request, not a right — no rule found requiring metadata [UNVERIFIED]); measurement values and tool used (micrometer, tread gauge, battery printout); tech name and ASE/state cert (mandatory on invoice only in some states, e.g., MI); replaced parts; OEM schedule reference for any "due" item; warranty terms.

**CA DVI note:** texts/emails used for authorization must be uniquely identified and **retained as part of the transaction record** (CCR 3353.1(e), 3358) — the DVI approval trail is a regulated record BAR can inspect.

## 6. Digital approvals: E-SIGN / UETA and state documentation

**Validity**
- E-SIGN (15 USC 7001) and state UETA (nearly all states): e-signature/record can't be denied effect solely for being electronic → a tap/click approval on a DVI link **can** be valid if **attribution and intent** are shown.
- **7001(c) consumer consent** applies only when a law requires a disclosure **in writing** and the business delivers it electronically: affirmative consent after notice of paper rights, withdrawal right, scope; consent in a way that reasonably demonstrates access to the format. MA says its UETA doesn't override 7001(c).
- Open question: if state law requires a *written* estimate given to the customer (CA, FL, MI), a link-only shop may need 7001(c)-style consent. Whether repair statutes trigger 7001(c) is [UNVERIFIED; open legal question]. CA resolves much of this by expressly allowing electronic authorization with documentation.

**Attribution evidence to look for:** who tapped approve (name), when (timestamp), from which phone/email, which line items at which prices. **Bare "Approved" with no trail = weak evidence of consent.** Revised amounts need a new approval record.

**California documentation model** (BAR WIR; BPC 9884.9)
- Authorization = written signature on estimate, or oral/electronic statement documented on it (CCR 3352(f)); obtained **before** work; customer gets a copy of anything signed (BPC 9884.7(a)(3); CCR 3353.1(a)).
- **Electronic:** date, time, name of authorizer, email or phone contacted (CCR 3353.1(d)). **Oral:** date, time, name, phone number called (3353.1(c)).
- Additional work: revised estimate listing added parts, labor, new total; authorization recorded on both work order and invoice; a note left with the vehicle doesn't count (CCR 3353.2, 3354, 3356(e)).
- Shop supplies listed on estimate and itemized on invoice; **generic supply charges prohibited** (CCR 3356(g)); no acronyms in place of descriptions.
- Customer may designate someone in writing to approve extra charges; shop and insurer can't be designees (BPC 9884.9(d)).
- Preventive-maintenance exception: no written estimate if price conspicuously displayed and acknowledged (BPC 9884.9(e)).

## 7. Texting: TCPA, mini-TCPAs, 10DLC, CTIA

| Rule | Key points |
|---|---|
| **TCPA** | Texts are "calls." *Facebook v. Duguid* (2021) narrowed "autodialer" to random/sequential number generators → less federal exposure for platform texts. Informational texts: prior express consent (e.g., number given for the service visit). Marketing: prior express **written** consent [general framework; not re-verified vs FCC text] |
| One-to-one consent rule | **Vacated** by 11th Cir. 2025-01-24 (*Insurance Marketing Coalition v. FCC*) |
| Revocation | Since **2025-04-11**: STOP, QUIT, END, REVOKE, OPT OUT, CANCEL, UNSUBSCRIBE = reasonable revocation; honor within **10 business days**. "Revoke-all-unrelated-messages" piece never took effect: waived to 2026-04-11, then to **2027-01-31** (FCC DA 26-12) — https://docs.fcc.gov/public/attachments/DA-26-12A1.pdf . **FCC 26-67** (released 2026-10-01) replaces it: a revocation of an informational text may be limited to that message category (a revocation of marketing stops all marketing); callers may designate and disclose an exclusive opt-out method; 10 business days unchanged. Effective 30 days after Federal Register publication (not yet published as of 2026-10-21) — https://docs.fcc.gov/public/attachments/FCC-26-67A1.pdf |
| State mini-TCPAs | **FL FTSA** (2021 amend.) and **OK OTSA** (2022): broader autodialer language, private rights of action; mainly reach *telephone solicitations* → a DVI text mixing in promotions raises risk |
| **A2P 10DLC** | Business texting from 10-digit numbers must register brand + campaign with The Campaign Registry; unregistered traffic blocked (Twilio error 30034); full carrier cutoffs reported ~Dec 2024 |
| **CTIA Messaging Principles** (carrier-enforced) | Opt-in confirmation with program name, frequency, "msg & data rates may apply," STOP/HELP instructions; honor every opt-out; answer HELP |

Mostly shop-owner compliance. For consumers: **preserve the text thread** — it's evidence of what was offered and approved.

## 8. Call-recording consent

- **All-party consent (phone calls):** California, Delaware, Florida, Illinois, Maryland, Massachusetts, Montana, Nevada (phone), New Hampshire, Pennsylvania, Washington.
- Sometimes added: Connecticut (civil), Michigan (disputed), Oregon (in-person only).
- Montana and Washington allow notice/announcement; Massachusetts turns on secrecy. Interstate calls: follow the stricter state.
- Consumers in all-party states shouldn't secretly record the advisor. Shops recording authorization calls need an announcement. A recorded oral authorization is strong evidence for both sides.

## 9. Privacy, telematics, Safeguards Rule

- **FTC v. GM/OnStar** final order (2026-01-14): 5-year ban on sharing connected-vehicle data with consumer reporting agencies; 20 years of express consent to collect/use/share, plus deletion and opt-out tools. Texas AG sued GM separately (Aug 2024).
- **California CPPA:** connected-vehicle review since July 2023; Honda fined $632,500 (2025); AG proposed $12.75M CCPA settlement with a connected-vehicle services provider (2026-05-08).
- **DVI data:** name, phone, VIN, plate, odometer, photos (may capture plates, interiors, documents, people), sometimes telematics/OBD. VIN alone as "personal information" [UNVERIFIED]; VIN linked to a customer record very likely covered. ~20 states have comprehensive privacy laws, most with thresholds small shops may not meet [UNVERIFIED count/thresholds].
- **FTC Safeguards Rule:** covers most dealers that finance/lease (GLBA "financial institutions"); since May 2024, unencrypted breaches affecting 500+ customers reported to FTC within 30 days. Dealer service lanes running DVI on the same database are inside the dealer's program; repair-only shops not extending credit generally outside [inference].
- **Skill privacy:** tell users to redact names, phones, addresses, plates before sharing audit output publicly; don't repeat PII in the audit.

## 10. Junk fees and price display

| Rule | Scope |
|---|---|
| FTC Unfair or Deceptive Fees Rule (eff. 2025-05-12) | **Only live-event tickets and short-term lodging — not auto repair** |
| **California SB 478** (Civ. Code 1770(a)(29), eff. 2024-07-01) | Advertised/listed prices must include all mandatory fees except government taxes and shipping; regulates display, not amounts; cross-industry. Mandatory shop-supply/environmental fee added to an *advertised* price (e.g., "$49.99 oil change" + fee) likely covered [inference; AG FAQ silent on auto repair]. Separately, BAR bans generic supply charges (CCR 3356(g)) |
| **Minnesota** (DTPA, eff. 2025-01-01) | Advertised prices include mandatory, not-reasonably-avoidable fees; variable-price services may disclose price factors and fees instead of a single total |
| Others (CO, VA, MA regs) | Fee-transparency rules adopted [UNVERIFIED scope/dates] |
| Federal | No fee amount set for hazmat/disposal/shop supplies (see `federal.md`) |

**Check:** advertised/quoted price vs invoice; fees not disclosed on the estimate; percentage "shop supply" fees with no itemization.

## 11. Accessibility (brief)
DOJ 2022 guidance: ADA Title III applies to websites; WCAG/Section 508 are "helpful guidance," no mandatory standard for private businesses; 2024 WCAG 2.1 AA rule covers only state/local government (Title II). Demand letters are common. Best practice for DVI links: alt text on photos, text equivalents for red/yellow/green, screen-reader-operable approve/decline.

## 12. Framing and liability guidance

### 12.1 Not a lawyer, not a mechanic
- **FTC v. DoNotPay** (final order 2025-01-16): AI "robot lawyer" sanctioned for unsubstantiated claims it could replace a lawyer; $193K relief. → Never claim to equal a lawyer or certified technician.
- UPL risk lowest with **general legal information + citations + suggested questions/options**; don't predict a specific user's legal outcome or draft filings as legal advice [general principle].
- Without hands-on inspection, assess only whether **documentation supports the finding**, not whether the part is bad. The Texas dealer won by proving necessity — an audit calling that work "fraud" would have been wrong.

### 12.2 Status labels instead of accusations
Use the SKILL.md labels: **Supported**, **Supported – optional**, **Evidence missing**, **Inconsistent**, **Beyond OEM schedule** (cite schedule source; ask why), **Not applicable to this vehicle**, **Needs OEM spec**, **Verify in person – safety**.
- The source research also proposed "**Documentation gap**" (a required estimate/authorization/invoice element appears absent) and "**Verify with second opinion**" (ambiguous safety evidence). Map these: documentation gaps go in the **Paperwork & compliance** section, not as an item status; ambiguous safety items → **Verify in person – safety**.
- **Avoid:** "fraud," "scam," "lied," "illegal," "rip-off," "you are entitled to a refund."
- **Prefer:** "This line item appears to lack the documentation that [state rule] describes; you may ask the shop for it, or ask [agency]."

### 12.3 Disclaimers and safety override
- Informational only; not legal advice or a mechanical diagnosis; laws vary and change; consult a licensed technician for condition and an attorney for rights.
- **Safety override:** brakes, steering, tires, suspension with ambiguous evidence → get it inspected **before** driving on the assumption it's fine. Never talk a user out of a safety repair because documentation is missing.

### 12.4 Defamation, anti-SLAPP, CRFA (when consumers publish)
- Truth and pure opinion are defenses; factual claims are riskier. "They charged me for work not done" = actionable if false; "slow and overpriced" = opinion.
- **Anti-SLAPP** in roughly 30+ states allows early dismissal of suits over reviews (2026 NJ appellate ruling dismissed a suit over a one-star review); federal courts treat some state anti-SLAPP laws differently.
- **Consumer Review Fairness Act (2016):** form-contract clauses barring/penalizing honest reviews are void — including DVI/repair-order terms. Businesses can still act on defamatory or clearly false reviews.
- Businesses can subpoena platforms to unmask anonymous reviewers (*Yelp v. Hadeed*).
- **Skill guidance:** help write **verifiable facts + opinion** ("The report recommended X; the photo shows Y; I asked for the measurement and didn't receive it; in my opinion…"). Don't draft accusations of crimes; point suspected fraud to regulators.

## 13. Audit checks

### Consumer
1. **Inputs:** DVI (photos, measurements, ratings), estimate(s), authorization records (texts/emails), invoice, year/make/model/mileage, OEM schedule, state.
2. **Per item:** measurement + spec/minimum + photo plausibly showing *this* component on *this* vehicle → assign status label.
3. **Schedule:** flag items not due per OEM schedule (cite FTC owner's-manual guidance) → **Beyond OEM schedule**.
4. **Paperwork (state-aware):** estimate before work; re-authorization for overruns (CA: any increase; FL: over greater of $10/10%, capped at $50); itemization; new/used/rebuilt disclosure; supply fees itemized (CA); mechanic cert number (MI); parts return honored if requested.
5. **Fees:** quoted/advertised price vs invoice; undisclosed mandatory fees (CA SB 478, MN).
6. **Output:** per-item "questions to ask the shop"; records-request checklist (§5); escalation path (§4) with the right agency; chargeback timing (60 days from statement); get-your-car-back notes (CA 3068 10-day records demand; FL 559.917 bond).
7. **Tone:** neutral, evidence-based; safety override and disclaimers; review help only facts-plus-opinion.

### Shop owner
1. **Evidence standard:** measurement, spec, clear photo with reference point (part identity, side, vehicle), plain-language note; flag techs missing these. Minimum-photo quotas are a risk if they encourage padding.
2. **Severity calibration:** red/safety only at or beyond spec limit or with observable failure; audit for red on in-spec items.
3. **Schedule alignment:** within OEM schedule, or labeled "shop-recommended, optional" with reason (central to CBC and quick-lube findings).
4. **Authorization trail:** name, date, time, contact method, itemized items and prices for every approval; retain texts/emails with the RO (3 yrs in CA); no work before approval.
5. **Invoice hygiene:** plain descriptions, no acronyms, part condition, itemized supplies (CA bans generic supply charges), cert numbers where required, parts-return offer.
6. **Incentive review:** commission/quota plans tied to parts and labor sold are the documented Sears-type root cause; balance with quality and comeback metrics (see `industry-standards.md` §8).
7. **Messaging:** 10DLC registration; CTIA opt-in and STOP/HELP; keep promotions out of transactional DVI texts (FTSA/OTSA risk); honor STOP within 10 business days.
8. **Call recording:** announce on authorization calls in all-party states.
9. **Privacy/security:** minimize PII in photos; DVI vendor data contracts; Safeguards program if a dealer; check state privacy thresholds.
10. **Price display:** include mandatory fees in advertised prices (CA, MN); no surprise environmental/shop-supply percentages.
11. **Reviews:** CRFA bars anti-review clauses; respond with facts; avoid suing over opinion.

## 14. Open questions
- Recent (2023–2026) enforcement thin: Mavis PA AVC is the main one; no recent FTC upsell case or dealer DVI class action; Firestone 2016 and Jiffy Lube Fanticola outcomes unconfirmed.
- Trust data dated: AAA Dec 2016 (~two-thirds distrust; 76% unnecessary recommendations; 73% overcharging). Consumer Reports: independents rated above dealers (2009, 2012, 2024). No CarMD/NHTSA/NADA/AASP upsell quantification found.
- E-SIGN 7001(c) vs state written-estimate rules with link-only delivery — unresolved.
- TCPA: FCC 26-67 effective date (30 days after Federal Register publication; not yet published as of 2026-10-21).
- TILA 1666i limits not re-verified.
- Statute currency: CA Civ. 3068 caps, FL $150 threshold, MI pending HB 5149 — check official sites. NY citation is GBL 398-d (not VTL); 2025 bills S516/A1385 would add fines, status unknown.
- No rule found requiring production of DVI photo metadata/originals.
- State privacy-law count/thresholds for repair shops not compiled.
- Arbitration clauses in DVI/e-authorization terms not researched.

# State Safety and Emissions Inspection Programs

**As of 2026-10-20; status, HI/RI/DE/MO/ME criteria and NY/MA/NC/PA fees rechecked 2026-10-21.** Official government inspection programs (not shop courtesy inspections). Use them as an **objective benchmark**: when a shop marks an item "fail / red / unsafe," compare its measurement to the official reject criteria for the vehicle's state.

**Key principle:** a state inspection standard is a **legal minimum for road use**, not a maintenance guideline. A part can pass and still be near the end of its life (several states require an "advisory" note for that). The reverse also holds: a shop can't honestly call a part a "state inspection failure" if it meets the state's criteria.

**Markers:** [S1] etc. = source list at end. **[UNVERIFIED]** = not confirmed from a primary or recent source. **[secondary source]** = news/aggregator only. The summary table's main source is a Wikipedia compilation [S1]; primary confirmations are noted per row. Paraphrased; check current text before relying on a figure. Out of scope: commercial/FMCSA inspections (`commercial-fleet.md`), detailed service limits (`technical-criteria.md`), repair-shop law (`state-repair-laws.md`).

**North Carolina:** brakes, lights, horn, steering, wipers, signals, tires (**2/32"** [secondary source S33]), mirrors. Fees **$13.60** safety ($12.75 + $0.85), **≤ $30** combined ($23.75 max + $6.25) (G.S. 20-183.7) [S47].
**Delaware** (Inspection Lane Manual rev. 2/20) [S42]: state-run free lanes, no inspector/repair-shop conflict of interest [S30]. Tires < **2/32"** in two adjacent grooves at 3 spots. Brakes tested on a dynamic pad tester (balance/deceleration limits), no lining-thickness figure. Chips/stars > **1"** in wiper range or cracks > **5"** on any window = reject.
**Hawaii** (HAR ch. 19-133.2) [S43]: tires < **2/32"** in two adjacent major grooves. Brakes: **no thickness measurement**; reject on pedal travel > 1" or reserve < 1.5", stop test at 4–8 mph suggesting worn pads/warped rotors, **lit pad-wear warning light**, leaks, parking brake not holding, rotor cracks to edge/deep scoring/contamination. Wheel bearing ≥ ⅛" with grinding. Windshield damage affecting visibility (no figure). **Any** exhaust leak. **Non-functional airbag may pass** if belts pass (noted on certificate).
**Rhode Island** (280-RICR-30-15-3) [S48]: lining < **1/32"** at thinnest point (AMC disc pads 2/32"); rotor below embossed/mfr minimum or cracked to edge; tires < **2/32"** in two adjacent grooves at 3+ spots; steering lash > **3"**, linkage play ¼"/⅜"/½" by wheel size, bearing > ⅛"; windshield damage in driver's wiper sweep (no size figure); **missing/deployed airbag or airbag light on = reject**.
**Missouri** (11 CSR 50-2) [S45]: at least one wheel/drum pulled. Bonded lining or bonded pad < **2/32"**; riveted lining < 2/32" above rivet; **riveted pads < 6/32"**. Tires: **no depth figure** — reject only when no tread pattern remains (outside wear indicator) or cord exposed; also cord-exposing cuts, knots, radial + bias on one axle. Windshield crack > **3"** or any unrepaired star/bull's-eye in driver's vision area.
**Texas:** no safety inspection for non-commercial vehicles. A TX shop telling a non-commercial customer "you need this to pass state inspection" for brakes/tires/lights is factually wrong.

## 4) Emissions and OBD-II basics

- Clean Air Act requires I/M in ~**33 state/local areas** not meeting air-quality standards; most use **OBD-II plug-in tests** for 1996+ gas and roughly 1997–2002+ diesel light vehicles [S35].
- **Typical OBD test:** (1) MIL lights at key-on engine-off (bulb check); (2) MIL **not** lit or commanded on while running — lit MIL with emissions code = fail; (3) **readiness monitors** set — most programs allow **1 unset for 2001+ and 2 for 1996–2000** (MO rule: up to 2 unset non-continuous monitors on 1996–2000 gas [S36]); (4) data link connector present and communicating. NY: 1996+ gas / 1997+ diesel ≤ 8,500 lb; Low Enhanced visual/gas-cap test for others ≤ 25 model years. VT: tampered/missing connector also rejects; DMV sets monitor count. CA: see `california-bar.md` (readiness rule eff. Oct 1, 2025).
- **Readiness traps:** monitors reset after battery disconnect or code clearing. Gas vehicles usually re-set within ~2 weeks of normal driving; diesels 8+ weeks. Programs increasingly read **permanent DTCs**, which clearing can't erase. "Not ready" right after a repair or battery replacement is **normal**, not proof of a new fault.
- **Waivers:** after a minimum spend on qualifying repairs plus failed retest. PA raised from $150 to **$450** eff. 9/1/2023 (bulletin SI 23-01), adjusted yearly (§177.282); 2026 figure [UNVERIFIED] [S54]. MA base caps $550–$750 by age, adjusted yearly [S51]. NC per G.S. 20-183.5. VT repair-assistance program.
- **Repair-shop link:** an emissions fail gives a **diagnostic starting point** (DTCs), not a parts list. Expect freeze frame, DTC, test results and root cause before catalytic converters or O2 sensors are replaced.

## 5) Using reject criteria as a benchmark

1. **Find the jurisdiction first.** If the vehicle isn't in a periodic-safety state (e.g., TX non-commercial, NH, FL, CA), **no shop finding is a "state inspection failure"**; such wording is a factual error. Also exempt: MO < 10 model years and < 150k mi; DE newest 7 model years; RI new vehicles before 24 months/24,000 mi; LA personal vehicles after 6/30/2026.
2. **Map each red/fail/unsafe item to the state criterion** and compare the shop's **measurement** (§2). Most common anchors: pads 2/32" (VT uses OEM min; RI 1/32"; MO riveted pads 6/32"; HI and DE don't measure — HI rejects on a lit pad-wear light; CA VSSI 1/32" bonded); tread 2/32" nearly everywhere (**MO: no depth figure — only no tread pattern or exposed cord**); rotors below OEM discard thickness (PA adds 0.015" scoring; VT ½" cumulative pitting); steering play per VA wheel-size scale; windshield varies widely.
3. **Classify:**
   - **Meets a state reject criterion** → legally required before the car passes (but *this* shop needn't do the repair).
   - **Fails OEM service limit but not state criterion** → legitimate maintenance recommendation, not a legal requirement.
   - **Within limits but approaching** → advisory / yellow tier (VT treats 2/32–4/32 tread this way).
   - **No measurement recorded** → unsupported. In PA a brake/tire rejection without recorded readings is a documentation defect (§175.29(f)(4)).
4. **Check wear consistency** ("pads 2 mm, rotors fine" vs "pads 7 mm, rotors replace"); cross-check `technical-criteria.md`.

**Red flags:**
- "State inspection failure" in a non-inspection state or on an exempt vehicle.
- MO shop citing "2/32" or "4/32" tread as a *state* reject (MO rejects only no-tread-pattern or exposed cord; < 2/32 is still a valid safety recommendation).
- "Fail" with a measurement that **meets** the minimum (e.g., "pads 3/32 — FAIL STATE").
- Failing on advisory-only items (§3 list).
- Rotors/pads "fail" with no micrometer reading or OEM minimum quoted; in PA or VA, a brake rejection with no wheels pulled or readings recorded.
- Pressure to authorize repairs before releasing rejection paperwork, or claims that repair/reinspection must happen at the same station (NY, PA, VA expressly allow elsewhere; MA's free retest is same-station but repair location is the owner's choice).
- Fee above cap (VA $20) or unposted (VT, PA require posting).
- Catalytic converter/major emissions parts sold without DTC/diagnostic documentation; "not ready" treated as a failed component; codes cleared right before the test.
- Repairs without documented owner permission (NY, PA require it).

**Consumer framing:** ask "Is this a **state inspection failure** or a **recommendation**?" A courtesy MPI/DVI is not a legal requirement. Ask for the measurement, compare with §2, know your retest window, get repairs or a second opinion elsewhere. Complaint channels: NY DMV VS-35; MO MSHP MVI; VA State Police Safety Division; PennDOT; VT DMV (identify only; don't file).

**Shop-owner framing:** configure DVI with three tiers — **Reject (state)**, **Recommend (OEM/maintenance)**, **Advise (monitor)**; never label an OEM-limit item "state fail"; require measurements on every brake and tire finding; inflated rejections risk the station's inspection license (VT, PA, NY, VA administrative penalties); keep replaced parts (PA); give itemized invoice + VIR pass or fail (VT); train on local advisory-only items; update "state requires" scripts after program changes (TX 2025; NH, LA 2026; NC emissions pending EPA final action; MO and WV repeal bills failed in 2026 but may return).

## 6) International notes

- **Canada — Ontario Safety Standards Certificate (SSC):** generally needed to plate a used vehicle transferred to a new owner; issued by licensed Motor Vehicle Inspection Stations ("DriveON" signage). Certifies minimum standards **on the inspection day only** — not a warranty. 36-day validity and "as-is" rules [UNVERIFIED]. Ontario Drive Clean light-vehicle emissions ended 4/1/2019. NS and NB periodic (2-yr), PEI annual, QC and BC generally none for passenger cars [secondary source].
- **UK — MOT:** first test by 3rd anniversary of registration, then annually; may test up to a month minus a day early keeping renewal date. Defects graded **dangerous, major, minor, advisory**; dangerous/major = fail; minor/advisory are notes. Can drive after a fail only if current MOT still valid and no dangerous defect; dangerous-defect driving risks fine up to £2,500, ban, 3 points. MOT doesn't assess engine, clutch or gearbox condition [UNVERIFIED]. The minor/advisory split is a good model for required vs recommended.
- **EU — Directive 2014/45/EU:** passenger cars first tested within 4 years of first registration, then at least every 2 years; states may test more often. Defects graded **minor, major, dangerous**; minimum test items and odometer recording [UNVERIFIED against EUR-Lex].

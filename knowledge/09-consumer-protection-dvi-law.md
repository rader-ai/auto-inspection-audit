## 2) Enforcement history and practices alleged

### 2.1 Landmark: Sears Auto Centers (1992)
- **What happened:** In June 1992 California regulators moved to revoke the repair licenses of Sears' auto centers. Undercover investigators had been overcharged about $223 per visit on average. Officials blamed a restructured wage and commission system that pushed employees to hit sales quotas; staff who missed targets reportedly lost hours or were transferred. (https://www.tampabay.com/archive/1992/06/12/sears-accused-of-auto-repair-fraud/)
- **Scale:** Coverage reported unnecessary repairs were recommended in 34 of 37 undercover cases. (https://www.upi.com/Archives/1992/06/16/Sears-hits-back-at-California-probe/4031708667200/, as summarized in search results; the exact figure was not re-read on the page.)
- **Remedy:** Sears denied wrongdoing. It settled for up to $46.6M in customer coupons and three years of probation for its 72 California centers (https://reference.org/news/Sears/5yYoAEhd). A 1995 report says about $20M was actually paid out (https://www.tampabay.com/archive/1995/08/04/sears-faces-lawsuit-over-tire-balancing/). Sears dropped commissions for auto service staff and ended a nationwide incentive plan that tied service-manager pay to repair and parts volume (https://www.upi.com/Archives/1992/07/21/Sears-settles-auto-repair-disputes-in-NJ/5979711691200/; https://www.moderntiredealer.com/articles/33372-what-happened-to-sears-auto-centers).
- **New Jersey parallel:** A $200,000 industry-reform fund and $3,000 in fines (UPI, same link).
- **Lesson for the audit skill:** The root cause was incentive design, specifically **quotas plus commissions tied to how much repair work was sold**. This is the textbook upsell driver.

### 2.2 California AG and BAR: Midas franchisee (2010)
- On 2010-01-25, AG Brown announced a $1.8M settlement with Maurice Glad, owner of 22 Midas shops. The shops advertised $79–$99 brake specials, then added $110–$130 for rotor resurfacing that was often unnecessary. They also charged for repairs that were unneeded or never performed. About 30 undercover runs found 105 violations, with an average of nearly $300 in unnecessary charges per run. Staff were accused of making false statements to pressure customers. The conduct also violated a 1989 injunction against scare tactics. Glad is permanently barred from holding a BAR license. (https://www.oag.ca.gov/news/press-releases/brown-reaches-18-million-settlement-owner-22-midas-auto-shops-over-massive-bai-0)
- **Pattern:** bait-and-switch with a low advertised price, padded with an "add-on" (rotor resurfacing) that was not needed.

### 2.3 BAR: Firestone / Bridgestone (2016)
- BAR accused 21 California Firestone stores. Over 30+ undercover runs starting in 2013, agents requesting oil changes, smog checks, or minor repairs were pitched unneeded services priced from $70 to $2,600; one Hayward oil change turned into an $856 recommendation. Some stores allegedly charged for TPMS valve stems and kits that were never installed. BAR found over 100 causes for discipline and reached a settlement with Bridgestone. Final terms were not in the article. (https://abc7news.com/post/7-bay-area-firestone-shops-accused-of-fraud/1404430/) **[UNVERIFIED: final settlement terms.]**
- **Pattern:** a low-ticket entry visit becomes the platform for upselling, plus **charging for parts not installed**.

### 2.4 BAR: Jiffy Lube franchisee (Fanticola family, 2016)
- BAR undercover runs at 41 Bay Area stores found 105 causes for discipline. 26 locations performed at least one unnecessary service, adding up to $240 to the bill. BAR sought to revoke or suspend all 51 licenses. (https://abc7news.com/post/agents-allege-pattern-of-fraud-at-dozens-of-bay-area-jiffy-lubes/1372636/; https://www.ratchetandwrench.com/articles/2812-51-location-jiffy-lube-franchise-under-fraud-investigation) **[UNVERIFIED: outcome.]**
- A related NBC4 I-Team investigation showed Jiffy Lube staff pushing services that experts called unnecessary. (https://nbclosangeles.com/news/local/you-were-robbed-nbc4-i-team-exposes-new-tricks-and-tactics-at-jiffy-lube-stores/2060915)
- EZ Lube (Orange County DA and BAR, 2008) paid $5M and agreed to document all services and charges. (https://www.automotive-fleet.com/news/ez-lube-to-pay-5-million-to-settle-fraud-case)

### 2.5 Pep Boys
- **New Jersey Division of Consumer Affairs:** An inspection at the Piscataway store found unnecessary and overcharged repairs and misleading statements about repair need. Six consumers were reimbursed $2,561.72, inside a broader $80,000 pricing settlement. (https://nj.gov/oag/newsreleases18/pr20180112c.html)
- **California BAR:** An undercover probe of four Valley shops produced a two-day closure, three years of probation, and a $31,000 fine. The issue was charging for work that should have been part of a free A/C check. (https://abc30.com/archive/9514630/)

### 2.6 Mavis Tire (PA 2025-26; GA 2022)
- **Pennsylvania:** AG Dave Sunday reached a $215,000 Assurance of Voluntary Compliance ($190K restitution, $25K costs, plus a $100K suspended penalty). The AG alleged Mavis recommended and charged for unneeded repairs and maintenance, billed for work not performed, and sometimes did poor work. Mavis denies wrongdoing and attributes any misconduct to employees who broke policy. Consumers may file complaints for restitution until 2027-01-26. (https://www.mychesco.com/?p=681517; https://news.dealershipguy.com/p/pennsylvania-ag-settles-case-on-auto-repairs) **[UNVERIFIED: exact announcement date, location count.]**
- **Georgia (2022):** a deceptive-practices settlement with restitution and an $80,000 claim fund. (https://www.wtoc.com/2022/04/05/mavis-tire-supply-enters-settlement/)

### 2.7 Other actions
- **Arizona AG sting program / Econo-Lube N' Tune (Meineke-related per search):** An undercover car with a minor A/C issue drew an estimate for $650 in unneeded parts and labor. The company paid $30,000 in civil penalties plus fees, without admitting liability. (https://www.azag.gov/press-release/ag-horne-announces-new-program-sting-operations-produces-first-consent-judgment)

- **Big O Tires class action (2018):** alleged that a "free multi-point inspection" was a scheme to sell costly extra work, including work billed but not performed. (https://topclassactions.com/lawsuit-settlements/consumer-products/auto-news/863643-big-o-tires-class-action-says-free-inspection-scheme/) **[UNVERIFIED: outcome.]**
- **Small-shop criminal and licensing cases:** Pacifica, CA shop lost its license after a BAR sting (2019) (https://abc7news.com/consumer-fraud-repair-auto-undercover-sting/5565499/). A Redwood City mechanic got 30 days in jail after a no-contest plea for unnecessary repairs (2012) (https://abc7news.com/auto-repair-consumer-fraud-state-bureau-of-automotive-repairs-san-mateo-county-district-attorneys-office/5169368/).
- **Dealerships:** CBC Marketplace hidden-camera tests found some dealers sold unneeded service and pushed maintenance earlier than the manufacturer schedule (https://www.cbc.ca/1.4300557). - **Valvoline:** a 2024 multistate settlement covered *labor* non-competes, not upselling (https://ag.ny.gov/press-release/2024/attorney-general-james-ends-unfair-labor-practices-major-auto-services-company). A CBC quick-lube investigation found add-ons of about $180–$250 per oil change and quoted ex-employees on sales targets (via https://www.aol.com/6-auto-repair-chains-accused-130050737.html; secondhand).

### 2.8 Federal backdrop
- **FTC CARS Rule** (dealer sales: misrepresentations, add-ons, express consent) was vacated by the 5th Circuit on 2025-01-27 on procedural grounds (no advance notice of proposed rulemaking). It never governed service departments directly. (https://www.hklaw.com/en/insights/publications/2025/02/fifth-circuit-strikes-down-ftcs-auto-retail-scam-rule)
- **Magnuson-Moss / FTC:** Dealers and manufacturers cannot void a warranty just because someone else did the maintenance. The FTC points consumers to the **owner's manual** as the best source for the maintenance schedule. (https://search.ftc.gov/news-events/news/press-releases/2024/07/ftc-warns-companies-stop-warranty-practices-harm-consumers-right-repair; https://business.ftc.gov/system/files/documents/cases/151022bmwletter.pdf)
- **FTC Section 5 (general UDAP)** and state UDAP acts are the catch-all hook for misrepresenting a vehicle's condition.

### 2.9 Recurring practices alleged (synthesis)
1. Sales quotas and commissions or bonuses tied to parts and labor sold (Sears; quick-lube ex-employee accounts).
2. False or exaggerated statements about condition or safety ("scare tactics") (Midas injunction; PA Mavis).
3. Low-price entry offers (brake specials, free checks, oil changes) used to sell unneeded add-ons (Midas, Firestone, Pep Boys, Big O).
4. Charging for parts or services not performed or not installed (Firestone TPMS, Midas, Mavis, Big O).
5. Recommending maintenance earlier than the OEM schedule (CBC dealers; quick-lube flushes).
6. Weak documentation (EZ Lube remedy required documenting every service).

## 3) Red-flag patterns (for the audit engine)

Each flag is a **signal to ask questions, not proof of fraud.**

**A. Evidence quality**
- A finding has no photo or video, or the photo does not show the claimed defect (wrong part, wrong side, not clearly this vehicle).
- No measurement where one is expected (brake pad mm, rotor thickness vs. the minimum stamped on the rotor, tread depth in 32nds, battery test printout, coolant or brake-fluid test-strip result).
- Measurements that don't fit together: pads "2 mm" but the rotor was not measured; identical readings on all four tires; a reading that contradicts the previous visit's.
- A "fluid is dark" claim with no test result. Color alone is a weak indicator. (Mechanical detail is covered by other divisions.)
- Industry vendors themselves report that more photos correlate with bigger tickets, and some shops set minimum-photo quotas (https://www.autovitals.com/?p=5914; https://autoflow.com/training/best-practices/how-to-perform-a-good-digital-vehicle-inspection-dvi/). The photos are a sales tool as well as evidence. One shop owner described DVI videos that flagged improbable failures, such as two ball joints on a 30k-mile car (https://artsautomotive.com/art-icles/articles-for-the-curious/digital-inspections-a-tool-for-communication-or-fraud/; anecdote).

**B. Scope and necessity**
- Services not in the OEM maintenance schedule for that mileage and duty cycle, or recommended well before the interval (fuel-system cleaning, multiple fluid flushes, "induction service").
- Bundles ("brake job" = pads + rotors + calipers + fluid flush) when only one component measured worn.
- A safety-urgency label ("red / unsafe") on items whose own measurement is within spec.
- Recommendations that repeat on every visit no matter the mileage.

**C. Paperwork and authorization**
- Work started or billed before authorization was documented. In CA this is a direct violation (see section 5.2).
- Final invoice exceeds the estimate with no recorded re-authorization.
- Vague line items ("shop supplies," "misc"), acronyms used instead of plain descriptions (barred in CA), or generic supply charges.
- Parts not identified as new, used, or rebuilt; no part numbers; no technician name or certification where the state requires it (MI).
- The DVI link or approval page shows "approved" items the customer does not remember approving, with no timestamp, approver name, or contact method.

**D. Sales environment**
- Pressure language ("unsafe to drive" with no evidence; "price good today only").
- Advisor pay tied to sales (it may be disclosed or discoverable; the pattern is documented above).
- Refusal to return replaced parts or let the customer see them.

## 4) Consumer dispute playbook

> Escalate in order. Keep everything in writing. Do not post public accusations before getting records (see section 6.4).

**Step 0. Before authorizing, if still possible**
- Decline or defer anything you don't understand. In most regimes an estimate is not a contract to perform all items.
- Ask for the measurement, the OEM spec or minimum, and the photo for each recommended item. Write "Return all replaced parts" on the estimate or authorization. Several states require the request **at the time of authorization** (CA BPC 9884.10 via BAR WIR: https://bar.ca.gov/wir; FL 559.905; NY GBL 398-d requires a timely written demand).
- Ask for a second opinion from an independent shop. Take the DVI report with you; the second shop can verify measurements.

**Step 1. Request records** (see section 4A)

**Step 2. Written complaint to the shop owner or manager.** Name the specific items, the evidence that is missing, and the remedy you want (refund of $X for item Y). BAR expects consumers to try the shop first. (https://www.bar.ca.gov/complaint)

**Step 3. Regulator complaint**
- **California BAR:** online, mail (form BAR-301), phone, or in person. Attach copies, not originals; the form is signed under penalty of perjury. A representative may inspect the vehicle and mediate. The complaint becomes part of the shop's file and can be subpoenaed for small claims. BAR mediates roughly 15–18k complaints a year. (https://www.bar.ca.gov/complaint; https://www.bar.ca.gov/arsc/newsletters/newsletter/spring-2020/complaint-mediation-process; https://www.bar.ca.gov/enforcement-process)
- **New York:** DMV Consumer Services Section handles improper-repair and deceptive-practice complaints (https://assembly.state.ny.us/comm/Affairs/20040322b).
- **Florida:** FDACS (motor vehicle repair registration) **[UNVERIFIED: agency complaint URL not fetched]**.
- **Elsewhere:** state AG consumer-protection division. Pennsylvania's Mavis restitution required consumers to file AG complaints (https://www.mychesco.com/?p=681517), so AG complaints can lead to restitution. **Dealer** service complaints can also go to the manufacturer's customer-relations line and, in some states, the DMV dealer board **[UNVERIFIED by state]**.
- **BBB:** voluntary complaint and mediation; useful for creating a public record, but has no enforcement power. **[General knowledge; not separately sourced.]**

**Step 4. Getting the car back while disputing**
- Repair shops generally hold a **possessory (garage/mechanic's) lien** for authorized work. Typical approach: **pay under protest** in writing (on the check or invoice), take the car, then dispute (https://www.slashgear.com/2118767/mechanic-refuse-release-car-what-do-if-happens; general guidance). A lien may fail for work that was **not authorized**. (https://www.creditkarma.com/auto/i/mechanics-lien; secondary)
- **California, Civil Code 3068:** the lien arises when a written statement of charges is presented or 15 days after the work is completed, whichever comes first. It is extinguished unless the shop applies for a lien sale or sues within 30 days. It is also extinguished if the shop fails to provide the work order or invoice and the owner's authorization within 10 days of a written demand. The shop may not strip installed parts. Caps apply for work or storage requested by someone other than the owner ($1,500 work / $1,025 storage). Prevailing-party fees of up to $1,750 apply in wrongful-withholding actions. (https://california.public.law/codes/civil_code_section_3068; mirror, verify on leginfo)
- **Florida 559.917:** the customer can post a cash or surety bond with the clerk (invoice amount plus storage minus amounts paid) and get the car released without filing suit. The shop has 60 days to sue on the bond (https://m.flsenate.gov/Statutes/559.917). FL 559.909 makes it unlawful to keep a car because the customer refuses to pay for unauthorized charges or charges above the final estimate (https://flsenate.gov/laws/statutes/2023/559.905; see 559.909).
- Storage charges add to the lien, so act fast.

**Step 5. Credit card chargeback (Fair Credit Billing Act)**
- Applies to credit cards and open-end credit, not debit cards. Send a **written** dispute to the issuer's billing-inquiries address within **60 days** of the first statement showing the charge. The issuer must acknowledge within 30 days and resolve within two billing cycles (no more than 90 days). You may withhold the disputed amount during the investigation. "Goods or services not accepted or not delivered as agreed" is a billing-error category. (https://www.law.cornell.edu/wex/fair_credit_billing_act_%28fcba%29; https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Fair_Credit_Billing_Act)
- **Quality disputes:** TILA's claims-and-defenses provision (15 USC 1666i) generally requires a good-faith attempt to resolve with the merchant, a purchase over $50, and a purchase in your home state or within 100 miles. **[UNVERIFIED against primary text this pass.]** Card-network chargeback rules are often broader than the statute.
- Caution: paying under protest by card and then charging back can prompt the shop to sue for the balance. Keep the documentation strong.

**Step 6. Small claims**
- Filing fees are typically modest and a lawyer is not required. Limits vary by state; one guide cites disputes under $10k (https://www.slashgear.com/2118767/mechanic-refuse-release-car-what-do-if-happens). **[UNVERIFIED: state limits vary, e.g., CA $12,500 for individuals.]** Bring the estimate, the authorization records, the invoice, DVI photos, the second-opinion report, and the returned parts. The BAR complaint file can be subpoenaed (BAR link above).
- **Replevin** (a suit to recover the vehicle) if the shop holds the car unlawfully (same slashgear source).

**Step 7. Arbitration**
- Some repair orders and dealer paperwork contain arbitration clauses. Check the signed authorization or terms on the DVI link. Clauses often carve out small claims. **[UNVERIFIED: prevalence in repair-order terms; not researched in depth.]** Express-warranty disputes against manufacturers may use manufacturer or BBB AUTO LINE programs. **[UNVERIFIED.]**

## 5) DVI-specific legal issues

### 5.1 E-SIGN Act and UETA: are link-based approvals valid?
- Under E-SIGN (15 USC 7001) and state UETA (adopted in almost every state), an electronic signature or record generally cannot be denied effect solely because it is electronic. A click or tap approval on a DVI link can therefore be a valid authorization if attribution and intent are shown. (https://www.mass.gov/doc/e-sign-act-at-a-glance/download)
- **Consumer-disclosure consent (7001(c))** applies only when a law requires a disclosure to be given **in writing** and the business wants to deliver it electronically. It requires affirmative consent after notice of paper rights, the right to withdraw, and scope, and consent given in a way that reasonably demonstrates the consumer can access the format. Massachusetts says its UETA does not override 7001(c). (https://consumercomplianceoutlook.org/2009/fourth-quarter/q4_02; https://nclc.org/wp-content/uploads/2024/09/Quick-E-Sign-slides2.pdf; https://www.mass.gov/doc/e-sign-act-at-a-glance/download)
- **Practical issue:** if state law requires a *written* estimate given to the customer (CA, FL, MI), a shop that only sends a link may need 7001(c)-style consent for the estimate to count as "written" delivery. Whether state repair statutes trigger 7001(c) is **[UNVERIFIED; open legal question]**. California's BAR resolves much of this by expressly allowing **electronic authorization** with specific documentation (5.2).
- **Attribution evidence the audit should look for:** who tapped "approve" (name), when (timestamp), from which phone or email, and which line items at which prices. A bare "Approved" status with no audit trail is weak evidence of consent.

### 5.2 State authorization-documentation rules (CA model)
- Authorization can be a written signature on the estimate, or an oral or electronic statement documented on it (CCR 3352(f)). It must be obtained **before** work begins, and the customer gets a copy of anything signed (BPC 9884.7(a)(3); CCR 3353.1(a)).
- **Electronic authorization must record:** date, time, name of the authorizing person, and the email or phone number contacted (CCR 3353.1(d)). **Oral authorization must record:** date, time, name, and the phone number called (3353.1(c)).
- Additional work requires a revised estimate listing the added parts, labor, and new total. Authorization must be recorded on both the work order and the invoice. A note left with the vehicle does not count (CCR 3353.2, 3354, 3356(e)).
- Shop supplies must be listed on the estimate and itemized on the invoice; **generic supply charges are prohibited** (CCR 3356(g)). Acronyms may not replace plain descriptions.
- A customer may designate someone in writing to approve extra charges. The shop and insurer cannot be designees (BPC 9884.9(d)).
- Preventive-maintenance exception: no written estimate is required when the price is conspicuously displayed and acknowledged (BPC 9884.9(e)).
- Sources: https://bar.ca.gov/wir ; https://codes.findlaw.com/ca/business-and-professions-code/bpc-sect-9884-9/

### 5.3 Texting inspection links: TCPA, state mini-TCPAs, 10DLC, CTIA
- **TCPA:** Texts count as "calls." *Facebook v. Duguid* (2021) narrowed "autodialer" to random or sequential number generators, which reduced federal exposure for platform-sent texts. (https://www.mondaq.com/article/news/1289988) Informational texts generally need prior express consent (for example, the customer gave their number for the service visit); marketing texts need prior express written consent. **[General TCPA framework; not re-verified against the FCC rule text this pass.]**
- **One-to-one consent rule** (lead-gen) was vacated by the 11th Circuit on 2025-01-24 (*Insurance Marketing Coalition v. FCC*) (https://www.kelleydrye.com/viewpoints/blogs/ad-law-access/eleventh-circuit-vacates-tcpa-11-consent-rule).
- **Revocation rule:** Since 2025-04-11, STOP, QUIT, END, REVOKE, OPT OUT, CANCEL and UNSUBSCRIBE count as reasonable revocation and must be honored within 10 business days. The "revoke-all-unrelated-messages" piece never took effect: the FCC's Consumer and Governmental Affairs Bureau waived it to 2026-04-11 and then, in DA 26-12 (2026-01-06), to 2027-01-31 (https://docs.fcc.gov/public/attachments/DA-26-12A1.pdf; https://www.kelleydrye.com/viewpoints/blogs/ad-law-access/fcc-issues-one-year-limited-waiver-of-new-tcpa-consent-revocation-rule).
- **FCC 26-67 (adopted 2026-09-30, released 2026-10-01):** This Report and Order rewrites 47 CFR 64.1200(a)(10)–(12) and replaces the waived revoke-all date. A revocation sent in response to an *informational* call or text may be treated as covering only that category of informational messages. A revocation sent in response to marketing still stops all marketing robocalls and robotexts from that caller. Callers may designate one or more exclusive opt-out methods (automated voice/key-press, reply text with a standardized word, or a designated website/phone number) if they disclose the method clearly and conspicuously on each call or text. Callers that designate none must still honor any reasonable method. The 10-business-day deadline is unchanged. The rules take effect 30 days after Federal Register publication. As of 2026-10-21 the Federal Register API showed no publication, so the effective date is not yet fixed. A further notice seeks comment on a 7-business-day deadline, mandatory two-way texting, and an optional revoke-all method. (https://docs.fcc.gov/public/attachments/FCC-26-67A1.pdf)
- **DVI implication:** A customer who replies STOP to an inspection-link or status text may, once FCC 26-67 is effective, be treated as opting out only of that category of informational texts. A STOP to a promotional or "service due" text ends all marketing texts from the shop.
- **State mini-TCPAs:** Florida's FTSA (2021 amendment) and Oklahoma's OTSA (2022) use broader autodialer language and have private rights of action. They mainly reach *telephone solicitations* (marketing), so a DVI text that mixes in promotions raises the risk. (https://goodwinlaw.com/en/news-and-events/news/2023/03/03_10-mini-tcpa-laws-you-should-know; https://www.compliancepoint.com/marketing-compliance/oklahoma-telephone-solicitation-act/)
- **A2P 10DLC:** Business texting from standard 10-digit numbers must be registered (brand plus campaign) with The Campaign Registry. Unregistered traffic is blocked; Twilio returns error 30034. Full carrier cutoffs were reported around Dec 2024. (https://support.twilio.com/hc/en-us/articles/14910496447771-Shutdown-of-Unregistered-10DLC-Messaging; https://support.callhub.io/hc/en-us/articles/13531215922201)
- **CTIA Messaging Principles** (industry rules that carriers enforce): opt-in confirmation with program name, frequency, "msg & data rates may apply," and STOP/HELP instructions; honor every opt-out; answer HELP. (https://www.infobip.com/docs/essentials/usa-and-canada-compliance/us-sms-compliance-guide; secondary summary of CTIA)
- **Audit relevance:** These are mainly shop-owner compliance items. For consumers, the main point is that the text thread itself is evidence of what was offered and approved, and should be preserved.

### 5.4 Call-recording consent
- All-party (two-party) consent for phone calls generally applies in **California, Delaware, Florida, Illinois, Maryland, Massachusetts, Montana, Nevada (phone calls), New Hampshire, Pennsylvania, Washington**. Some lists add Connecticut (civil), Michigan (disputed), and Oregon (in-person only). Montana and Washington allow notice or announcement; Massachusetts turns on secrecy. For interstate calls, follow the stricter state. (https://ktslaw.com/blog/globalprivacy-and-cybersecuritylaw/2024/7/wiretap-laws-in-the-united-states; https://help.ringio.com/en/articles/6314449-two-party-consent-states-call-recording)
- **Audit relevance:** Consumers in all-party states should not secretly record the advisor call. Shops recording authorization calls need an announcement. A recorded oral authorization is strong evidence for both sides.

### 5.5 Privacy and vehicle or telematics data
- **FTC v. GM/OnStar:** The final order (2026-01-14) bans sharing connected-vehicle data with consumer reporting agencies for 5 years, and requires express consent to collect, use, or share connected-vehicle data for 20 years, plus deletion and opt-out tools. (https://www.parkerpoe.com/news/2026/01/ftc-finalizes-order-alleging-improper-collection-and-sale; https://www.theregister.com/2026/01/15/ftc_gm_tracking_ban/) Texas AG sued GM separately in Aug 2024 (https://captaincompliance.com/news/ftc-finalizes-settlement-with-gm-over-unauthorized-driver-data-sales-as-texas-ag-targets-connected-car-privacy-violations/).
- **California CPPA:** connected-vehicle review opened July 2023; Honda fined $632,500 (2025); the AG announced a proposed $12.75M CCPA settlement with a connected-vehicle services provider (2026-05-08) (https://www.repairerdrivennews.com/2025/03/18/honda-to-pay-632500-fine-for-california-privacy-act-violations/; https://consumer-protection-dispatch.pillsburylaw.com/california-ag-ccpa-penalty-data-minimization/).
- **DVI implications:** DVI platforms collect name, phone, VIN, plate, odometer, photos (which can capture plates, interiors, documents, or people), and sometimes telematics or OBD data. Whether a VIN alone is "personal information" under CCPA and similar laws is **[UNVERIFIED]**, but VIN linked to a customer record is very likely covered. About 20 states have comprehensive privacy laws, and most have revenue or volume thresholds that small independent shops may not meet. **[UNVERIFIED count and thresholds; not fetched.]**
- **Audit-skill privacy:** Users will upload invoices and photos containing PII and VINs. The skill should tell users to redact names, phones, addresses, and plates before sharing audit output publicly.

### 5.6 FTC Safeguards Rule (dealers)
- It covers most auto dealers that finance or lease, because they are "financial institutions" under GLBA. Since May 2024, unencrypted customer-information breaches affecting 500+ customers must be reported to the FTC within 30 days of discovery. (https://www.ftc.gov/node/88472; https://wardsauto.com/dealers/dealers-face-new-safeguards-rule-requirement)
- **Relevance:** Dealer service lanes that run DVI on the same customer database fall within the dealer's information-security program. Independent repair-only shops that don't extend credit are generally outside it. **[Inference.]**

### 5.7 Junk-fee and all-in pricing (shop supplies, environmental, "hazmat" fees)
- **FTC Unfair or Deceptive Fees Rule:** effective 2025-05-12, but limited to **live-event tickets and short-term lodging**. It does **not** cover auto repair. (https://btlaw.com/en/insights/alerts/2025/ftc-finalizes-junk-fees-rule-new-pricing-disclosure-requirements-take-effect-may-12-2025; https://www.cooley.com/news/insight/2024/2024-12-20-ftc-finalizes-junk-fee-rule-aimed-at-live-event-ticket-and-short-term-lodging-fees)
- **California SB 478 (Civ. Code 1770(a)(29))**, effective 2024-07-01: advertised or listed prices must include all mandatory fees except government taxes and shipping. It regulates price display, not amounts. It applies across industries. (https://oag.ca.gov/hiddenfees; https://oag.ca.gov/system/files/attachments/press-docs/SB%20478%20FAQ%20%28B%29.pdf) A mandatory shop-supply or environmental fee added to an *advertised* repair price (e.g., "$49.99 oil change" plus a fee) is likely covered. **[Inference; the AG FAQ does not address auto repair specifically.]** Separately, BAR already bans generic shop-supply charges (CCR 3356(g)).
- **Minnesota** (effective 2025-01-01, Deceptive Trade Practices Act): advertised prices must include mandatory fees that are not reasonably avoidable. Unlike California, variable-price services may disclose price factors and fees instead of a single total. (https://tcbmag.com/six-things-to-know-about-minnesotas-junk-fees-law/; https://www.bakerlaw.com/insights/minnesota-passes-junk-fee-law-and-california-issues-faqs)
- Other states (e.g., Colorado, Virginia, Massachusetts regulations) have adopted fee-transparency rules. **[UNVERIFIED scope and dates; see https://perkinscoie.com/insights/update/ftc-junk-fee-rule-here-along-wave-state-laws.]**
- **Audit check:** compare the advertised or quoted price to the invoice. Flag fees that were not disclosed in the estimate and percentage "shop supply" fees with no itemization.

### 5.8 Accessibility of customer-facing DVI links (brief)
- DOJ's 2022 guidance says Title III applies to websites and points to WCAG and Section 508 as "helpful guidance," but sets no mandatory technical standard for private businesses. The 2024 WCAG 2.1 AA rule covers only state and local governments (Title II). (https://www.morganlewis.com/pubs/2022/04/doj-breaks-silence-on-ada-web-accessibility-with-new-guidance) Web-accessibility demand letters are common. Shops and DVI vendors should provide alt text on photos, text equivalents for color-coded red/yellow/green ratings, and screen-reader-operable approve/decline buttons. **[Best practice.]**

## 6) Liability and framing guidance for the audit skill

### 6.1 Don't present as a lawyer or a mechanic
- **FTC v. DoNotPay (final order 2025-01-16):** The FTC sanctioned an AI "robot lawyer" for claiming to substitute for a human lawyer without testing that claim. The order bars such claims without substantiation and required $193K in relief. (https://www.ftc.gov/node/87474) **Lesson:** the skill must not claim it equals a lawyer or a certified technician, and should not market itself as one.
- Unauthorized-practice-of-law risk is lowest when the tool provides **general legal information plus citations** and **suggests questions and options**, and does not tell a specific user what legal outcome they will get or draft filings presented as legal advice. **[General principle; no state UPL-AI ruling fetched.]**
- **Mechanical diagnosis:** without hands-on inspection, the tool can only assess whether the *documentation supports the finding*. It cannot say whether the part is actually bad. The Texas dealer verdict shows that a shop can win by proving necessity (https://www.grsm.com/insight/grsm-secures-complete-defense-verdict-for-franchised-new-car-dealership-in-faulty-repair-and-deceptive-practices-lawsuit/). An audit that labeled that work "fraud" would have been wrong.

### 6.2 Recommended finding vocabulary
Use evidence-status labels, not verdicts:
- **"Supported"**: evidence in the report (measurement plus photo plus spec) matches the recommendation.
- **"Evidence missing"**: no measurement, photo, or spec provided; ask for it.
- **"Inconsistent"**: report data contradicts itself or the recommendation (e.g., the measurement is within spec but the item is marked urgent).
- **"Beyond OEM schedule"**: the item is not due per the owner's manual at this mileage or time (cite the schedule source); ask why.
- **"Documentation gap"**: an authorization, estimate, or invoice element required by the state rule appears absent.
- **"Verify with second opinion"**: safety-relevant items where the evidence is ambiguous.
Avoid: "fraud," "scam," "lied," "illegal," "rip-off," and "you are entitled to a refund." Prefer "this line item appears to lack the documentation that [state rule] describes; you may ask the shop for it, or ask [agency]."

### 6.3 Required disclaimers
- Informational only. Not legal advice or a mechanical diagnosis. Laws vary by state and change. Consult a licensed technician for vehicle condition and an attorney for legal rights.
- **Safety override:** if a finding involves brakes, steering, tires, or suspension and the evidence is ambiguous, tell the user to get the vehicle inspected **before** driving on the assumption that the item is fine. The tool must never talk a user out of a safety repair because documentation is missing.

### 6.4 Defamation risk when consumers publish
- Truth and pure opinion are defenses, and statements of fact are riskier. "They charged me for work not done" is a factual claim and actionable if false; "slow and overpriced" is opinion (https://www.findlaw.com/legal/practice/practice-guide/online-reviews-and-defamation.html; https://minclaw.com/can-business-sue-for-bad-review).
- **Anti-SLAPP** statutes in many states (counts vary, roughly 30+) allow early dismissal of suits over speech on public issues, including reviews. A 2026 NJ appellate ruling dismissed a suit over a one-star review. Federal courts have treated some state anti-SLAPP laws differently. (https://natlawreview.com/article/new-jerseys-anti-slapp-law-scores-another-victory-appellate-division-protects; https://mdedge.com/content/handling-defamatory-online-reviews)
- **Consumer Review Fairness Act (2016):** form-contract clauses that bar or penalize honest reviews are void. That includes DVI or repair-order terms of service. Businesses can still act on defamatory or clearly false reviews. (https://www.ftc.gov/node/60366; https://www.haynesboone.com/alerts/complying-with-the-consumer-review-fairness-act)
- Businesses can subpoena platforms to unmask anonymous reviewers (*Yelp v. Hadeed*).
- **Skill guidance:** if a user wants to post a review, help them write **verifiable facts plus opinion** ("The report recommended X; the photo provided shows Y; I asked for the measurement and didn't receive it; in my opinion…"). Don't help draft accusations of crimes. Suggest regulator channels for suspected fraud.

## 7) Audit implications

### 7A) Consumer mode
1. **Collect inputs:** DVI report (photos, measurements, color ratings), estimate(s), authorization records (texts or emails), invoice, vehicle year/make/model/mileage, OEM maintenance schedule (user-supplied or looked up), and state.
2. **Per-item evidence check:** For each recommendation, is there a measurement, a spec or minimum, and a photo that plausibly shows *this* component on *this* vehicle? Assign a label from 6.2.
3. **Schedule check:** Flag items not due under the OEM schedule. Cite the FTC's owner's-manual guidance.
4. **Paperwork check (state-aware):** Estimate before work; re-authorization for overruns (CA: any increase; FL: over the greater of $10 or 10%, capped at $50); itemization; new/used/rebuilt parts disclosure; supply fees itemized (CA); mechanic certification number (MI); parts return honored if requested.
5. **Fee check:** Compare quoted or advertised price to the invoice. Flag undisclosed mandatory fees (CA SB 478, MN).
6. **Output:** "Questions to ask the shop" for each flagged item, a records-request checklist (section 4A), and an escalation path (section 4) with the correct agency for the state. Include chargeback timing (60 days from statement) and lien and get-your-car-back notes (CA 3068 10-day records demand; FL 559.917 bond).
7. **Tone:** neutral and evidence-based. Include the safety override and disclaimers. Offer review-writing help only in the facts-plus-opinion style.

### 7B) Shop-owner mode
1. **Evidence standard per finding:** require a measurement, the spec, a clear photo with a reference point (part identity, side, vehicle), and a plain-language note. Flag technicians whose findings lack these. Treat minimum-photo quotas as a risk if they encourage padding.
2. **Severity calibration:** "Red / safety" only when a measurement is at or beyond the spec limit or there is an observable failure. Audit for red ratings on items within spec.
3. **Schedule alignment:** keep maintenance recommendations within the OEM schedule, or label them "shop-recommended, optional" with the reason. Early recommendations were central to the CBC and quick-lube findings.
4. **Authorization trail:** capture name, date, time, contact method, and itemized items and prices for every approval. Retain the texts and emails as part of the RO, 3 years in CA. No work before approval.
5. **Invoice hygiene:** plain descriptions, no acronyms, part condition, itemized supplies (CA bans generic supply charges), certification numbers where required, and a parts-return offer.
6. **Incentive review:** commission or quota plans tied to parts and labor sold are the documented root cause in Sears-type cases. Consider balancing with quality and comeback metrics.
7. **Messaging compliance:** 10DLC registration; CTIA opt-in and STOP/HELP handling; keep promotional content out of transactional DVI texts (FTSA/OTSA risk); honor STOP within 10 business days.
8. **Call recording:** announce recording on authorization calls in all-party states.
9. **Privacy and security:** minimize PII in photos; vendor contracts covering DVI data; Safeguards Rule program if the business is a dealer; check state privacy-law thresholds.
10. **Pricing display:** include mandatory fees in advertised prices (CA, MN). Don't add surprise "environmental" or "shop supply" percentages.
11. **Review response:** CRFA bars anti-review clauses. Respond to reviews with facts; avoid suing over opinion.

## 8) Open questions and uncertainties
- **Recent (2023–2026) enforcement is thin in search results.** The Mavis PA AVC is the main recent unnecessary-repair settlement found. No recent federal (FTC) case on repair upselling and no dealer DVI class action was found. Final outcomes of the Firestone 2016 and Jiffy Lube Fanticola BAR actions were not confirmed.
- **Consumer trust data is dated.** The AAA figure (about two-thirds distrust; 76% cite unnecessary recommendations; 73% overcharging) is from Dec 2016 (https://newsroom.aaa.com/2016/12/u-s-drivers-leery-auto-repair-shops/). Consumer Reports data is satisfaction-focused: independents rated above dealers in 2009, 2012, and 2024 (https://www.consumerreports.org/media-room/press-releases/2009/05/consumer-reports-survey-reveals-higher-satisfaction-with-independent-auto-mechanics/; https://www.ratchetandwrench.com/site-placement/latest-news/news/33038965/survey-drivers-report-higher-satisfaction-with-independent-repair-shops-over-dealerships). CarMD, NHTSA, NADA, and AASP sources were not found to quantify upselling. **[UNVERIFIED gap.]**
- **E-SIGN 7001(c) interaction** with state "written estimate" requirements when only a link is sent is unresolved.
- **TCPA:** Revoke-all was waived to 2027-01-31 (DA 26-12) and then replaced by FCC 26-67. The open point is the 26-67 effective date (30 days after Federal Register publication; watch for the FCC public notice).
- **TILA 1666i** quality-dispute limits ($50, 100 miles, home state) were not re-verified against the primary text.
- **Statute currency:** the CA Civ. 3068 dollar caps, the FL $150 threshold, and the MI pending bill HB 5149 should be checked on official sites (leginfo.legislature.ca.gov, flsenate.gov, legislature.mi.gov). NY citation: GBL 398-d (not VTL); 2025 bills S516/A1385 would add fines, enactment status unknown.
- **Whether DVI photo metadata or original files must be produced** on request: no rule was found. Discovery in small claims or BAR records access may be the only route.
- **State comprehensive privacy-law count and thresholds** as applied to repair shops: not compiled.
- **Arbitration clauses in DVI or e-authorization terms:** prevalence and enforceability not researched.

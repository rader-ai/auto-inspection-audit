# 01 — US Federal Law & Agencies Relevant to Auto Repair Inspections, Estimates and Invoices

## 1) Scope & as-of date

- **As of:** 2026-10-20. Research done 2026-10-20. Federal law changed a lot in 2025–2026, especially EPA tampering enforcement and right-to-repair bills. Re-verify anything marked [UNVERIFIED] or "status" before relying on it.
- **Covers:** US federal statutes, regulations and agency policy that bear on (a) what a technician may lawfully recommend or do, (b) what documentation must exist, and (c) whether a recommendation is legally *required* or merely *sold*. State repair-shop acts (written estimate, return of parts, licensing) are the main consumer-protection law for repair shops, and they are covered by other divisions. **There is no general federal "auto repair fraud" statute or federal written-estimate rule.**
- **Not legal advice.** This file is a reference for an audit skill. It should send serious matters to the appropriate agency or counsel.

## 3) Rules

### 3.1 EPA / Clean Air Act

**(a) Tampering and defeat-device prohibition: CAA §203(a)(3), 42 U.S.C. 7522(a)(3)**
- *Rule:* (A) No one may remove or make inoperative an emission-control device or element of design installed to comply with federal rules. After the vehicle's first sale, this applies to knowing conduct. (B) No one may make, sell, offer or install a part whose principal effect is to bypass, defeat or disable emission controls, where they know or should know it is used that way. https://www.law.cornell.edu/uscode/text/42/7522
- *Repair carve-out:* The statute does not require owners to use OEM parts for maintenance or repair. Temporarily disabling a device to repair or replace it, then reinstalling it so it works, is not tampering (Cornell summary of 7522). [UNVERIFIED: exact statutory placement of the "temporary removal for repair" language. EPA's July 2026 guidance states the same principle.] https://www.law.cornell.edu/uscode/text/42/7522 ; https://www.repairerdrivennews.com/2026/07/02/epa-acts-on-trumps-freedom-to-fix-memo/
- *Penalties (42 U.S.C. 7524(a)):* As enacted, $2,500 per vehicle for a non-manufacturer/dealer violating (3)(A), and $2,500 per part for any (3)(B) defeat-device violation. Manufacturers and dealers face $25,000 per vehicle for (3)(A). https://www.law.cornell.edu/uscode/text/42/7524 . Inflation-adjusted amounts for penalties assessed on or after 2025-01-08 are **$5,911** and **$59,114** (40 CFR 19.4, Table 1). https://www.ecfr.gov/current/title-40/part-19/section-19.4
- *Plain English:* A shop may not delete a DPF, EGR or catalyst, install a "tuner" that disables emission controls, remove O2 sensors, or program out OBD monitors. Each vehicle or part counts as a separate violation. Customer consent and "off-road use" claims are not defenses.

**(b) EPA Tampering Policy (Nov. 23, 2020) and the "reasonable basis" test**
- A repair or aftermarket part is not tampering if there is a *reasonable basis* to believe it will not hurt emissions. Examples: restoring the certified configuration, relying on a parts maker's equivalence claim, testing, EPA certification, or CARB Executive Order. The policy focuses enforcement on **companies and service shops** rather than individuals. It treats **OBD tampering** as separately enforceable, and it does not preempt state anti-tampering laws. https://www.arnoldporter.com/en/perspectives/blogs/environmental-edge/2026/07/new-presidential-memorandum-and-epa-guidance-on-vehicle-tampering

**(c) 2025–2026 enforcement changes (important for currency)**
- **January 2026:** DOJ told prosecutors to drop criminal CAA tampering charges, and pending OBD-tampering criminal cases are being dismissed. DOJ says it will **continue civil enforcement** with EPA. The CAA has a 5-year limitations period, and states may still enforce their own laws. https://www.hunton.com/the-nickel-report/doj-curbs-criminal-prosecutions-for-motor-vehicle-tampering-but-noncompliance-remains-a-costly-proposition ; https://www.arnoldporter.com/en/perspectives/blogs/environmental-edge/2026/07/new-presidential-memorandum-and-epa-guidance-on-vehicle-tampering
- **December 2025:** EPA OECA announced a "Compliance First" enforcement approach (Hunton, above).
- Several diesel tuners convicted of tampering received presidential pardons (trade press). https://thedrive.com/news/trump-pardons-9-diesel-tuners-charged-with-deleting-emissions-equipment
- **June 29, 2026 "Freedom to Fix" Presidential Memorandum:** Directs EPA to clarify policy, speed up aftermarket-part conformity testing, and *consider* deprioritizing civil enforcement against individuals who in good faith restore their **own** vehicle to its original configuration. It does not cover shop-performed deletes. https://www.whitehouse.gov/presidential-actions/2026/06/lowering-the-cost-of-living-by-promoting-the-freedom-to-fix/ ; https://www.repairerdrivennews.com/2026/07/01/trump-signs-freedom-to-fix-memo-focused-on-emission-repairs/
- **~July 1, 2026 EPA actions:**
  - A letter to manufacturers says repairs are lawful if the certified configuration is restored before the vehicle returns to service. It also says OEMs must give independent repairers service information, training and tools on reasonable terms, including for DEF systems. The letter states that it "does not change the law."
  - A letter recognizes SEMA's SC-E program as a "reasonable basis" certification alternative to CARB for aftermarket emission parts.
  - EPA also noted that using a non-certified part may not carry warranty protection.
  - Sources: https://www.epa.gov/newsreleases/epa-delivers-presidents-freedom-fix-memorandum-vehicles-and-equipment ; https://www.epa.gov/newsreleases/epa-implements-new-freedom-fix-presidential-memorandum-gives-sema-greenlight-certify ; https://www.repairerdrivennews.com/2026/07/02/epa-acts-on-trumps-freedom-to-fix-memo/
- *Plain English:* The tampering **statute is unchanged**. Only Congress can change it (SEMA brief: https://sites.sema.org/ext-assets/government-affairs/advocacy/pdfs/2025_09%20_12_brief_ETTN_Trump_EPA_Sept_2025.pdf). Criminal risk is currently low. Civil and state risk remains, especially for shops.

**(d) Aftermarket catalytic converter policy (51 FR 28114, Aug. 5, 1986; EPA fact sheet)**
- **When an aftermarket (non-OE) converter may be installed:** only if (1) the converter is missing, (2) a state/local I/M program has found it damaged or needing replacement, or (3) the vehicle is older than 5 years/50,000 miles (8 years/80,000 miles for MY1995+) **and** a legitimate need is documented (for example, plugged, or exhaust leaks that cannot be repaired). Otherwise the replacement must be OE or a certified equivalent.
- **Aftermarket converters may not be used when:** the existing converter is present and working, the replacement is covered by recall or warranty, or the vehicle is returning from overseas.
- **Installer duties:**
  - If no I/M program requires the replacement, the customer and installer must both sign a statement of the reason.
  - The invoice must show the customer's name and full address, make, model year, mileage, and reason for replacement.
  - Keep invoices and statements for **6 months**. Keep the removed converter, tagged to the customer, for **15 days**.
  - Install the same type (OC/TWC/TWC+OC) in the original location, connect it to any air injection, and replace all original converters unless the part is rated to replace several.
  - Use the correct application per the catalog, and fill out the warranty card.
- **Labeling and warranty:** New converters carry a label in the form N/XX/YYYY/ZZZZ, with a 5-year/50,000-mile shell warranty and 25,000-mile performance warranty. Used converters must be tested and labeled U/…. Installing an untested salvage converter, or one the customer brings in, is a violation.
- Source: https://archive.epa.gov/region02/auto/web/pdf/catcvrts.pdf
- *Note:* The original OE converter is covered by the federal emissions warranty for 8 years/80,000 miles on MY1995+ vehicles (same fact sheet). In 2021 state air agencies asked EPA to replace the 1986 policy. [UNVERIFIED whether EPA has revised it.] https://www.epa.gov/sites/default/files/2021-03/documents/commentsreceived-requestforinfocatalystpolicy.pdf . California and some other states require CARB-approved converters, which other divisions cover.

**(e) Section 609 MVAC refrigerant rules (CAA §609; 40 CFR Part 82 Subpart B)**
- Anyone who services a motor-vehicle A/C system **for consideration** must use EPA-approved recover/recycle equipment and must be trained and certified by an EPA-approved program, such as ASE, MACS or ESCO. (40 CFR 82.34(a)) https://www.ecfr.gov/current/title-40/part-82/section-82.34 ; list of approved programs (page updated 2026-06-25): https://www.epa.gov/mvac/section-609-technician-training-and-certification-programs
- Shops must certify to EPA that they own and properly use approved equipment, identified by make, model and serial number, and that their technicians are certified (40 CFR 82.42(a)). https://www.ecfr.gov/current/title-40/part-82/section-82.42
- Refrigerant-handling equipment sold for MVAC service must meet §82.36. Refrigerant recovered at disposal facilities must be reclaimed or properly recycled before reuse (§82.34(d)–(e)).
- *Plain English:* Every A/C refrigerant line item implies a certified technician and approved machine. R-134a and R-1234yf need separate, dedicated equipment and fittings. [UNVERIFIED: specific SAE J2788/J2843 citations were not re-checked.]

**(f) AIM Act HFC phasedown / Technology Transitions (40 CFR Part 84; final rule 88 FR, Oct. 24, 2023)**
- New **light-duty passenger vehicle** A/C must use refrigerant with GWP of 150 or less starting with **MY2025**, and no earlier than 2024-10-24. This effectively ends R-134a, which has a GWP of about 1,430, in new LDVs and moves them to R-1234yf. Medium-duty passenger vehicles, HD pickups and complete HD vans follow from **MY2028**, and listed nonroad equipment from 2028-01-01. https://www.epa.gov/climate-hfcs-reduction/technology-transitions-hfc-restrictions-sector
- A May 26, 2026 reconsideration rule (91 FR 31284) changed other sectors and does **not** appear to change the MVAC dates. https://www.hklaw.com/en/insights/publications/2026/05/epa-finalizes-changes-to-technology-transitions-provisions
- The restriction applies to manufacturing new vehicles. Servicing existing R-134a vehicles with R-134a remains lawful. [UNVERIFIED: confirm in the rule text that there is no servicing restriction.] SNAP (CAA §612) sets which substitute refrigerants are acceptable for MVAC. "Retrofitting" a yf system to 134a or another unlisted refrigerant is a red flag. [UNVERIFIED: SNAP MVAC listings were not re-fetched.]

**(g) Used oil (40 CFR Part 279), briefly.** Generators must store used oil in tanks or containers that are in good condition, not leaking, and labeled "Used Oil," and must clean up releases. https://www.ecfr.gov/current/title-40/part-279/section-279.22 . This matters to an audit only as a source for "hazmat/disposal fee" line items. Federal law does not set a fee amount.

**(h) EPA's role in state I/M programs (40 CFR Part 51 Subpart S)**
- I/M programs are required in certain ozone and CO nonattainment areas. EPA sets minimum program design, and states run the programs through their SIPs (§51.350). https://www.ecfr.gov/current/title-40/part-51/section-51.350
- For MY1996+ light-duty vehicles, the OBD check per 40 CFR 85.2222 may replace tailpipe testing. Vehicles with unset readiness monitors, or with missing or leaking exhaust, can be rejected from testing (§51.357). https://www.ecfr.gov/current/title-40/part-51/section-51.357
- To get a retest, the motorist needs a completed repair form listing the repairs performed, the recommended repairs not performed, and the facility. Enhanced programs monitor and publish repair-facility performance (§51.369(b)). https://www.ecfr.gov/current/title-40/part-51/section-51.369
- Waivers require repairs by a "recognized repair technician." Spending on tampering-related repairs does **not** count toward the waiver minimum (§51.360). https://www.ecfr.gov/current/title-40/part-51/section-51.360

### 3.2 NHTSA / Vehicle Safety Act

**(a) "Make inoperative": 49 U.S.C. 30122**
- *Rule:* Manufacturers, distributors, dealers, rental companies and **motor vehicle repair businesses** (any business offering repair for pay) may not knowingly make inoperative any part of a device or element of design installed to comply with an FMVSS. The exception is when they reasonably believe the vehicle won't be driven while the item is inoperative, apart from testing during repair. https://www.law.cornell.edu/uscode/text/49/30122
- *Exemptions:* 49 CFR Part 595 covers disability modifications (§595.7). The modifier must attach a label and give the owner a document, keeping a copy for 5 years, and only the listed FMVSS may be affected. https://www.law.cornell.edu/cfr/text/49/595.7
- *Plain English for an auditor:* The ban reaches items an FMVSS **requires**. Examples include air bags and seat belts (FMVSS 208/209), the rear-visibility camera (FMVSS 111), TPMS (FMVSS 138), ESC (FMVSS 126), and lighting (FMVSS 108). Disabling, bypassing (such as an air-bag resistor "fix") or not reconnecting these is the risk. Most ADAS features, such as lane-keeping and adaptive cruise, are not yet FMVSS-required. **FMVSS 127 (AEB)** has a compliance date of **2029-09-01**, which is under industry challenge with no 2026 change found. https://www.federalregister.gov/documents/2024/11/26/2024-27349/federal-motor-vehicle-safety-standards-automatic-emergency-braking-systems-for-light-vehicles ; https://www.nelsonmullins.com/insights/blogs/driving-forward-developments-in-transportation-law-and-innovation/all/the-road-ahead-for-fmvss-127-whither-the-automatic-emergency-braking-mandate . A skipped ADAS calibration is therefore mainly a state-law, negligence and OEM-procedure issue rather than a clear §30122 violation. [UNVERIFIED: no NHTSA Chief Counsel interpretation on ADAS calibration under §30122 was located.]
- No federal ADAS calibration standard exists yet. H.R. 7389 §221, which has not been enacted, would order an NHTSA study and calibration recommendations. https://www.fleetmaintenance.com/equipment/government-and-regulations/article/55382001/house-committee-on-energy-and-commerce-passes-motor-vehicle-modernization-act-industry-moves-closer-to-federal-right-to-repair-adas-laws

**(b) Counterfeit / substandard air bags**
- No federal statute aimed specifically at installing counterfeit air bags was found. [UNVERIFIED. General federal counterfeit-goods law, 18 U.S.C. 2320, and §30122 may apply.] States are filling the gap, for example Idaho in 2026. https://www.repairerdrivennews.com/2026/04/06/idaho-governor-signs-counterfeit-and-non-functional-airbag-prohibition-bill/
- NHTSA has warned since 2012, and again in 2024 and January 2026, about counterfeit and substandard Chinese replacement inflators, notably DTN, linked to deaths. It urges inspection of any vehicle with a prior deployment, with priority for salvage and rebuilt titles. Scan tools cannot reliably detect counterfeits. https://www.nhtsa.gov/press-releases/consumer-alert-nhtsa-alerts-used-car-owners-buyers-dangerous-substandard-replacement ; https://www.nhtsa.gov/press-releases/urgent-warning-two-more-deaths-substandard-dangerous-chinese-air-bag-inflators ; https://static.nhtsa.gov/odi/tsbs/2012/MC-10093270-2280.pdf

**(c) Recalls: 49 U.S.C. 30118–30120**
- Dealers may not sell or lease **new** vehicles or new equipment in inventory once notified of a recall until it is remedied (§30120(i)). Rental companies must ground covered vehicles within 24 or 48 hours. No one may sell recalled replacement **equipment, including tires,** for installation unless it has been remedied (§30120(j)). https://www.law.cornell.edu/uscode/text/49/30120
- The free remedy does not apply when the vehicle was first sold more than **15 years** before the recall, or the tire more than **5 years** before (§30120(g)). Same source.
- **No federal law requires independent shops, or dealers selling *used* vehicles, to check for or perform open recalls.** Recall remedies are performed free by manufacturer-authorized dealers. [UNVERIFIED as a negative; no contrary federal provision found.] The FTC has acted against *deceptive* recall-themed service mailers (2018) and against used-car ads touting "inspections" without disclosing open recalls (2016). https://www.ftc.gov/news-events/news/press-releases/2018/10/washington-dc-area-car-dealerships-marketing-firm-settle-deceptive-advertising-charges
- H.R. 7389 would allow electronic recall notices. It has not been enacted. https://www.fleetmaintenance.com/equipment/government-and-regulations/article/55382001/house-committee-on-energy-and-commerce-passes-motor-vehicle-modernization-act-industry-moves-closer-to-federal-right-to-repair-adas-laws

**(d) Tire registration: 49 CFR 574.8**
- When selling new tires, independent dealers must either give the buyer a registration form pre-filled with the full TIN and dealer identity, or register the tires for the buyer themselves (paper or electronic). Manufacturer-owned dealers must record and submit the TINs. https://www.ecfr.gov/current/title-49/part-574/section-574.8
- *Plain English:* Invoices for new tires should record the full DOT TINs. A missing TIN is a documentation gap that also prevents recall notification.

**(e) Inspection numbers that people often mis-cite**
- FMVSS set *manufacturing* performance standards. They generally do not set in-service wear limits for passenger cars. Those come from state inspection laws, which other divisions cover, and from OEM specifications.
- The 2/32" tread figure is commonly cited, but the federal rule is **FMCSA 49 CFR 393.75**, which applies to *commercial* vehicles: 4/32" on steer tires of trucks and buses, 2/32" elsewhere, with no exposed ply, no separation, and no audible leak. https://www.ecfr.gov/current/title-49/part-393/section-393.75
- An auditor should not treat "below 4/32" on a passenger car as a federal violation.

### 3.3 Federal odometer law: 49 U.S.C. 32701 et seq.; 49 CFR Part 580
- During service, repair or replacement of an odometer or cluster, the reading must stay the same. If that is impossible, the odometer must be set to **zero**, and a written notice must be attached to the **left door frame** showing the prior mileage and the date of service. Removing or altering that notice with intent to defraud is prohibited (§32704). https://www.law.cornell.edu/uscode/text/49/32704
- Altering or rolling back mileage is separately prohibited (§32703). Disclosures on transfer fall under Part 580, which other divisions cover. [Part 580 text not re-fetched.]
- *Plain English:* Any invoice line for cluster replacement, cluster repair or "mileage programming" should match a door-frame sticker or a documented mileage-preserving procedure.

### 3.4 FTC

**(a) FTC Act §5, 15 U.S.C. 45(a):** prohibits unfair or deceptive acts or practices. Misrepresenting that a repair is needed, billing for work not done, or making false "inspection" claims are classic deception theories. In practice, though, **auto-repair-shop fraud is enforced mainly by state AGs and state repair bureaus.** No recent FTC case against a repair shop for unnecessary repairs was found. [UNVERIFIED as a negative.]
- FTC consumer guidance from 2012 tells consumers to:
  - get a written estimate and require approval before work exceeds it;
  - get an itemized repair order with odometer readings in and out;
  - ask for the old parts;
  - compare recommendations with the owner's manual schedule;
  - get second opinions, including from diagnostic-only shops;
  - have the technician rule out simple causes (connections, terminals) before replacing an alternator, battery or starter;
  - note that "tune-ups" may not apply to modern vehicles.
- Source: https://consumer.ftc.gov/articles/auto-repair-basics

**(b) Magnuson-Moss Warranty Act anti-tying: 15 U.S.C. 2302(c); 16 CFR 700.10**
- A warrantor cannot make warranty coverage depend on using branded parts or authorized service unless those are provided free. Language such as "void if serviced by anyone other than an authorized dealer" or "use only [brand] parts" is prohibited and deceptive. A warrantor *may* deny a claim if it shows that the non-OEM part or the service actually **caused** the damage. https://www.law.cornell.edu/cfr/text/16/700.10
- The CAA separately confirms that owners need not use OEM parts for maintenance (42 U.S.C. 7522). EPA's July 2026 guidance repeats this for emission parts, with a caveat about non-certified parts.
- *Enforcement:*
  - FTC final orders against Harley-Davidson and MWE (Westinghouse) on 2022-10-27 require warranties to state that independent repair and third-party parts do not void coverage. https://www.ftc.gov/news-events/news/press-releases/2022/10/ftc-approves-final-orders-right-repair-cases-against-harley-davidson-mwe-investments-weber
  - In July 2024, FTC staff sent warning letters to 8 companies about tie-in and "warranty void if removed" stickers. https://search.ftc.gov/news-events/news/press-releases/2024/07/ftc-warns-companies-stop-warranty-practices-harm-consumers-right-repair
  - The FTC issued a Policy Statement on repair restrictions in July 2021 (same sources).
  - No 2025–2026 FTC MMWA auto actions were found. [UNVERIFIED]

**(c) Used Car Rule, 16 CFR Part 455 (brief):** Dealers must display a Buyers Guide (as-is or warranty), and it is deceptive to misrepresent a vehicle's mechanical condition or warranty. https://www.ecfr.gov/current/title-16/part-455 . This is relevant when a dealer's pre-sale "multi-point inspection" is used in marketing. The last major revision was in 2016. [UNVERIFIED whether any 2024–2026 amendment exists.]

**(d) CARS Rule (Combating Auto Retail Scams):** **Vacated** by the 5th Circuit on 2025-01-27 (NADA v. FTC) for procedural error. It was never in effect and covered dealer sales and F&I, not repair. https://www.hklaw.com/en/insights/publications/2025/02/fifth-circuit-strikes-down-ftcs-auto-retail-scam-rule

### 3.5 OSHA (brief)
- **Asbestos, 29 CFR 1910.1001(f)(3) and mandatory Appendix F:** For brake and clutch inspection, disassembly, repair and assembly, employers must use either a negative-pressure enclosure with HEPA vacuum or a low-pressure/wet cleaning method (or a demonstrated equivalent). Shops doing 5 or fewer brake or clutch jobs per week may use the wet-wipe method. Blowing dust off with compressed air is not permitted unless a capture system is used. https://www.ecfr.gov/current/title-29/part-1910/section-1910.1001
- **Vehicle lifts:** No lift-specific OSHA standard. Lifts fall under the General Duty Clause (OSH Act §5(a)(1)) and industry standards (ANSI/ALI ALOIM). [UNVERIFIED: not re-fetched.]
- *Audit relevance:* An "environmental/shop supplies" line item is legitimate but should not be a disguised labor charge. Asbestos rules do not justify a brake replacement.

### 3.7 E-SIGN and TCPA (high level)
- **E-SIGN, 15 U.S.C. 7001:** An e-signature or electronic record cannot be denied effect *solely* because it is electronic. This makes app or text approvals of digital inspection estimates valid in principle. Where a law requires that a consumer receive information *in writing*, an electronic copy satisfies that law only after E-SIGN consumer consent: disclosures about paper copies, withdrawal, and hardware/software, plus a demonstrated ability to access the format. Records must be retainable and reproducible (§7001(e)). https://www.law.cornell.edu/uscode/text/15/7001 . Many states' written-estimate and authorization rules are such "in writing" requirements, and state UETA also applies (other divisions).
- **TCPA, 47 U.S.C. 227; 47 CFR 64.1200:** Autodialed or prerecorded calls and texts need prior express consent, and *written* consent for marketing. Consumers may revoke consent by **any reasonable method**. "STOP," "quit," "cancel," "unsubscribe" and similar replies count per se. Revocations must be honored **within 10 business days**, and callers cannot require a single exclusive opt-out channel (§64.1200(a)(10)). https://www.ecfr.gov/current/title-47/chapter-I/subchapter-B/part-64/subpart-L/section-64.1200
  - A text sending the customer's own inspection link for a vehicle they dropped off is generally informational. Upsell or "service due" campaigns are marketing.

## 4) Audit implications

### 4.1 Checks that apply to both audiences

1. **Emissions-system recommendations**
   - Any line item that removes, deletes, "tunes off," straight-pipes, or installs a "test pipe," "O2 spacer/defouler," "EGR block-off," "DPF/DEF delete," or "emissions off-road tune" is a **federal tampering red flag** under CAA 203(a)(3), whatever the customer wants. Civil exposure is about $5,911 per vehicle or part.
   - An O2-sensor "simulator" or spacer used to clear a catalyst-efficiency code (P0420/P0430) is a defeat-device pattern.
2. **Catalytic converter replacement**
   - Request: the documented need (failed efficiency test with fuel trims, O2/AFR data and misfire history ruled out; a backpressure test; a physical rattle or plug); the vehicle's age and mileage; whether the converter is still under the 8-year/80,000-mile federal emissions warranty (MY1995+) or a recall, in which case send it to the dealer at no cost; the EPA/CARB label of the part installed; and the signed reason statement.
   - Red flags: an aftermarket converter on a vehicle under 8 years/80,000 miles with no I/M order; "universal" parts without an application match; no customer signature; a removed converter not kept or tagged; a catalyst replaced without fixing the upstream cause (misfire, oil burning, coolant), which will kill the new one.
3. **A/C service**
   - Request: the technician's §609 certificate number and program, and the recovery machine model for the refrigerant type.
   - Check: the refrigerant type matches the underhood label (R-134a vs R-1234yf); charge weight matches the OEM spec; the line items include recovery/evacuation/vacuum and a leak test. A "recharge only" with no leak test on a system that was low suggests a leak was ignored.
   - Red flags: "converted yf to 134a"; "top-off" with sealant and no disclosure; charging by "feel"; an uncertified technician.
4. **Safety-system "make inoperative"**
   - Red flags: SRS light "cleared" with no part replaced; a resistor across an air-bag connector; a seat-belt pretensioner bypassed; TPMS sensors removed or disabled ("TPMS delete" or programming off); backup camera left disconnected after body or glass work.
   - Request the SRS scan before and after, and the part numbers and source for any air bag or inflator.
   - Treat any air bag from an unknown or online source, or any vehicle with a salvage/rebuilt title and prior deployment, as a NHTSA counterfeit/DTN inflator concern.
5. **ADAS**
   - Windshield, bumper, alignment or ride-height work on a vehicle with cameras or radar should show OEM-required calibration, or a documented determination that none was required, with before and after reports. Federal law does not yet mandate this. Treat it as an OEM-procedure, state-law and negligence issue, and flag "calibration performed" with no report.
6. **Recalls**
   - Cross-check the VIN on NHTSA's recall lookup (nhtsa.gov/recalls). If the shop recommends paid repair of a component that is under an open recall or extended warranty/service campaign, that is a significant finding. The consumer can get it fixed free at a dealer, unless the vehicle is over 15 years old.
   - An independent shop has no federal duty to check recalls. A good practice is to disclose open recalls.
7. **Tires**
   - New-tire invoices should list the full DOT TINs and show that registration was handled (49 CFR 574.8).
   - "Unsafe, below 4/32" on a passenger car is a *recommendation*, not a federal limit. The FMCSA 4/32 and 2/32 numbers apply only to CMVs. Check state law and OEM specifications (other divisions).
   - Check the DOT date code if the tires are called "too old." Age guidance is OEM or industry, not federal.
8. **Odometer / cluster work**
   - A cluster replacement or repair line must match either a "mileage preserved" note or a door-frame notice showing prior mileage and date.
   - Odometer readings in and out on the RO should be consistent with each other and with earlier ROs. A large discrepancy is a §32703/32704 red flag.
9. **Warranty-related upsells**
   - Statements like "you must do this at the dealer," "you must use OEM parts or your warranty is void," or "aftermarket filter voids warranty" are **false** under MMWA/16 CFR 700.10 unless the item is free under the warranty.
   - Conversely, a dealer denying warranty must show that the aftermarket part or service *caused* the failure. Request that causation evidence in writing.
10. **Maintenance-schedule inflation.** Per FTC guidance, compare recommended services with the owner's manual schedule for the vehicle's mileage and conditions. A "dealer/shop schedule" that exceeds the OEM schedule (for example, flushes at a fraction of the OEM interval, or "tune-ups" on coil-on-plug engines) should be flagged as *beyond need* unless an inspection finding justifies it.
11. **Digital approvals and texting**
    - The audit trail for each approval should include who approved, when, which line items, the dollar amount, and the channel. A tap-to-approve is valid under E-SIGN, but where state law requires a written estimate or authorization, check that E-SIGN consent was obtained.
    - Approvals of a *revised* amount need a new approval record.
    - Marketing texts after a "STOP" reply are a TCPA issue.
12. **I/M-related repairs.** In I/M areas, the repair form must list both the repairs done and the repairs recommended but not done (§51.369(b)(3)). Tampering-repair costs do not count toward the waiver minimum. A shop that says "you need $X more of repairs to qualify for a waiver" should be checked against the state's waiver limit and rules.

### 4.2 Consumer-specific
- **Ask for:** the DVI photos and measurements behind each "red" item, the codes and freeze-frame data, the refrigerant and technician certification, converter paperwork, tire TINs, and the old parts. Federal law does not require old parts be returned, but many states do.
- **Free alternatives:** run a VIN recall check before paying; check emissions-warranty coverage (8 years/80,000 miles for catalytic converter and ECM on MY1995+); confirm that warranty work can be done at an independent shop.
- **Do not accept** "delete" or "off-road" emissions work. Federal civil exposure, and state exposure, can attach to the shop and also complicate registration and inspection. It does not reduce your risk at a state I/M test.
- **Where to complain:**
  - Your state AG or repair bureau is the primary route.
  - FTC (reportfraud.ftc.gov) for warranty-tying or deceptive practices.
  - EPA (epa.gov "report a violation") for tampering.
  - NHTSA (nhtsa.gov) for safety defects or counterfeit air bags.
  - This skill should only *point to* these channels. It does not file anything.

### 4.3 Shop-owner-specific
- **Compliance evidence to keep on file:**
  - §609 certificates for each A/C technician, plus the equipment certification sent to the EPA regional office (82.42).
  - Converter invoices and signed reason statements for 6 months, and removed converters tagged for 15 days.
  - Tire TIN registration records.
  - Odometer notices.
  - SRS and ADAS pre/post scans and calibration reports.
  - OSHA brake-dust method documentation.
  - E-SIGN consent records.
  - TCPA consent and opt-out logs, with STOP honored within 10 business days.
- **Technician-quality audit:** Flag any technician recommendation in the deletes/defeat-device or "make inoperative" categories as a **hard stop**. Flag converters without documented diagnostics, and "warranty void" scripting at the counter, as **policy violations** that create civil exposure under CAA 7524 (per vehicle or part) and FTC §5/MMWA exposure.
- **Advisor scripts:** Do not tell customers that independent or aftermarket parts void warranties, and do not tell them a recall must be paid for. Disclose open recalls you find. Tag OEM-required vs shop-recommended vs condition-based items separately. That separation is what lets "beyond need" be audited.
- **2025–2026 enforcement climate:** Criminal tampering risk is lower federally, but civil enforcement continues, the 5-year limitations period runs, state AGs and air agencies are active, and the 2026 "Freedom to Fix" relief targets *individual owners restoring OEM configuration*, not shops. Do not change shop policy because of the pardons or the DOJ memo.

## 5) Open questions / uncertainties
1. **Status of H.R. 7389 / H.R. 8870 and the REPAIR Act after June 2026.** No floor vote was confirmed. A transportation reauthorization deadline was 2026-09-30, and its outcome is unknown. [UNVERIFIED]
2. **EPA tampering policy after July 2026.** It is unclear whether EPA formally deprioritized enforcement against individuals, whether the OBD carve-out in the 2020 policy still stands, and whether the 1986 aftermarket converter policy has been revised or replaced, given SEMA certification. [UNVERIFIED]
3. **Exact statutory text of the CAA "temporary removal for repair" exception.** The Cornell summary states it, but placement and wording were not checked against the US Code PDF.
4. **AIM Act MVAC.** Whether there is any servicing restriction or SNAP change affecting R-134a service of existing vehicles. The MY2025 LDV date was confirmed on EPA's sector table, but the regulatory text was not read in full.
5. **TCPA.** The current status of the one-to-one consent vacatur and the delayed "revoke-all" provision. The search budget was exhausted before this could be checked.
6. **FTC.** Whether any 2025–2026 MMWA or auto-repair enforcement exists under the current Commission, and whether the Used Car Rule has been amended since 2016.
7. **Counterfeit air bags.** Whether any federal statute specifically prohibits installing them. None was found.
8. **ADAS and §30122.** No NHTSA interpretation was found on whether a failure to calibrate counts as "making inoperative." It likely does not, because most ADAS is not FMVSS-required until FMVSS 127 takes effect in 2029, and that date itself is uncertain.
9. **Recalls on used vehicles.** The negative (no federal duty for independent shops or used-car dealers) is based on the absence of contrary text in §30120. NHTSA's recall page could not be fetched (HTTP 403).

## 3) Rules (rule — citation — plain-English meaning)

### 3.1 Registration, coverage, signage

1. **Who is an ARD.** Anyone who, for compensation, diagnoses or repairs vehicle malfunctions, or collects payment for repairs it refers or sublets to someone else. — B&P §9880.1(a) — https://california.public.law/codes/business_and_professions_code_section_9880.1 . BAR's 2025 rule (eff. Oct 1, 2025) confirms that referral and sublet brokers must register. — https://bar.ca.gov/arsc/newsletters/newsletter/fall-2025/legislation-and-regulations-update
2. **What counts as repair.** All maintenance and repair by an ARD, including body work. Excluded: work under a pre-arranged *commercial business agreement* (fleet; warranties and service plans sold to the public don't count) and *roadside services* (motor clubs and permitted tow operators). — B&P §9880.1(e),(k),(l). Warranty work is presumed to be compensated. — 16 CCR §3303(g), https://www.law.cornell.edu/regulations/california/16-CCR-3303
3. **"Preventative maintenance" (statutory list).** Tire pressure checks and rotation; fluid and filter changes; belts; wiper blades; bulbs and headlamp restoration; fuel-induction service; additives added through fill points; top-offs; plus the removal and reinstallation needed to do them. — B&P §9880.1(j). This matters because these services can skip a written estimate under §9884.9(e) (see 3.2).
4. **"Customer" excludes the shop and the insurer.** The customer is the person who presents the vehicle and authorizes work. The ARD, its staff and an insurer on a claim are not customers. — B&P §9880.1(f).
5. **Registration is mandatory and must be current.** — B&P §9884.6 — https://california.public.law/codes/business_and_professions_code_section_9884.6 . Auto body shops must declare body work on the application and attest to their local business license, resale permit, EPA hazardous-waste ID and air-district spray-booth permit. — B&P §9889.52 — https://california.public.law/codes/business_and_professions_code_section_9889.52
6. **Recent registration and administrative changes.** Renewal requirements changed Apr 1, 2024. An omnibus rule (Apr 1, 2025) defined "change of ownership," "change of address" and "Responsible Managing Employee." The change-of-address rule (adopted Mar 30, 2026, eff. **Jul 1, 2026**) sets a 14-day deadline to report an address change and 30 days for other material changes. Mobile and referral ARDs: a separate mobile registration is needed outside a set radius (eff. Oct 1, 2025). — https://www.bar.ca.gov/Regulatory-actions ; https://bar.ca.gov/arsc/newsletters/newsletter/fall-2025/legislation-and-regulations-update
7. **Signs and display.** Each location must post BAR's official ARD sign where customers can see it. The sign gives BAR's phone number and website and tells customers they may ask for replaced parts back, and must ask when the work order is written. — B&P §9884.17, https://california.public.law/codes/business_and_professions_code_section_9884.17 ; 16 CCR §§3351.3–3351.4. Licenses must be displayed under glass. Mobile ARDs hand the customer a paper copy of the sign with each work order and show their name, ARD number and phone in 72-point type on the vehicle. Smog stations post a "Notice to Consumers" (H&S §44017.3; 16 CCR §3340.22.2). STAR and VSSI stations post their own signs. — https://www.bar.ca.gov/licensee-signs
8. **Online ads (eff. Oct 1, 2025).** Every internet ad must show the BAR-registered business name, ARD number and phone number. Multi-location ads must show this for each location, or link to a site that does. — 16 CCR §3371.2 — https://www.bar.ca.gov/licensee-signs . All ads and signs must use the name and address on the registration. — 16 CCR §3371, https://www.law.cornell.edu/regulations/california/16-CCR-3371

### 3.2 Estimates

9. **A written estimate is required before any work or charges.** The ARD must give a written estimate of parts and labor for a *specific job*. No work may be done and no charges may accrue until the customer authorizes it. — B&P §9884.9(a), https://california.public.law/codes/business_and_professions_code_section_9884.9 ; 16 CCR §3353 preamble and (a), https://www.law.cornell.edu/regulations/california/16-CCR-3353
10. **"Specific job" must be in plain language (eff. Jul 1, 2025).** It means all repairs in one transaction. Each repair must be listed in terms a layperson understands, without trade jargon. — 16 CCR §3352(b), https://www.law.cornell.edu/regulations/california/16-CCR-3352
11. **Parts are presumed new.** Every part is treated as new unless the estimate labels it used, rebuilt or reconditioned. — 16 CCR §3353(a).
12. **Auto body and collision estimates must be itemized.** Labor and parts are listed separately and each part is identified. New replacement crash parts must be OEM unless labeled "non-OEM aftermarket." BAR says vague labels such as "Opt-OEM" or "Like Kind Quality" do not satisfy the rule. Paint and materials may be estimated by an hourly rate or at actual cost (the two methods added to Write It Right in Jan 2026). — B&P §9884.9(c); 16 CCR §3353(b); https://www.bar.ca.gov/wir ; https://fenderbender.com/55341723
13. **Estimates based on a third-party estimate (eff. Jul 1, 2025).** If the ARD works from an insurer's or warranty company's estimate, it must:
    - tell the customer that estimate is governed by the Fair Claims Settlement Practices Regulations;
    - record its intent to repair per that estimate, plus the payor's name, the claim number and the payor's total;
    - attach the payor's estimate;
    - notify the payor before going ahead with customer-approved changes that alter payment.

    Before authorization, the ARD must also list the payor-approved amount if known. If it is not known, the estimate must carry a notice that the customer must contact the payor for payment approval. — 16 CCR §3353(b)(1),(d); "third-party payor" defined at §3303(w).
14. **Teardown estimates (amended Jul 1, 2025).** A teardown (disassembly needed to assess condition) is itself a repair. — §3352(e). Before teardown, the estimate must show:
    - the teardown cost and a general description of the area to be disassembled (an itemized list of every part removed is not required);
    - the reassembly cost;
    - parts normally destroyed in teardown (gaskets, seals, O-rings);
    - a warning, where applicable, that original condition may not be restorable;
    - a **maximum reassembly time**.

    After teardown, the shop must give a **second, itemized estimate** and get authorization for the repair or for reassembly. If the customer declines, that goes on the teardown invoice. — 16 CCR §3353(c); https://www.bar.ca.gov/wir ; https://www.repairerdrivennews.com/2026/01/08/california-bar-updates-write-it-right-guide-on-estimates/
15. **Sublet work.** The estimate must state any work that may be done by an outside party. Sublets need the customer's consent unless the customer can't reasonably be notified. The ARD stays fully responsible and must name the sublet facility on request. Smog inspections may not be sublet. — B&P §9884.9(b); 16 CCR §3353(f); https://www.bar.ca.gov/wir
16. **Towing.** If the ARD provides or arranges a tow, it must disclose towing fees on a **separate document** from the estimate and invoice before taking payment, following Vehicle Code §22651.07, and get separate authorization. — 16 CCR §3353(e) (eff. Jul 1, 2025).
17. **Preventive maintenance exemption.** No written estimate is needed for §9880.1(j) services if the customer authorizes and the service is free, or its total price is conspicuously posted or shown to and acknowledged by the customer. — B&P §9884.9(e).
18. **What estimates may and may not include (BAR guidance):**
    - sales tax goes on the invoice only;
    - a hazardous or toxic waste disposal charge must be itemized separately with the ARD's EPA ID number;
    - generic "shop supplies" may not be charged; only supplies actually used and shown on the estimate;
    - core charges are allowed for exchange parts, but not for warranty parts;
    - Smog stations must post their labor rate.

    — https://www.bar.ca.gov/wir
19. **Specialized estimates.** Automatic transmissions: the unit must be inspected before removal, including control-module code retrieval on electronic units, and the customer told if minor or external service would likely fix it. "Rebuilt" and similar words are allowed only after the specified work is done. "Exchanged" must be paired with new, used, rebuilt or similar. The standards may not be used to push a rebuild when a cheaper fix is what the customer wants. — 16 CCR §3361.1, https://www.law.cornell.edu/regulations/california/16-CCR-3361.1 . Windshields: OEM or non-OEM must be stated, plus a drive-away wait warning. A/C: words like "service," "inspection," "diagnosis," "top off" or "performance check" trigger a mandatory 16-point inspection. — 16 CCR §3366, https://www.law.cornell.edu/regulations/california/16-CCR-3366

### 3.3 Authorization and exceeding the estimate

20. **Definitions.** "Authorization" means consent given by handwritten signature, or by an oral or electronic statement approving a specific job. "Oral" includes in person, phone, or any electronic channel where the voice is heard. "Electronic" covers digital, wireless, optical and similar means, so it includes texts, email and app approvals. — 16 CCR §3352(f)–(h).
21. **No diagnosis or repair, even no-charge or warranty work, without specific authorization.** — 16 CCR §3353.1(a), https://www.law.cornell.edu/regulations/california/16-CCR-3353.1
22. **What must be recorded for each type of authorization** (on the estimate or on supplemental documents tied to it):

    | Type | Required record |
    |---|---|
    | Written | Signature and date |
    | Oral | Date, time, name of the authorizer, and the phone number called (if a call was placed) |
    | Electronic | Date, time, name of the authorizer, and the phone number or email used |

    — 16 CCR §3353.1(b)–(d).
23. **Emails and texts are part of the record.** Supplemental documents, including chains of emails and texts, must each be uniquely identified and kept with the same transaction. — 16 CCR §3353.1(e); §3358(e).
24. **Exceeding the estimate.** Before any extra diagnosis or repair, or any charge above the authorized price, the shop must give a **revised estimate** listing the added parts and labor, their cost and the **new total**, then obtain and record authorization. — 16 CCR §3354(a), https://www.law.cornell.edu/regulations/california/16-CCR-3354 ; B&P §9884.9(a). Consent must come *after* the shop discovers the estimate is insufficient and *before* the extra work. — B&P §9884.9(a)
25. **Recording oral or electronic increases.** The work order must record the date, time, authorizer, phone or email, the added parts and labor, and the added cost. The invoice must repeat this, *or* the customer signs or initials the statutory acknowledgment of notice and oral approval of the increase. — B&P §9884.9(a); 16 CCR §3356(e).
26. **No silent changes.** Once an itemized work order is authorized, the method of repair or the parts supplied may not change without new authorization. — 16 CCR §3354(b).
27. **Designated person.** At initial authorization, the customer may name someone else to approve additional work. The designation has a required title and requires the designee's name and contact details, the customer's signature and date, and the work-order number. The ARD, an insurer on the claim, and their agents may never be the designee. — B&P §9884.9(d); 16 CCR §3354(c).
28. **After-hours drop-off or towed-in vehicles ("unusual circumstances").** Before any diagnosis, the shop must prepare an estimate, communicate it, and get documented authorization. A note left with the car is not authorization. — 16 CCR §3353.2, https://www.law.cornell.edu/regulations/california/16-CCR-3353.2 ; https://www.bar.ca.gov/wir
29. **Copies.** The customer must get a copy of any document they sign, immediately. Having a customer sign a work order that omits the requested repairs or the odometer reading is a ground for discipline. — B&P §9884.7(a)(2)–(3).

### 3.4 Invoices and parts identification

30. **Statutory invoice contents.** All work, including warranty work, goes on an invoice. Each service and part is described, with labor and parts separated, separate subtotals, and tax shown. Used, rebuilt or reconditioned parts must be disclosed, and so must a component system that mixes new and non-new parts. Crash parts must be identified as OEM or non-OEM aftermarket. One copy goes to the customer and one is kept. — B&P §9884.8, https://california.public.law/codes/business_and_professions_code_section_9884.8
31. **Regulatory invoice contents.** — 16 CCR §3356, https://www.law.cornell.edu/regulations/california/16-CCR-3356
    - The ARD number, plus the business name and address *exactly as BAR has them*.
    - Every service, including diagnosis and warranty work, priced individually.
    - Every part priced and labeled new, used, reconditioned, rebuilt, OEM crash part, or non-OEM aftermarket crash part. A kit may be one line if brand and part number are shown.
    - A labor subtotal, a parts subtotal, tax, and a grand total.
    - Authorization details for unusual-circumstance and oral or electronic approvals.
    - Teardown declines and unreturned parts.
    - A legible copy for the customer.
32. **Only listed parts can be charged. Generic charges are banned.** An item not listed as a part can't be billed. Separate charges for "shop supplies," "miscellaneous parts," or fees for communicating with the smog database are prohibited. — 16 CCR §3356(f)–(g). BAR also says acronyms should not be used on invoices. — https://www.bar.ca.gov/wir
33. **Specialized invoices.**
    - Smog: attach the Vehicle Inspection Report (16 CCR §3340.41(a)).
    - A/C: record high- and low-side pressures and the center outlet temperature (§3366).
    - Windshield: installation date and time, cure time, OEM or non-OEM.
    - Mobile repair: the location of the repair.
    - Ball joints: the measured wear of the replaced joint *and* the manufacturer's maximum allowable wear (§3360.2).

    — https://www.bar.ca.gov/wir ; https://www.law.cornell.edu/regulations/california/16-CCR-3360.2

### 3.5 Replaced parts, records, trade standards

34. **Return of replaced parts.** If the customer asks at the work-order or estimate stage, replaced parts must be returned at completion. Exempt: parts too large or heavy under regulation, exchange (core) parts, and warranty parts that must go back to the manufacturer. For exempt parts the shop must offer to *show* the part, tell the customer orally, and note on the work order and invoice that it can't be returned. Nothing has to be shown if the part was not charged. — B&P §9884.10, https://california.public.law/codes/business_and_professions_code_section_9884.10 ; 16 CCR §3355, https://www.law.cornell.edu/regulations/california/16-CCR-3355
35. **Record retention: 3 years.** Keep invoices (including supplier invoices for parts and sublet labor), estimates with the customer's authorization records, and work orders with supplemental authorization records. Paper or electronic is fine. Records must be open to BAR during business hours and share a unique transaction identifier. — B&P §9884.11, https://california.public.law/codes/business_and_professions_code_section_9884.11 ; 16 CCR §3358, https://www.law.cornell.edu/regulations/california/16-CCR-3358
36. **Accepted trade standards (Article 8).** Specific standards exist for:
    - ball joints (wear measured with a purpose-built gauge in thousandths of an inch or mm, per maker's specs; §§3360.1–3360.3);
    - automatic transmissions (§3361.1);
    - engine changes (§3362.1);
    - VIN, emissions and recall labels (§3364);
    - auto body, frame, sectioning and corrosion protection (§3365);
    - windshields (§3365.1);
    - A/C (§3366, plus equipment rule §3351.6);
    - airbags (§3367; previously deployed airbags banned);
    - towing referral fees (§3368).

    There is no brake-specific Article 8 standard. Elsewhere, repairs must be good and workmanlike per manufacturer procedures. — https://www.bar.ca.gov/ats
37. **Pending rules (not yet in force as of 2026-10-20).**
    - **Airbag Safety** (§3367 amendment): would ban components that fake a working airbag, require OEM-design restoration, and require OEM or authorized-source parts with receipts kept. 45-day comment ran Jul 10–Aug 24, 2026; 15-day comment Sep 2–17, 2026.
    - **Storage Fees**: storage fees must be reasonable and itemized on estimates and invoices, posted, and reported to BAR, with a BAR rate-lookup tool. Second modified text comment closed Aug 28, 2026.

    — https://www.bar.ca.gov/Regulatory-actions ; https://fenderbender.com/55359060

### 3.6 Prohibited acts and discipline (B&P §9884.7)

38. **Grounds for denial, suspension, revocation or probation** (unless the ARD shows a bona fide error). These cover acts by the ARD *or its technicians and employees*. — https://california.public.law/codes/business_and_professions_code_section_9884.7
    - (a)(1) Any untrue or misleading statement, written or oral, that the shop knew or should have known was untrue or misleading with reasonable care.
    - (a)(2) A work order signed without the requested repairs or the odometer reading.
    - (a)(3) No copy of a signed document given to the customer.
    - (a)(4) Fraud.
    - (a)(5) Gross negligence.
    - (a)(6) Material failure to comply with the Act or regulations.
    - (a)(7) Willful, material departure from accepted trade standards without the owner's consent.
    - (a)(8) False promises likely to induce authorization.
    - (a)(9) Unauthorized sublet.
    - (a)(10) A Penal Code §551 conviction (insurance-related kickbacks).

    "Fraud" includes intentional *or grossly negligent* misrepresentation of material fact, promises not made in good faith, and intentional concealment. — §9884.7(e). Discipline usually applies to the offending location only, but can reach every location for repeated, willful violations. — §9884.7(b)–(c). Remedial training is for documentation or minor violations only, never fraud. — §9884.7(d)
39. **False or misleading records and statements (regulations).**
    - No ARD may omit or insert anything on an estimate, invoice or work order that makes it false or misleading. — 16 CCR §3373, https://www.law.cornell.edu/regulations/california/16-CCR-3373
    - Misleadingness is judged from the overall impression on the targeted audience, *including the gullible or uninformed*. — §3372, https://www.law.cornell.edu/regulations/california/16-CCR-3372
    - Price ads are misleading if they are bait-and-switch, falsely imply what's included, create a false impression that more work is needed, or omit routinely needed extras. A disclosure can cure the last point, and must say the extra cost may be substantial if it is expected to exceed 25%. — §3372.1, https://www.law.cornell.edu/regulations/california/16-CCR-3372.1
40. **How BAR applies these to unnecessary repairs.** BAR's main tool is the undercover vehicle: a car with a known condition, or no defect, is brought in to see whether the shop recommends unneeded work. Typical charges are untrue or misleading statements (a)(1), fraud (a)(4)/(e), and false records (§3373). — https://jtadminlaw.com/2025/06/01/citations-and-accusations-brought-by-the-ca-bureau-of-automotive-repair-against-automotive-repair-dealers-and-smog-check-stations/ . Separately, the CLRA makes it unlawful to represent that a part, replacement or repair service is needed when it is not. — Civil Code §1770(a)(15), https://california.public.law/codes/civil_code_section_1770
41. **Citations and fines.** Each citation includes an order of abatement and may include a fine. The cap is **$5,000 per inspection or investigation** (B&P §125.9). Factors include:
    - seriousness and harm;
    - prior history and number of violations;
    - good or bad faith;
    - taking payment for work not done;
    - restitution and mitigation;
    - intent;
    - negligence;
    - involvement of owners or managers;
    - cooperation.

    Fines are due in 30 days. Unpaid fines block renewal. — 16 CCR §§3394.50–3394.52 (eff. Jul 1, 2023), https://www.law.cornell.edu/regulations/california/16-CCR-3394.51 ; B&P §125.9, https://california.public.law/codes/business_and_professions_code_section_125.9 . The remedial-training program that keeps a citation off BAR's website was extended to Jan 1, 2028 by SB 774 (2025). — https://bar.ca.gov/arsc/newsletters/newsletter/fall-2025/legislation-and-regulations-update . Cost recovery in Accusations runs under B&P §125.3: for example, $22,091 was ordered in a 2017 smog and repair revocation, ARD 241395. — https://bar.ca.gov/pdf/accusations/ard241395_2017_10_26_dec.pdf
42. **Notable enforcement.**
    - **Sears (1992):** BAR and DCA alleged unneeded repairs in 34 of roughly 37–38 undercover runs (reports differ), tied to sales quotas and commission pay, and sought to revoke all 72 CA centers. The settlement put the CA centers on 3-year probation, Sears paid about $46.6M in customer coupons nationwide, and Sears dropped its commission and quota scheme. — https://www.upi.com/Archives/1992/06/16/Sears-hits-back-at-California-probe/4031708667200/ ; https://www.tampabay.com/archive/1992/06/12/sears-accused-of-auto-repair-fraud/

    - **Firestone (from 2013):** a BAR Accusation covering more than 20 CA locations for fraud and misleading statements after undercover visits. The outcome is **[UNVERIFIED]**. — https://www.aftermarketnews.com/?p=71811
    - **2024:** Airport Collision RPR & Tow (Inglewood) was revoked for an unjustified tow charge and charges for work not authorized or performed. — https://bar.ca.gov/pdf/accusations/ard206811_2024_06_04_dec.pdf .     - **2025:** Hernandez Smog (South Gate) was revoked after two undercover runs. The inspector passed a vehicle that should have failed, once for a $200 payment. — https://www.bar.ca.gov/pdf/accusations/tc-302850_2025_12_24_dec.pdf

### 3.7 BAR "Write It Right" (updated January 2026)

Write It Right is BAR's compliance guide for ARDs. It is now a web page at https://www.bar.ca.gov/wir. A PDF link (/pdf/WriteItRight.pdf) is referenced but did not serve a PDF. Key points:

- Each line item uses plain-language "specific job" descriptions, with no acronyms.
- An estimate comes before any work, including diagnosis.
- Authorization is recorded by type: written gets signature and date; oral gets date, time, name and phone; electronic gets date, time, name and email or text number.
- Texts and emails get unique identifiers and are kept with the transaction.
- A revised work order with a new total is required before added work.
- Designated-person rules apply.
- After-hours drop-offs need an estimate and documented contact; a note is not enough.
- Towing fees go on a separate document.
- No "shop supplies." The hazardous-waste fee is itemized with an EPA ID.
- Parts are presumed new unless labeled. Crash parts are OEM unless labeled non-OEM aftermarket; "Opt-OEM" and "LKQ" are not acceptable.
- Teardown needs both a teardown estimate and an itemized post-teardown estimate.
- Third-party estimate rules apply.
- Replaced parts are returned if requested at authorization, and unreturned parts are noted.
- Records are kept 3 years, scanned if signed on paper.
- Invoices are required for warranty and no-charge work too.
- A lien sale is available only for authorized charges that meet the invoice rules.

The Jan 2026 changes added mobile-invoice location, clarified that teardown estimates need only the general area, and added the two paint and materials methods. — https://www.bar.ca.gov/wir ; https://fenderbender.com/55341723

**On multi-point or digital inspections specifically:** I found **no BAR rule or guidance that specifically governs courtesy multi-point or digital vehicle inspections** **[UNVERIFIED that none exists]**. BAR treats them through the general rules:

- An inspection performed for a charge is a diagnosis or repair that requires an estimate and authorization (§§9884.9, 3353.1).
- Any finding or recommendation is a "statement" subject to §9884.7(a)(1) and (a)(4) and CLRA §1770(a)(15).
- Any recommendation that is converted into a line item must meet estimate, authorization and invoice rules.
- Trigger words carry fixed meanings. For example, "service," "inspection" or "performance check" on A/C means the full §3366 16-point check.
- Ball-joint replacement must be backed by measured wear compared with the spec, recorded on the invoice (§3360.2).

### 3.8 Smog Check (H&S Code §44000 et seq.)

43. **Who needs it.** Biennially at registration renewal; at change of ownership (seller's duty; a pass within 90 days for private sales); and when first registering in CA. Covered: gasoline and hybrid vehicles 1976 and newer, diesel 1998 and newer (≤14,000 lb GVWR). Exempt: gasoline and hybrid vehicles of the most recent 8 model years for renewal (a smog abatement fee applies instead) and 4 model years for transfer (a transfer fee applies), 1975 and older, diesel 1997 and older or over 14,000 lb, EVs and motorcycles. Stations set their own inspection prices. The certificate fee is $8.25. Stations must give an estimate and get authorization before testing. — https://www.bar.ca.gov/smog-check
44. **Station types.** Test-only, test-and-repair, repair-only, and STAR (a voluntary higher-performance certification, judged partly by Follow-up Pass Rate). Vehicles directed to STAR must be initially tested and retested at STAR stations. — https://www.bar.ca.gov/star ; 16 CCR §3340.41(e), https://www.law.cornell.edu/regulations/california/16-CCR-3340.41 . STAR rules were updated Jul 1, 2022. — https://www.bar.ca.gov/Regulatory-actions
45. **OIS / OBD test.** OBD-II vehicles fail for any of the following:
    - the MIL does not light at key-on with the engine off, or is on or commanded on while running;
    - a stored DTC;
    - no communication with the test equipment;
    - a software configuration that does not match the manufacturer's or a CARB-exempted configuration;
    - incomplete readiness monitors beyond the limits.

    — 16 CCR §3340.42.2, https://www.law.cornell.edu/regulations/california/16-CCR-3340.42.2 . **Readiness amendment eff. Oct 1, 2025:** gasoline 1996+ and diesel 1998+ cannot pass OBD with readiness "not complete." The details of the new limits are in the amended text. The LII copy I read appeared to predate the amendment. — https://bar.ca.gov/arsc/newsletters/newsletter/fall-2025/legislation-and-regulations-update
46. **Smog repairs must follow manufacturer or nationally recognized procedures.** Inspectors use biometric login (palm-vein scanners; device update eff. Jul 1, 2026). The Vehicle Inspection Report must be attached to the invoice. — 16 CCR §3340.41(a),(d),(f); https://www.bar.ca.gov/Regulatory-actions
47. **What a failure does and doesn't imply.** A failure identifies an emissions *condition* or OBD fault. It does **not** by itself prove that any particular part has failed. Repairs must be diagnosed under §3340.41(d), estimated and authorized like any other repair, and may be covered by emissions warranty. A CARB EO is needed for aftermarket emissions-related parts. The Smog Check Referee handles disputes, unusual vehicles and hard-to-find parts. — https://www.bar.ca.gov/smog-check ; https://ww2.arb.ca.gov/our-work/programs/aftermarket-performance-and-add-parts
48. **Consumer Assistance Program (CAP)** (amounts eff. Jan 1, 2025).
    - **Repair assistance:** for household income ≤225% of the federal poverty level, after a failed biennial test. BAR pays 80% with a ≥20% owner copay, up to $500, rising to $1,100 (MY 1976–1995) or $1,450 (MY 1996+) if BAR agrees repairs are necessary and cost-effective. Work must be done at a contracted STAR test-and-repair station. Diagnosis is paid at 1.5× the posted hourly rate.
    - **Retirement:** $2,000 (income-eligible) or $1,350 (others).

    — 16 CCR §3394.3, https://www.law.cornell.edu/regulations/california/16-CCR-3394.3 ; §3394.4, https://www.law.cornell.edu/regulations/california/16-CCR-3394.4

### 3.9 Brake and Lamp → Vehicle Safety Systems Inspection (VSSI)

49. **Status.** Lamp and brake station and adjuster licensing became **inoperative Sept 27, 2024**. VSSI (16 CCR Art. 2.5, §§3311.1–3314.2, adopted Mar 27, 2024) replaced it. Inspections are done only by licensed technicians at licensed stations, using the **VSSI Manual (Sept 2023)**, which is incorporated by reference. SB 861 (2025) cleaned up statutory cross-references. — https://www.law.cornell.edu/regulations/california/16-CCR-3311.1 ; https://bar.ca.gov/arsc/newsletters/newsletter/fall-2025/legislation-and-regulations-update
50. **Who needs a VSSI.** Required for DMV registration of revived salvage and junk vehicles, specially constructed vehicles, motorcycles (in that registration context), trailers of 3,000 lb GVWR or more, and off-highway vehicles converted to on-highway use. Brake and lamp **"fix-it" tickets no longer require a certificate**: the defect is fixed and signed off by a licensed station or by law enforcement. — https://www.bar.ca.gov/safety-systems
51. **Selected VSSI Manual pass/fail thresholds.** These are *Do-Not-Certify* minimums, not replacement recommendations. — https://www.bar.ca.gov/pdf/manuals/VSSI.pdf
    - **Brake linings:** at or above the manufacturer's service limit. If no limit is available, at least 1/32 in. for bonded linings or 1/64 in. above rivet heads.
    - **Rotors:** not below minimum thickness. Drums: not above maximum diameter. Thickness variation, runout, cracks and corrosion are also checked. Gauges must read to 0.001 in. for rotors and 0.005 in. for drums.
    - **Tires:** at least 2/32 in. for passenger and light truck. Heavy-duty: 4/32 in. steer, 2/32 in. other.
    - **Other checks:** booster function, warning lights, headlight aim, airbags (no evidence of prior deployment), structural rust and frame damage, and an OBD scan for MY2000+.
    - **An open NHTSA recall fails the inspection.**
    - The station must give a written estimate for the inspection under §9884.9 and §3353.

### 3.10 Auto body / collision and CDI overlap

52. An auto body shop is an ARD location doing body or crash repair. — B&P §9889.51, https://california.public.law/codes/business_and_professions_code_section_9889.51 . Estimates must be itemized and crash parts identified (see 3.2 #12, 3.4 #30–31). §3365 requires OEM or accepted industry repair specs, sectioning standards and corrosion protection. — https://www.bar.ca.gov/ats . BAR offers a **free collision-repair inspection** for repairs done in CA after the owner bought the vehicle. It checks that invoiced repairs were actually done. — https://www.bar.ca.gov/collision-inspection
53. **Insurer overlap.** An insurer may not require a particular shop. It may recommend one only on request or after written notice of the right to choose, and it must restore the vehicle to pre-loss condition at no extra cost if the claimant uses the recommended shop. If a policy names a preferred shop, the insurer may not cut reasonable costs at another shop by comparing them with preferred-shop pricing. — Ins. Code §758.5. ARD estimates built on insurer estimates must follow §3353(b)(1) and (d). The insurer can't be the customer's designee. — B&P §§9880.1(f), 9884.9(d)

### 3.11 CARB (separate from BAR)

54. **Aftermarket, add-on and modified emissions parts** need a CARB **Executive Order** to be legal on emission-controlled vehicles. EO numbers are checked by Smog Check stations, Referees and CARB through a lookup tool. — https://ww2.arb.ca.gov/our-work/programs/aftermarket-performance-and-add-parts . The anti-tampering statute is Vehicle Code §27156 **[UNVERIFIED text]**.
55. **Catalytic converters.** In California, a replacement aftermarket converter must be CARB-approved (EO) for the specific vehicle, or be OEM. Used or recycled converters may not be sold or installed. **[UNVERIFIED in this session]**: the CARB page returned 404. This is widely reported CARB policy, but confirm it on arb.ca.gov before relying on it.
56. **Clean Truck Check (HD I/M)** is a CARB program, not BAR. It applies to heavy-duty non-gasoline vehicles operating in CA, including out-of-state vehicles, with an annual compliance fee and periodic testing by CARB-credentialed testers. CARB's notice (Mar 10, 2026) says the requirements are in effect. — https://ww2.arb.ca.gov/our-work/programs/CTC . The >14,000 lb GVWR threshold, the semiannual testing frequency and the fee amount are **[UNVERIFIED]**.

### 3.12 Other California laws affecting repair

57. **SB 478 all-in pricing (CLRA §1770(a)(29), operative Jul 1, 2024).** It is unlawful to advertise or display a price that leaves out mandatory fees. Only government-imposed taxes and fees, and actual shipping, may be left out. — https://california.public.law/codes/civil_code_section_1770 . *Applied to shops (my analysis):* an advertised "$49.99 oil change" plus a mandatory shop-imposed "environmental" or "disposal" fee is a drip-pricing risk. 16 CCR §3356(g) already bans generic shop-supply charges on invoices outright.
58. **CLRA §1770(a)(15)–(16):** misrepresenting that a repair is needed, or that work was supplied as promised, gives a private right of action. — same URL.
59. **"Certified" used vehicles.** The governing statute is **Vehicle Code §11713.18**. (§11950 concerns buy-here-pay-here market-value labels and is not the certification statute.) A dealer may not call a used car "certified" if it has odometer issues, a lemon or salvage brand, substantial impact, fire or flood damage, frame damage, or is sold as-is or with a warranty disclaimer. The buyer must get a **completed inspection report listing every component inspected** before sale. — https://california.public.law/codes/vehicle_code_section_11713.18 ; https://california.public.law/codes/vehicle_code_section_11950
60. **Recording phone authorizations.** California requires **all-party consent** to record confidential communications. Recording penalties are up to $2,500 per violation (more for repeat offenses); cell and cordless calls are covered by §632.7. There is a civil claim of $5,000 per violation or 3× damages. Unlawfully obtained recordings are generally inadmissible. — Penal Code §632, https://california.public.law/codes/penal_code_section_632 ; §632.7, https://california.public.law/codes/penal_code_section_632.7 ; §637.2, https://california.public.law/codes/penal_code_section_637.2 . A shop recording approval calls needs an up-front disclosure. Written notes under §3353.1(c) are required either way.
61. **CCPA/CPRA (brief).** This applies to shops meeting a threshold, e.g., annual gross revenue above **$26,625,000** (CPI-adjusted, eff. Jan 1, 2025), among other tests. DVI photos, VINs, telematics and contact data are personal information. New CCPA regulations (risk assessments, cybersecurity audits, ADMT) were finalized in Sept 2025. Some provisions are effective Jan 1, 2026; the phase-in dates are **[UNVERIFIED]**. — https://cppa.ca.gov/regulations/cpi_adjustment.html ; https://cppa.ca.gov/regulations/
62. **Garage and mechanic's lien (Civil Code §3068 et seq.).** A possessory lien arises for compensation *legally owed* for repairs and storage. It attaches when the written statement of charges is presented, or 15 days after completion, whichever is earlier. With owner consent, the shop must apply for a lien sale or sue within 30 days or the lien is extinguished. The lien is also extinguished if the shop refuses the legal owner an inspection or won't produce the work order or invoice and authorization within 10 days of demand. Against legal owners and lessors, the lien is capped at **$1,500** for work and **$1,025** for storage ($1,250 if a lien-sale application is filed within 30 days). Storage after 60 days requires Vehicle Code notices. — https://california.public.law/codes/civil_code_section_3068 . BAR says a lien sale is permitted only for authorized charges with compliant invoices. — https://www.bar.ca.gov/wir . **[UNVERIFIED]**: whether the $1,500 cap binds only against a separate legal owner or lessor, versus the registered owner who authorized the work. Read the official text. AB 987 (2025) adds holiday storage overcharges and certain emergency tows to the list of presumptively unreasonable fees (Veh. Code §§10652.5, 22524.5). — fall-2025 BAR newsletter URL above.

### 3.13 Consumer complaints

63. **Process.** Try to resolve it with the shop first. Then file with BAR online (also by mail, phone or in person per a 2020 BAR article; anonymous filing is possible). A representative contacts the consumer within 7–10 business days, gathers estimates, invoices and photos, may inspect the vehicle, and mediates.
    - **Possible outcomes:** a refund, a bill adjustment, or no-charge re-repair. BAR negotiates about $5M a year in such relief.
    - **Not covered:** repairs outside CA, rental cars, boats, heavy-duty commercial vehicles and trailers.
    - **Complaints vs. enforcement:** a complaint is not a finding of violation. Violations may lead to education, an office conference, a citation, or a formal investigation and Accusation.
    - **Use in court:** complaint reports go in the shop's master file and **can be subpoenaed for small claims or civil court**.

    — https://www.bar.ca.gov/complaint ; https://www.bar.ca.gov/arsc/newsletters/newsletter/spring-2020/complaint-mediation-process
64. **Limits.** I found no BAR authority to *order* restitution through mediation. Restitution is a mitigating factor in citations (§3394.51) and can be a probation term in Accusations. Binding money recovery comes from small claims or civil court (CLRA, UCL) **[UNVERIFIED as an official BAR statement]**. No filing deadline is published on the complaint page.

### 4.1 Checks for every California estimate, work order and invoice (both audiences)

| # | Check | Pass condition | Red flag | Cite |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| A1 | Shop identity | ARD number plus business name and address that match BAR records (use BAR's license lookup) | No ARD number, mismatched DBA, or an unregistered referral or mobile operator | §3356(b); §9884.6; §3371.2 |
| A2 | Estimate before work | A dated estimate exists *before* the first labor time, including diagnosis or inspection if charged | Diagnosis or inspection charge with no prior estimate; estimate dated after work | §9884.9(a); §3353.1(a) |
| A3 | Authorization record | Matches its type: signature and date; or date, time, name and phone; or date, time, name and email or text number | "Customer approved" with no time or name; approvals by an insurer or the shop itself | §3353.1; §9880.1(f) |
| A4 | Add-on work | A revised estimate with added items and a **new total**, authorized *before* the work; invoice repeats the oral or electronic approval details or carries the signed acknowledgment | Invoice total exceeds the authorized total with no revision record | §3354; §9884.9(a); §3356(e) |
| A5 | Plain-language "specific job" | Each line is understandable to a layperson | Unexplained acronyms ("R&R PCV, MAF svc, BG induction") | §3352(b); WIR |
| A6 | Parts condition | Every part labeled where it is not new; crash parts labeled OEM or non-OEM aftermarket | "Opt-OEM," "LKQ," "quality replacement," unlabeled reman | §§9884.8, 3353(a)-(b), 3356(c)(2) |
| A7 | Banned or generic fees | None | "Shop supplies," "misc. parts," "EPA/environmental fee" without an EPA ID line, "smog data fee" | §3356(f)-(g); WIR |

| A14 | Odometer and requested repairs on the signed work order | Present | Missing (a stand-alone disciplinary ground) | §9884.7(a)(2) |

### 4.2 Checks for inspection findings and recommendations (MPI/DVI)

- **Each finding is a "statement."** A recommendation the tech knew or should have known was false or misleading can be a §9884.7(a)(1) violation; with intent or gross negligence it can be fraud (§9884.7(a)(4),(e)); and it is a CLRA §1770(a)(15) claim. Audit question: *what measured evidence supports this finding?*
- **Require measurements, not adjectives.** Ask for brake pad and shoe mm or 32nds, rotor thickness against the cast minimum, drum diameter against the maximum, tire tread in 32nds at several points, battery test printout (CCA or state of health), fluid test-strip or meter readings, and scan-tool DTC and freeze-frame data. California-specific anchor: **ball-joint replacement must show measured wear and the maker's maximum allowable wear on the invoice** (§3360.2). An invoice without both is a direct regulatory defect.
- **Use VSSI minimums as a floor reference, not a replacement trigger.** Pads above the manufacturer service limit (or 1/32 in. bonded if no spec), rotors above minimum thickness and tires above 2/32 in. would *pass* California's state safety inspection. A "red / fail / unsafe" label on components above those limits needs justification, e.g., a wear rate or a manufacturer's earlier replace spec. A "yellow / monitor" label is defensible. Calling it *required* or *unsafe* invites (a)(1) and (a)(8) scrutiny. — VSSI Manual
- **A/C "inspection" or "performance check" on a DVI** triggers the §3366 16-point standard, and the invoice must record pressures and vent temperature. Check that these appear if the term was used or billed.
- **Transmission recommendations.** Was the pre-removal inspection and code retrieval done (§3361.1)? Is a "rebuilt" or "reman" label backed by the defined work? Was a cheaper external fix offered?
- **Smog-driven recommendations.** A smog fail identifies a condition, not a part. Ask for the diagnostic path under §3340.41(d), the VIR, and whether emissions warranty or CAP applies. Readiness monitors simply being incomplete (Oct 2025 rule) may need a drive cycle, not parts.
- **Flush and service "packages."** Compare against the owner's-manual schedule (BAR's consumer page tells owners to follow the manual). Under §3372.1, an ad or menu that creates a false impression that services are needed is misleading.
- **Open recalls.** VSSI fails a vehicle for an open NHTSA recall, and recall repairs are free at dealers. A DVI recommending paid work on a recalled component without flagging the recall is a red flag. — https://www.bar.ca.gov/auto-repairs
- **Photos and video.** No California rule requires DVI photos **[UNVERIFIED that none exists]**. But photos sent by text or email for approval become supplemental authorization records and must be kept 3 years with the unique ID (§3353.1(e); §3358).

### 4.3 Consumer-specific

- **Before authorizing:** ask for the written estimate and to have replaced parts returned (you must ask now, at the work-order stage). Ask for measurements and photos for each red item. Refuse vague line items.
- **Second opinion:** you can decline and take the car. The shop must still reassemble within the stated max time if a teardown was done.
- **Paying under protest / lien:** a shop can hold the car only for charges legally owed. Unauthorized amounts above the authorized estimate are not "legally owed" under the Act, which is my reading **[analysis]**. Request the work order, authorizations and invoice in writing.
- **Evidence to gather for a complaint:** estimate(s), revised estimates, texts, emails and app approvals with timestamps, invoice, returned parts, photos, the DVI report, an independent shop's measurements, and a smog VIR or VSSI report if relevant.
- **Escalation path:** shop management → BAR complaint (mediation; the report can be subpoenaed) → small claims or a CLRA demand. For collision work, BAR's free collision inspection. For insurer issues, CDI.
- **Don't secretly record calls** with the shop (Penal Code §632). Take notes instead.

### 4.4 Shop-owner-specific (QA program)

- **Self-audit sampling:** pull ROs monthly and test A1–A15. Cross-check DVI "red" items against measured values and declined-work rates per technician and advisor. Outlier sell-through rates are the Sears-pattern risk.
- **Compensation risk:** Sears (1992) tied unneeded repairs to quotas and commissions. Review pay plans so findings require measured evidence. Make DVI templates force numeric entries for brakes, tires, ball joints, batteries and A/C.
- **Authorization tooling:** confirm the DVI or text-approval platform captures date, time, approver name and phone or email, and links them to the RO number. Confirm it produces a revised estimate showing a **new total** before approval, and exports records for 3 years.
- **Fee hygiene:** remove "shop supplies" and "misc." lines. Itemize any hazardous-waste fee with the EPA ID. Make advertised prices all-in (SB 478).
- **Signage and ads:** ARD sign posted. Mobile techs carry sign copies. Online listings show the BAR name, ARD number and phone (eff. Oct 2025).
- **Teardown and insurer jobs:** adopt the July 2025 teardown, third-party estimate and towing-document templates.
- **Watch list:** the airbag rule (§3367) and storage-fee rules (pending); biometric and change-of-address rules (Jul 1, 2026); BAR sunset review (Jan 1, 2028); remedial-training sunset (Jan 1, 2028).
- **If cited:** response deadlines are short; one practitioner says often 15 days. A citation's fine cap is $5,000 per investigation. Restitution and cooperation reduce penalties (§3394.51).

# 06 — Commercial, Fleet, and Specialty Vehicle Inspection Requirements (US)

## 1) Scope & as-of date

- **As of:** 2026-10-20. Regulations change. Re-check the primary sources before relying on any number here.
- **Covers:** federal FMCSA inspection, maintenance, and recordkeeping rules (49 CFR Part 396 and its minimum periodic inspection standards); CVSA roadside inspection levels and Out-of-Service (OOS) criteria (public information only); ATA TMC Recommended Practices (public descriptions only); selected state commercial programs (California BIT, Texas commercial inspection, school buses, and the national school bus specifications); rideshare/TNC, taxi, and for-hire inspections; fire apparatus and ambulance standards (brief); CARB Clean Truck Check (heavy-duty emissions I/M); and how to audit commercial inspection paperwork.
- **Why it matters to the skill:** a commercial or fleet vehicle report is checked against a *regulatory floor*: specific numbers, required items, a qualified inspector, and retention periods. A passenger-car multi-point inspection usually has none of these. The skill should (a) work out whether the vehicle is a regulated CMV or a regulated for-hire vehicle, (b) apply the matching checklist and limits, and (c) flag both directions of error. One is a "fail" or "replace" call on a component that meets the federal minimum, presented as if regulation required it. The other is a "pass" on a component that does not meet the minimum.
- **Out of scope:** paid standards text (CVSA OOS Criteria handbook, TMC RP Manual, NFPA 1910/1900, CAAS GVS). Only their public descriptions are summarized here.
- **Research limitation:** the web search budget ran out during research. Items marked **[UNVERIFIED]** could not be confirmed from a primary source in this session.

### Which vehicles are federally covered (CMV definition, 49 CFR 390.5 / 390.5T)
In interstate commerce, a CMV is a self-propelled or towed vehicle that meets **any** of these:
1. GVWR, GCWR, or actual gross weight of **10,001 lb or more**, whichever is greater.
2. Designed or used to carry **more than 8 passengers** (including the driver) for compensation.
3. Designed or used to carry **more than 15 passengers** (including the driver) without compensation.
4. Carrying **placardable hazmat**.

Source: https://www.law.cornell.edu/cfr/text/49/390.5. The Cornell page notes that §390.5 is suspended and a temporary §390.5T is in effect. The working definitions are believed to be the same, but confirm before citing the section number. **[UNVERIFIED: exact current section designation]**

Part 396 exemptions include private nonbusiness passenger carriers (for some provisions) and, for DVIRs, driveaway-towaway operations and carriers operating only one CMV (https://www.law.cornell.edu/cfr/text/49/396.11). Intrastate-only vehicles fall under state law. Most states adopt the FMCSRs with modifications, and some apply them to intrastate vehicles of 26,001 lb or more instead of 10,001 lb. Texas is one example (see §3.6).

## 3) Rules & numeric criteria

### 3.1 49 CFR 396.3 — Systematic inspection, repair, and maintenance
- Every motor carrier and intermodal equipment provider (IEP) must systematically inspect, repair, and maintain all vehicles under its control, or have someone do it for them. Parts must be in safe and proper operating condition at all times. This covers Part 393 equipment plus frames, suspension, axles, wheels, rims, and steering.
- **Buses:** pushout windows, emergency doors, and emergency door marking lights must be inspected at least **every 90 days**.
- **Required records for each vehicle the carrier has controlled for 30 consecutive days:**
  - identification: company number if any, make, serial number, year, tire size, and owner name if not owned by the carrier;
  - a schedule showing the type and due date of inspections and maintenance;
  - a record of the dates and nature of inspections, repairs, and maintenance;
  - for buses, records of the pushout window, emergency door, and marking-light tests.
- **Retention:** **1 year** where the vehicle is housed or maintained, and **6 months** after the vehicle leaves the carrier's control.
- Source: https://www.law.cornell.edu/cfr/text/49/396.3

### 3.2 49 CFR 396.11 / 396.13 — Driver vehicle inspection reports (DVIR) and pre-trip
- **Post-trip DVIR:** the driver prepares a written report at the end of each day's work on each vehicle operated.
- **Required coverage (11 items):** service brakes including trailer brake connections, parking brake, steering mechanism, lighting devices and reflectors, tires, horn, windshield wipers, rear vision mirrors, coupling devices, wheels and rims, and emergency equipment.
- **No-defect reports are not required:**
  - Property carriers: since **Dec. 18, 2014** (https://downloads.regulations.gov/FMCSA-2012-0336-0046/content.htm).
  - Passenger carriers: since **Sept. 17, 2020** (85 FR 50787, Aug. 18, 2020: https://www.govinfo.gov/content/pkg/FR-2020-08-18/pdf/2020-15667.pdf).
  - Current text: a driver need not prepare a report if no defect was found or reported (https://www.law.cornell.edu/cfr/text/49/396.11).
- **Corrective action:** before the vehicle is driven again, the carrier must repair any listed defect likely to affect safety. It must then **certify on the DVIR** that the defect was repaired or that repair was unnecessary.
- **Retention:** the DVIR, the repair certification, and the next driver's review certification are kept **3 months** from the date of the report.
- **Electronic DVIRs:** allowed under §390.32.
- **Pre-trip (§396.13):** the driver must be satisfied the vehicle is in safe operating condition. Where a prior DVIR listed defects, the driver must review it and sign to acknowledge that required repairs were certified (https://www.law.cornell.edu/cfr/text/49/396.13).
- **Roadside inspection reports (§396.9):** the carrier must certify corrections and return the report within 15 days. **[UNVERIFIED in this session; standard FMCSR figure, re-check §396.9(d)]**

### 3.3 49 CFR 396.17 / 396.19 / 396.21 / 396.23 — Annual (periodic) inspection
- **Frequency:** every CMV, and each unit of a combination (tractor, semitrailer, full trailer, converter dolly), must have passed an inspection of all Appendix A items at least once in the **preceding 12 months** (https://www.law.cornell.edu/cfr/text/49/396.17).
- **Proof on the vehicle:** either the inspection report or a decal/sticker. The decal must show the date, the name and address of whoever keeps the report, vehicle ID if not marked, and a certification that the vehicle passed.
- **Who may perform it:** the carrier itself, or a commercial garage, fleet leasing company, truck stop, or similar business acting as the carrier's agent. Either way the inspector must meet §396.19. The carrier remains responsible.
- **State programs:** passing a state periodic program that meets Appendix A counts for 12 months from the end of the month inspected. A mandatory state program that FMCSA finds as effective as §396.17 replaces it (§396.23).
- **Inspector qualifications (§396.19):**
  - The inspector must understand the Part 393 and Appendix A criteria and be able to identify defective components.
  - The inspector must know and have mastered the methods, tools, and equipment.
  - Capability is shown by either (1) a federal or state training program, or a state or Canadian province certificate, or (2) **at least 1 year** of combined training and/or experience. Qualifying experience includes manufacturer or commercial training; mechanic or inspector work for a carrier, garage, or leasing company; or government CMV inspector work.
  - **Evidence of qualification** must be kept for the whole period the person inspects **plus 1 year**. Not required for state periodic program inspections.
  - Source: https://www.law.cornell.edu/cfr/text/49/396.19
- **Annual inspection report contents (§396.21):**
  1. the inspector's identity;
  2. the carrier or IEP;
  3. the date;
  4. vehicle identification;
  5. the components inspected and results, including any that fail Appendix A;
  6. a certification that the inspection is accurate and complete.
  - **Retention:** **14 months** where the vehicle is housed or maintained. It must be produced on official request. If someone else did the inspection, the carrier must obtain a copy.
  - Source: https://www.law.cornell.edu/cfr/text/49/396.21
- **Common errors to watch for:** a form that lacks a component-by-component result, or a decal with no traceable report behind it, does not meet §396.21.

### 3.4 49 CFR 396.25 — Brake inspector qualifications
- Applies to any employee responsible for brake inspection, maintenance, service, or repair on carrier-controlled CMVs.
- **Qualification routes:**
  1. A state, provincial, federal, or union apprenticeship; a government-approved training program; or a state or provincial certificate. For brake *inspections*, passing the **CDL air brake** knowledge and skills tests also qualifies.
  2. **At least 1 year** of brake-related training or experience. Qualifying sources include manufacturer or commercial brake training, the carrier's own maintenance program, or a garage or leasing company.
- **ASE certification is not named** as a route.
- **Evidence:** kept at the principal place of business or where the inspector works, for the period of employment as a brake inspector **plus 1 year**. Not required for air brake inspections by people who passed the CDL air brake tests.
- Source: https://www.law.cornell.edu/cfr/text/49/396.25

### 3.5 Minimum Periodic Inspection Standards — formerly "Appendix G to Subchapter B", now **Appendix A to Part 396**
**Naming note:** this appendix was **redesignated** in 2021–2022 (86 FR 57068/57077, Oct. 14, 2021; 86 FR 62111; 87 FR 59037). Reports and vendor material that still cite "Appendix G" mean the same standard.

The values below are **reject (fail) thresholds** for the annual inspection, paraphrased. Source for all: https://www.law.cornell.edu/cfr/text/49/appendix-A_to_part_396

**1. Brakes**
- **Pushrod stroke at or beyond the readjustment limit is a defect.** Measure with the engine off, reservoir at 80–90 psi, and brakes fully applied. Examples of readjustment limits:

| Chamber | Standard stroke | Long stroke |
|---|---|---|
| Clamp Type 6 | 1-1/4 in | — |
| Clamp Type 30 | 2 in | 2-1/2 in |
| Bendix DD-3 Type 30 | 2-1/4 in | — |
| Rotochamber Type 50 | 3 in | — |

  Other chamber types have their own limits. Unlisted actuators fail above 80% of rated stroke or the marked limit.
- **Lining/pad thickness (reject if less than):**

| Axle | Brake type | Reject threshold |
|---|---|---|
| Steering | Air drum | 1/4 in at shoe center |
| Steering | Air disc | 1/8 in |
| Steering | Hydraulic disc or electric | 1/16 in or less |
| Non-steering | Air drum | 1/4 in at shoe center |
| Non-steering | Air disc | 1/8 in |
| Non-steering | Hydraulic or electric drum | 1/16 in or less |

  These are periodic-inspection limits. The roadside/operational limits in **§393.47** are lower for steering axles: 3/16 in for air drum (continuous-strip lining) and 1/8 in for air disc (https://jjkellercompliancenetwork.com/regsense/details/r49CFR393.47). Do not confuse the two.
- **Wedge brakes:** scribe-mark movement over 1/16 in is a defect.
- **Low-air warning:** must activate at 55 psi or below, or at half the governor cut-out pressure, whichever is less.
- **Hydraulic master cylinder:** less than 1/4 full is a defect.
- **Other brake defects:**
  - parking brake that will not apply;
  - cracked drums or rotors;
  - hose damage that exposes the outer reinforcement ply;
  - missing or broken shoes, linings, pads, springs, anchor pins, spiders, cam rollers, pushrods, or chamber mounting bolts;
  - audible air leaks at the chambers;
  - insufficient vacuum reserve;
  - ABS malfunction lamp and automatic adjuster requirements also apply.

**2. Coupling (reject if more than)**
- Fifth wheel: horizontal movement between upper and lower halves over 1/2 in; pivot bracket pin movement over 3/8 in; slider movement over 3/8 in.
- Pintle hook horn section, or drawbar/towbar eye, worn more than 20%.
- Safety chains: any measurable reduction in link cross-section.
- Saddle-mount: horizontal movement between halves over 1/4 in.
- Drawbar tongue: slider or subframe movement over 1/4 in.

**3. Exhaust**
- Leaks forward of or directly below the driver/sleeper compartment.
- Bus exhaust outlets:
  - gasoline: more than 6 in forward of the rearmost part of the bus is a defect;
  - other fuels: more than 15 in forward of the rearmost part, or forward of any openable door or window (emergency exits excepted), is a defect.

**4. Fuel:** visible leak, missing filler cap, or tank not securely attached.

**5. Lighting:** every Part 393-required lamp and reflector must work.

**6. Safe loading:** spare tire or cargo that could fall; missing front-end structure where required; defective intermodal container securement.

**7. Steering**
- **Steering-wheel lash (free play), reject if more than:**

| Wheel diameter | Manual steering | Power steering |
|---|---|---|
| 16 in | 2 in | 4-1/2 in |
| 18 in | 2-1/4 in | 4-3/4 in |
| 20 in | 2-1/2 in | 5-1/4 in |
| 22 in | 2-3/4 in | 5-3/4 in |

  The appendix lists further diameters. Use the full table.
- **Ball and socket joints:** more than 1/4 in non-rotational movement at any linkage point.
- Other steering defects in the appendix include loose column, gearbox mounting, and U-joint wear. **[UNVERIFIED in detail this session]**

**8. Suspension:** broken or missing leaves; broken main leaf; broken coil spring; missing rubber spring; deflated air suspension; axle out of normal position (e.g., cracked or broken U-bolt or spring hanger).

**9. Frame:** cracked, broken, loose, or sagging members; loose or missing fasteners; body or frame contacting a tire or wheel.

**10. Tires**
- **Steering-axle tires on power units: reject below 4/32 in** at any point in a major groove.
- **All other tires: reject below 2/32 in.**
- Other tire defects:
  - exposed ply or belt;
  - tread or sidewall separation;
  - cuts to the ply;
  - overloading;
  - flat or audible leak;
  - "Not for highway use" marking;
  - bias and radial tires mixed on one axle;
  - regrooved tires on steer axles (except urban/suburban bus exceptions);
  - boot or blowout patch;
  - recaps or retreads on bus front wheels.

**11. Wheels and rims:** cracks or breaks; elongated stud holes; loose or missing fasteners; cracked welds; welded repairs on aluminum steer wheels.

**12. Windshield glazing**
- Areas excluded from evaluation: 2 in at the top, 1 in at the sides, and the area below the top of the steering wheel.
- Cracks are allowed only if no wider than 1/4 in and not intersected by another crack.
- Damaged areas (e.g., chips) are allowed only if no larger than 3/4 in in diameter and at least 3 in from another damaged area.

**13. Wipers:** inoperative or ineffective wipers on a power unit.

**14. Motorcoach seats:** any passenger seat not securely fastened.

**15. Rear impact guard**
- Trailers and semitrailers of 10,001 lb GVWR or more, built on or after Jan. 26, 1998: the guard must be within 4 in of each side, no more than 22 in above the ground, no more than 12 in forward of the rear, with at least 4 in vertical section.
- Older CMVs (built after 1952): no more than 30 in above the ground, within 18 in of each side, no more than 24 in forward of the rear.

**Audit relevance:** a shop recommending replacement "per DOT" of steer tires at 5/32 in or drive tires at 4/32 in, or air-drum linings at 3/8 in, is recommending maintenance *above* the federal floor. That can be good preventive practice (fleet pull points, TMC guidance), but it is **not legally required**. The report must not call the vehicle "failed" or "out of service" on that basis.

### 3.6 CVSA North American Standard inspection levels and OOS criteria (public info only)
**Levels** (sources: https://cvsa.org/wp-content/uploads/Roadside-Inspection-Operational-Policies.pdf ; https://cvsa.org/inspections/level-viii-definition-purpose ; https://cvsa.org/inspections/all-inspection-levels/)

| Level | Name | What it covers |
|---|---|---|
| I | North American Standard Inspection | Full driver credentials/HOS plus full vehicle, including under-vehicle. |
| II | Walk-Around | Driver plus vehicle items that can be checked without getting under it. *(Level II from a secondary source.)* |
| III | Driver-Only | Credentials, HOS, etc. No mechanical violations. |
| IV | Special | One-time examination of a particular item, usually to support a study or check a suspected trend. |
| V | Vehicle-Only | Level I vehicle items with no driver present. Can be done at any location, e.g., at a terminal. |
| VI | Enhanced NAS inspection | For transuranic and highway-route-controlled radioactive shipments. |
| VII | Jurisdictional Mandated | State-mandated programs that fit no other level: school buses, limousines, taxis, shared-ride, hotel shuttles, intrastate operations. **No CVSA decal**, though a jurisdiction decal may be applied. |
| VIII | Electronic | Done wirelessly while the vehicle is moving, with no officer interaction. Defined 2017. Still in operational test. |

**CVSA decal** (https://cvsa.org/inspections/about-inspection-decals/)
- Only Level I or Level V inspections with **no critical violations** earn one. Level VI has a special decal.
- Valid for up to **3 consecutive months**.
- Reflects vehicle condition only, not the driver.
- A CVSA decal is **not** the §396.17 annual inspection.

**OOS criteria**
- The NAS OOS Criteria are a **paid CVSA publication**: handbook, restricted PDF, app (https://cvsa.org/news/2024-oosc-handbook/ ; https://cvsa.org/news/2018-oosc-app/).
- Updated annually, effective **April 1**. The 2026 edition had **17 changes**, including licensing/CDL restrictions, alcohol thresholds, ELD tampering, brakes, cargo securement, and placarding (https://jjkeller.com/news/article/17-new-updates-to-the-OOS-Criteria-now-in-effect_id-baec21be-b729-4594-d515-e5ab91086683 ; https://www.thetrucker.com/trucking-news/equipment-tech/ready-or-not-cvsas-2026-out-of-service-criteria-is-now-in-effect).
- OOS conditions are **imminent-hazard** thresholds. They are deliberately more lenient than Appendix A. An example is the "20% of service brakes defective" rule. **[UNVERIFIED — commonly cited; exact wording is in the paid handbook]**
- **For auditing:** a vehicle can pass OOS roadside and still fail the annual inspection standard, and both still sit below a fleet's preventive-maintenance pull points. Reports should name **which standard** they apply.

### 3.8 State commercial programs

**California — BIT (Basic Inspection of Terminals), Vehicle Code §34501.12 and §34505.5**
- **Covered vehicles** (§34500):
  - (a) motortrucks with 3+ axles over 10,000 lb GVWR;
  - (b) truck tractors;
  - (c) buses, school buses, SPABs, youth buses, farm labor vehicles, modified limousines, and general public paratransit vehicles;
  - (e) trailers and dollies used with those vehicles;
  - (f) motortruck combinations over 40 ft;
  - (g) hazmat vehicles;
  - (k) CMVs of 26,001 lb or more, or a CMV towing a trailer over 10,000 lb;
  - plus others.
  - Source: https://california.public.law/codes/ca_veh_code_section_34500
- **Terminal inspections:**
  - Since 2016, terminals are selected by **performance**, using FMCSA SMS BASIC percentiles (https://www.chp.ca.gov/Documents/2021%20AB%201960%20-%20Basic%20Inspection%20of%20Terminals%20Program.pdf).
  - An unsatisfactory terminal must be reinspected **within 120 days**.
  - Review of an unsatisfactory rating: request within 5 business days; CHP decides within 10.
  - Vehicle sample by fleet size: 1–2 = all; 3–8 = 3; 9–15 = 4; 16–25 = 6; 26–50 = 9; 51–90 = 14; 91+ = 20.
  - The section sunsets **Jan. 1, 2031** unless extended.
  - Source: https://california.public.law/codes/ca_veh_code_section_34501.12
- **90-day carrier self-inspection (§34505.5):**
  - Applies to vehicles in §34500 (a), (b), (e), (f), (g), and (k).
  - Each vehicle is inspected **at least every 90 days**, as part of the maintenance and lubrication program.
  - Minimum items: brake adjustment; brake system components and leaks; steering and suspension; tires and wheels; connecting devices.
  - A defective vehicle may only be driven to repair. A carrier representative must sign off before it returns to service.
  - Records must identify the vehicle, give the date and nature of each inspection and repair, and carry a signature. They are kept **2 years** at the terminal.
  - Source: https://california.public.law/codes/ca_veh_code_section_34505.5
- **AB 3278 (2024):** effective Jan. 1, 2025, removed subdivision **(j)** vehicles from BIT and from the 90-day rule. (j) covers "other motortrucks regulated by DMV, DCA, or USDOT". One search summary instead described (j) as hazmat trucks; the statute lists hazmat under (g). Source: https://www.chp.ca.gov/siteassets/policy/ib/ib-removal-of-motortrucks-identified-in-sec.-34500j-of-the-cvc.pdf

**California — school buses and related vehicles**
- CHP inspects and certifies school buses, SPABs, and youth buses. The vehicle must carry a certificate dated **within 13 months** of operation.
  - School buses: https://california.public.law/codes/ca_veh_code_section_2807
  - SPABs: https://california.public.law/codes/ca_veh_code_section_2807.1
  - Youth buses: https://california.public.law/codes/ca_veh_code_section_2807.3
- Carriers must also inspect each bus every **3,000 miles or 45 days**, whichever comes first. At minimum this covers brake adjustment, tires, and steering (13 CCR §1232: https://regulations.justia.com/states/california/title-13/division-2/chapter-6-5/article-6/section-1232).

**Texas — commercial inspection after the 2025 changes**
- HB 3297 ended safety inspections for **non-commercial** vehicles on Jan. 1, 2025. **All commercial vehicles in all counties still need a passing safety inspection**, and emissions where applicable. Commercial vehicles are exempt from the $7.50 replacement fee (https://www.dps.texas.gov/news/vehicle-safety-inspection-program-changes-now-effect).
- **Texas definition of a commercial vehicle for inspection** (page dated Mar. 19, 2026: https://www.dps.texas.gov/section/vehicle-inspection/inspection-criteria-commercial-inspection):
  - GVWR over 26,000 lb;
  - farm vehicle of 48,000 lb GVWR or more;
  - 15+ passengers;
  - placarded hazmat;
  - GVWR over 10,000 lb in interstate commerce;
  - school bus operating at 60 mph outside an urban district.

### 3.12 CARB Clean Truck Check (California HD I/M)
- **Covers:** diesel and alternative-fuel vehicles **over 14,000 lb GVWR** on California roads, including out-of-state vehicles. Out-of-state motorhomes are excepted.
- **Exempt:** zero-emission vehicles; military tactical, emergency, and historic vehicles.
- **Requirements:**
  - report the vehicle in **CTC-VIS**;
  - pay the annual fee: **$31.18 in 2025; $32.13** for deadlines on or after Jan. 1, 2026;
  - pass the test, done by a **CARB-credentialed tester** (online course, at least 80% on the exam, renewed every 2 years). A passing test can be submitted up to 90 days before the deadline.
- **Test type:**
  - **OBD** for 2013+ diesel and 2018+ alternative-fuel engines;
  - **smoke opacity plus visual inspection** for older engines.
- **Frequency:**
  - most vehicles: **twice a year**;
  - agricultural vehicles and California motorhomes: annually;
  - OBD vehicles: rising to **4 times a year starting ~October 2027** (sources differ on the exact start).
- **Enforcement:**
  - Roadside emissions monitoring devices flag likely high emitters. The owner then has **30 days** to submit a passing test.
  - Noncompliance can bring DMV **registration holds**.
- Sources: https://ww2.arb.ca.gov/clean-truck-check-overview-fact-sheet ; https://ww2.arb.ca.gov/clean-truck-check-emissions-compliance-testing-requirements ; https://ww2.arb.ca.gov/resources/fact-sheets/clean-truck-check-compliance-fee-update-effective-112026 ; https://ww2.arb.ca.gov/resources/fact-sheets/clean-truck-check-faq-0

## 4) Audit implications

### 4.1 First, classify the vehicle and the inspection type
1. **Is it a CMV?** Check GVWR/GCWR on the door-jamb label: 10,001 lb or more in interstate commerce, or the 9+/16+ passenger thresholds, or placarded hazmat. If intrastate only, apply the state threshold (often 26,001 lb).
2. **Which inspection is this?** Each type has a different required scope and standard. An audit must not judge a DVIR against Appendix A, or call a PM a "DOT inspection".
   - DVIR (driver);
   - systematic PM (§396.3);
   - annual or periodic (§396.17 / Appendix A, or a state equivalent such as Texas or BIT 90-day);
   - roadside (CVSA level);
   - for-hire 19-point (CPUC) or TLC;
   - emissions (Clean Truck Check);
   - NFPA 1910 (apparatus);
   - school bus (CHP 13-month or the state NSTSP-based program).
3. **Which standard does each finding cite?** Appendix A reject limits, §393 operational limits, CVSA OOS (imminent hazard), OEM spec, TMC RP, or fleet policy pull point. These are different thresholds.

### 4.2 Regulatory completeness checks (annual inspection report, §396.21)
- [ ] The inspector is named, and the report identifies the carrier or IEP.
- [ ] The date is shown, and was within the past 12 months at the time of use. For state programs, count 12 months from the end of the month inspected.
- [ ] The vehicle is identified (VIN/unit number), and each unit of a combination has its own report.
- [ ] **Every Appendix A category** is listed with a result: brakes, coupling, exhaust, fuel, lighting, safe loading, steering, suspension, frame, tires, wheels/rims, windshield, wipers, and, where applicable, motorcoach seats and rear impact guard. Missing categories make the report incomplete.
- [ ] **Measured values** are recorded where a numeric limit exists: pushrod stroke by chamber type and size, lining thickness, tread depth at each position, steering lash. A bare "OK" with no measurement is weak evidence.
- [ ] A certification statement and signature are present.
- [ ] A decal or report copy is on the vehicle, and the decal's details match the report.
- [ ] The report is retained **14 months**.

### 4.3 Qualified inspector checks
- [ ] Annual inspector: evidence of a §396.19 qualification (state or federal training or certificate, or at least 1 year of training/experience) on file for the whole time they inspect plus 1 year. The exception is state program inspections.
- [ ] Brake inspector or technician: evidence of a §396.25 qualification (training program, certificate, CDL air brake tests, or at least 1 year) for employment plus 1 year.
- [ ] TNC/TCP in California: a **BAR-licensed facility** (check the BAR license number on the form).
- [ ] Clean Truck Check: a **CARB-credentialed tester** (tester ID).
- [ ] Red flag: the inspector signature matches the person who sold the repairs, and nothing shows the inspector is qualified. This is not illegal, but it is a conflict of interest worth noting.

### 4.5 Accuracy red flags (shop or vendor reports on commercial vehicles)
- **"DOT requires replacement"** at values above the regulatory limit. Examples: steer tires at 5/32 in or more; drive or trailer tires at 3/32 in or more; air-drum linings at 5/16 in or more. Unless the shop cites a fleet policy or OEM spec, this is over-selling.
- **Steer-axle lining limits stated wrongly:** Appendix A (1/4 in drum) and §393.47 (3/16 in drum) differ. Check which one the report claims.
- **"Out of adjustment" brake** with no stroke measurement, no chamber size, or a measurement not taken at 80–90 psi with brakes fully applied.
- **Manual readjustment of an automatic slack adjuster** recorded as the "fix" for over-stroke, with no diagnosis of the root cause (cam, bushings, adjuster). Industry guidance treats this as improper practice.
- **Steering lash** noted as excessive without the wheel diameter, or without saying whether the steering is power or manual. The limit depends on both.
- **Wheel-end "bearing play"** called excessive without a dial-indicator end-play value. The RP 618 target is 0.001–0.005 in.
- **Windshield crack** failed even though it lies in the excluded border zones, or is narrower than 1/4 in and not intersecting another crack.
- **"Failed DOT inspection"** on a vehicle that is not a CMV (e.g., a 9,000 lb GVWR pickup used intrastate). Federal Part 396 does not apply. State rules may.
- **Annual inspection decal with no matching report**, a report backdated, or every component marked "pass" with zero findings on a high-mileage unit while the same work order sells repairs on those very components. That is an internal inconsistency.
- **CVSA language misused:** calling a vehicle "out of service" for a non-OOS defect, or presenting a CVSA decal as the annual inspection.
- **School bus:** a shop "certifying" a California school bus. Only CHP issues the annual certificate. The shop does the 45-day/3,000-mile carrier inspections.
- **TNC (California):** an inspection done at a non-BAR facility; an inspection older than 12 months, or over 50,000 miles since the last one; any of the 19 points missing.
- **Clean Truck Check:** a tester with no CARB credential; an OBD vehicle smoke-tested instead; testing frequency misrepresented to justify extra tests.

### 4.6 Perspective-specific guidance
- **Fleet manager / carrier safety officer:** priorities are completeness and defensibility. Annual reports must cover every Appendix A item with measurements. DVIR defects must close out with a signed repair certification before dispatch. Inspector and brake-technician qualification files must be current. Retention clocks must be met: 3 months / 14 months / 1 year / plus 1 year. Recommendations above the legal floor should be checked against the fleet's PM policy, which often uses TMC-style pull points. They should not simply be accepted as "required".
- **Shop owner auditing a technician:**
  - Confirm the tech meets §396.19 and §396.25 if the shop does annuals or brake work for carriers.
  - The report should list measured values, the cited standard, and photos.
  - Every "fail" must map to a specific Appendix A or §393 criterion. Every advisory should be labeled as preventive, not regulatory.
  - The invoice should match the findings: no billed repairs for passed items, and no failed items left unaddressed or undocumented as "declined".
  - Labor should be consistent with the inspection scope.
- **Driver / owner-operator / rideshare driver (consumer):**
  - Know your vehicle's required checklist: 11-item DVIR; CPUC 19 points; your city's TNC form.
  - Ask the shop *which standard* each failure is based on, and for the measurement.
  - A tire or brake "fail" above the legal minimum is a recommendation, not a legal requirement.
  - For TNC, a pass at a properly licensed facility (BAR in California) is what the platform or regulator needs.
  - Do not let a TNC inspection visit become an upsell of unrelated maintenance.

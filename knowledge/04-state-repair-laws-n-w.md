# 04 — State Auto Repair Consumer Protection Laws (Montana through Wyoming, plus DC)

## 1) Scope and as-of date

- **As of:** 2026-10-20. The research used web sources that were reachable on that date. Most statute pages showed their own currency notes, and those notes are quoted where they matter.
- **Jurisdictions (26):** MT, NE, NV, NH, NJ, NM, NY, NC, ND, OH, OK, OR, PA, RI, SC, SD, TN, TX, UT, VT, VA, WA, WV, WI, WY, DC. Another file covers A–M.
- **What this file covers:** repair-specific consumer statutes and regulations about estimates, authorization, overages, invoices, used or rebuilt parts, return of replaced parts, record retention, and bans on unnecessary repairs. It also notes shop registration and technician licensing. It does **not** cover emissions or safety-inspection station licensing, auto body or insurer appraisal rules (unless they bear on estimates), or lemon laws, except in passing.
- **Not legal advice.** All text is paraphrased. Each claim is tagged by how well it was checked:
  - **[V]** checked against a primary source (legislature, agency, or official code site) during this research.
  - **[S]** supported only by secondary sources (news, legal aid, or code aggregators).
  - **[UNVERIFIED]** could not be confirmed. This includes cases where a statute's *absence* was assumed but not searched exhaustively.
- **Research limit:** the shared web-search budget ran out partway through. After that, verification relied on direct fetches of known official URLs. Areas left thin are flagged in Section 5.

### Nevada
- **Registration:**
  - Since 1/1/1998, a "garage" must register with the DMV yearly, list its services and number of mechanics, and post a **$5,000 surety bond**. Garages serve the public with repair of major systems, diagnostics, alignment, and oil changes; fuel-only stations are excluded.
  - A shop that knowingly fails to register or keep its bond **cannot enforce a lien** or sue on repair contracts for that period (NRS 487.530–.570).
  - A "Registered Garage" sign is required (NRS 487.6871). [V]
- **Payment methods:** before authorization, the shop must disclose in writing which payment methods it accepts (NRS 487.6874). [V]
- **Estimates:**
  - A written estimate is required for repairs over **$50**, showing labor and parts. For diagnosis jobs, it must also include the costs of diagnosis, disassembly, and reassembly if the customer declines the repair (NRS 487.6875).
  - A waiver must be in writing and signed at authorization (NRS 487.6879). [V]
- **Overage:**
  - If added charges would exceed the estimate by the **lesser of 20% or $100**, the shop must notify the owner (and any insurer) and may not do the added work until they respond (NRS 487.6877/.6881/.688).
  - Repairs must follow manufacturer specifications and the most recent agreed estimate. [V]
- **Invoice:** the statement of charges must carry the authorizer's name and signature, an itemized list of parts with labor, and descriptions of other charges. A non-compliant statement blocks lien enforcement (NRS 487.6893). [V]
- **Parts:** returned on request. Warranty/exchange parts are excepted, but the customer may inspect them (NRS 487.6883). [V]
- **Records:** estimates, statements, and waivers kept **1 year** (NRS 487.6885). [V]
- **Enforcement:**
  - Deceptive practices are defined by reference to NRS 598 plus DMV regulations.
  - Administrative fines up to $10,000; civil penalties of $500 per knowing violation (NRS 487.6889–.6897).
  - The Bureau of Consumer Protection may run undercover investigations. [V]
- **Changes 2023–2026:** none to the repair sections. A 2025 act repealed NRS 487.002 (Advisory Board on Automotive Affairs). [V]
- Source: https://www.leg.state.nv.us/nrs/nrs-487.html ; also https://law.justia.com/codes/nevada/chapter-487/statute-487-6875

### New Hampshire
- **RSA 358-D (1977):**
  - A written estimate is required only **on request**, before work. It must break down the work and give estimated labor and parts prices and a completion date (358-D:2).
  - Written permission is needed to proceed, with an oral exception when written approval is impracticable (358-D:3).
  - The shop may not charge more than **10% over the estimate without the customer's written consent** (358-D:5).
- **Invoice (358-D:10):**
  - Lists all work, including subcontracted work.
  - Lists each part over $0.50 with its retail cost, plus labor hours and cost.
  - States any guarantee terms and clearly marks used, rebuilt, or reconditioned parts.
  - The shop keeps a copy **1 year**.
- **Parts (358-D:9):** returned if requested in advance of the work, except warranty/exchange parts.
- **Coverage:** excludes motor trucks and motorcycles (358-D:1).
- **Remedies (358-D:12):**
  - A violation is an unfair/deceptive act under RSA 358-A:2.
  - A violating shop **loses any right to payment**, except for work it proves was authorized.
  - Paying a bill does not waive the customer's rights.
- A sign of at least 6 sq ft is required (358-D:11). [V]
- **Changes 2023–2026:** airbag/SRS provisions (358-D:11-a, 11-b) are in the chapter. Their enactment dates were not checked. [UNVERIFIED]
- Sources: https://gc.nh.gov/rsa/html/NHTOC/NHTOC-XXXI-358-D.htm ; https://gc.nh.gov/rsa/html/XXXI/358-D/358-D-2.htm ; …-5.htm ; …-9.htm ; …-10.htm ; …-12.htm

### New Jersey
- **N.J.A.C. 13:45A-26C.2** (automotive repair deceptive practices, under the Consumer Fraud Act, N.J.S.A. 56:8-1):
  - **Authorization:** paid work needs a signed written authorization stating the repair and the odometer reading. Oral authorization is allowed for after-hours or third-party drop-offs and must be recorded with date, time, name, and phone number.
  - **Estimate:** a written estimate is required before paid work, in one of several forms: not-to-exceed price, parts/labor breakdown, specific-repair price, or dollar-limit authorization. It can be waived only by a signed writing.
  - **Diagnostics:** the fee must be agreed in advance and cannot be billed twice if the customer has the repair done.
  - **Overage:** none without consent obtained before the extra work; oral consent is recorded.
  - **Invoice:** parts and labor itemized separately, with each part stated as new, rebuilt, reconditioned, or used.
  - **Parts:** returned if requested before work, unless impractical or exchange/warranty.
  - **Other:** a customer-rights sign is required; written guarantees have set content rules; misleading statements meant to induce authorization are barred. [V via Cornell LII; Justia returned HTTP 403]
- **Licensing:** auto body repair facilities are licensed separately by the MVC under N.J.A.C. 13:21-21. Its estimate rule (13:21-21.10) requires listing non-new parts and a parts-return statement. No general repair-shop license was found. [S/V partial]
- **Record retention:** not found in 26C.2. [UNVERIFIED]
- Source: https://www.law.cornell.edu/regulations/new-jersey/N-J-A-C-13-45A-26C-2 ; https://www.law.cornell.edu/regulations/new-jersey/N-J-A-C-13-21-21-10

### New Mexico
- **12.2.6 NMAC** (AG rule under the Unfair Practices Act; effective 1/1/1998; recompiled 2001; no later amendments shown):
  - **Estimate:** a written estimate is required for repairs **> $100**, with related repairs diagnosed together added up. A signed waiver is valid only if the customer was fully informed.
  - **Overage:** **greater of 10% or $50**.
  - **Oral approval:** must record the person, phone numbers, exact work approved, and date/time; a written copy is due at pickup.
  - **Invoice:** itemized unless parts/materials are ≤ $50; sublet work marked.
  - **Parts disclosure:** used, rebuilt, or aftermarket crash parts must be disclosed, otherwise they are presumed new.
  - **Replaced parts:** kept available for inspection.
  - **Other:** labor-rate methods and warranty terms must be posted. [V]
- **Note:** the Justia copy (current through Sept 2024) showed the $100 estimate rule. The overage language was seen only in the state-hosted copy. The two are believed consistent. [V/partial]
- Source: https://srca.nm.gov/parts/title12/12.002.0006.html

### New York
- **Registration:** VTL § 398-c requires every repair shop to register with the DMV. Fees: $10 application fee, then $150 per location every 2 years. A green-and-white "Registered State of New York Motor Vehicle Repair Shop" sign is posted outside. [V]
- **Estimates and invoices (VTL § 398-d; 15 NYCRR Part 82):**
  - A written estimate is required **on request**; a reasonable fee may be charged.
  - The shop cannot exceed the estimate without consent; phone approvals are recorded on the invoice with date, time, and name.
  - The invoice details each repair and part with costs, flags used or non-original parts, and shows the odometer reading at drop-off. Body parts on vehicles up to 18,000 lb are marked OEM, non-OEM, or used.
  - **Parts:** returned on a **written request before work**, except warranty/exchange parts. Parts are held for phone-authorized jobs.
  - **Records:** estimates, work orders, invoices, and parts purchase orders kept **2 years** (15 NYCRR 82.9).
- **Other:** body-shop estimators need a DMV license. Complaints go to the DMV on form VS-35 within 90 days or 3,000 miles, and the DMV may order restitution. [V]
- **Local:** Nassau and Suffolk County consumer-affairs licensing lists did **not** show a general auto repair license (Nassau licenses dismantlers and scrap processors; Suffolk does not list auto repair). NYC was not confirmed. [S — absence not confirmed; check county codes]
- **Changes 2023–2026:** none found. 398-c was last revised in 2014 per nysenate.gov.

### North Carolina
- **Motor Vehicle Repair Act, G.S. 20-354 to 20-354.9:**
  - **Exemptions:** jobs of **$350 or less**, government shops, vehicles of 26,001 lb or more, and certain third-party-paid jobs.
  - **Estimate:** written estimate required before diagnosis or repair when the cost will exceed **$350** (parts, labor, diagnostics, teardown, taxes, supplies). It records the customer's choice on keeping replaced parts and the daily storage charge. A written waiver is allowed, including for a set period. A partial waiver is implied for after-hours drop-offs (20-354.3).
  - **Estimate fee:** must be disclosed and authorized in writing (20-354.4).
  - **Overage:** prompt notice required. The cap is the estimate plus authorized increases **plus 10%**. A shop may not hold the car once the customer pays that amount (20-354.5).
  - **Invoice:** itemizes labor, parts, and merchandise, and identifies used, rebuilt, or reconditioned parts (20-354.6).
  - **Sign:** 24 inches per side (20-354.7).
  - **Prohibited acts (20-354.8):** charging for unauthorized repairs; misrepresenting the need for repairs or a safety danger; altering documents; substituting used parts without notice.
  - **Remedies:** private civil action with attorney fees (20-354.9). No record-retention rule. [V]
- Source: https://ncleg.gov/EnactedLegislation/Statutes/HTML/ByArticle/Chapter_20/Article_15B.html

### Ohio
- **Ohio Adm. Code 109:4-3-13** (Consumer Sales Practices Act rule; current version effective **March 21, 2026**):
  - **Estimate choice:** when anticipated cost exceeds **$50**, the customer initials a choice of written, oral, or no estimate. A signed version is used for after-hours drop-offs.
  - **Small jobs:** if no estimate was given and the job is under $50, the shop needs authorization before exceeding $50.
  - **Overage:** authorization is needed for added work of **10% or more** of the estimate (excluding tax); the bill may not exceed the estimate by more than 10% without approval.
  - **Invoice:** itemizes parts with costs, labor charge, and **who performed the work**, and marks used, rebuilt, or remanufactured parts.
  - **Parts:** replaced parts offered back, with exceptions for rebuild/warranty. Intended reuse must be disclosed before work.
  - **Prohibitions:** falsely claiming a repair is necessary or was done; falsely calling a vehicle dangerous; charging for unauthorized work; materially understating estimates. [V]
- **Discrepancy:** the AG business guide still says **$25**, but the authenticated 2026 rule text says **$50**. Treat $50 as current. The rule summary did not describe what the 2026 amendment changed. [UNVERIFIED: delta]
- **Local:** Summit County has its own motor vehicle repair code (ch. 769). [S]

### Oregon
- **ORS 646A.480–.495 (2009 c.133):**
  - **Estimate:** required **before beginning any work**. It describes the work, splits it into tasks, lists labor, parts, and incidental charges, and gives a total, which may be a reasonable range. If the shop will disassemble the vehicle to evaluate it, the estimate must also give teardown/evaluation cost, reassembly cost, and reassembly time (646A.482).
  - **Separate authorization:** required for any evaluation, teardown, or repair action costing **> $200**, and for changes that raise cost by more than the **lesser of 10% or $200**.
  - **How to authorize:** signature on the estimate; phone (record name, phone number, date, and time on the estimate); or fax/email, attached to the estimate (646A.486).
  - **Prohibited (646A.490):** billing for work not performed even if it was on an authorized estimate; using used parts when the estimate said new; knowingly installing used/reconditioned parts without disclosure.
  - **Reassembly:** the shop must reassemble the vehicle.
  - **Records:** kept **1 year**. [V]
- **Enforcement:** treated as an unlawful trade practice under the UTPA (ORS 646.608). [S: 2009 measure analysis; the exact 646.608 paragraph was not fetched]
- **Changes:** the official ORS 646A page says the **2026 session amended or repealed some sections of chapter 646A**. Whether 646A.480–.495 were affected is [UNVERIFIED].

### Pennsylvania
- **37 Pa. Code § 301.5** (AG Automotive Industry Trade Practices, under the UTPCPL):
  - **Authorization:** no charge for repairs or prices not authorized **in writing** (or clearly posted). When cost is unknown at drop-off, the customer chooses: (i) no work until the shop gives a price and gets approval; (ii) work up to a customer-set cap; or (iii) no cap, after the hourly rate is disclosed.
  - **Oral authorization:** logged with date and time, the employee, the approver, what was approved, and the number dialed if the shop called.
  - **Before authorization, the shop must disclose:** parts-return/inspection rights; whether each part is new, used, reconditioned, or rebuilt; storage charges; any estimate/diagnosis fee.
  - **Invoice:** itemized parts with names/numbers and prices, labor hours × rate, and odometer reading.
  - **Prohibited:** false or careless statements about the need for repairs, unsafe condition, or work done; charging for repairs not performed.
  - **Timing and quality:** repairs completed within 24 hours or the stated time unless the customer agrees to a delay; work not done in a workmanlike manner corrected free on prompt complaint. [V]
- **No statutory dollar threshold or overage percentage.** Record retention not stated (§ 301.2 has a 60-day rule for price-reduction advertising records).
- Sources: https://www.pacodeandbulletin.gov/Display/pacode?file=/secure/pacode/data/037/chapter301/s301.5.html ; …/chap301toc.html

### Rhode Island
- **Motor Vehicle Repair Shop Act, R.I. Gen. Laws ch. 5-38.3** (1994; amended 2004):
  - **Estimate:** written estimate **on request**, with a creditable fee allowed. It lists parts and labor, flags parts that are not new or not OEM-grade, states whether labor is clock-hour or flat-rate (naming the manual), and gives notice of parts-return rights. A job rate is allowed for mechanical/electrical repairs under $200.
  - **Overage:** no charge beyond the estimate without consent.
  - **Oral authorization:** noted on the invoice with date, time, method, and person.
  - **Invoice:** itemized parts and labor, used/rebuilt status, odometer in and out, warranty terms.
  - **Parts:** returned on a timely written request; presumed requested for phone-authorized jobs.
  - **Time cards:** mechanics' time cards open to customer inspection.
  - **Enforcement:** by the AG (5-38.3-4 to -6). [V]
- **2025:** a law on auto body/insurer appraisal (with a "loser pays" provision) was reported. It concerns insurer claims, not shop estimates. [S]

## 4) Audit implications — what an auditor can check

These checks apply whether the reviewer is a consumer checking their own paperwork or a shop owner auditing a technician's inspection report (DVI), estimate, and invoice.

1. **Find the state first.** Ten jurisdictions in this half (MT, NE, ND, OK, SC, SD, TX, VT, WV, WY) have no repair-specific statute found; only UDAP applies there. Do not cite thresholds that do not exist. Frame findings as "misrepresentation of need" or "charged for work not performed," which UDAP bans everywhere (e.g., TX DTPA § 17.46(b)(13) and (b)(22)).
2. **Estimate trigger** — compare the invoice total against the state threshold:
   - **Required automatically:** NV > $50; NM > $100; WA > $100; NC > $350; OR any work, plus separate auth for actions > $200; NJ any paid work; DC any repair; WI > $50 (written repair order with estimate choices); OH > $50 (customer chooses).
   - **Only on request:** NH, NY, RI, VA (> $25), TN (> $250, plus a duty to disclose the right).
   - **No fixed $ but written authorization required:** PA; UT (express authorization).
   - **Waiver:** most states let the customer waive the estimate only **in a signed writing**. If the shop claims a waiver, look for the signed document.
3. **Overage cushion** — compute (invoice − tax) ÷ authorized estimate. Many states measure pre-tax.

   | Cushion | Jurisdictions |
   |---|---|
   | **0% (any increase needs consent)** | NJ, NY, RI, WI |
   | **10%** | NH (written consent), NC, OH, UT, VA (20% for vehicles ≥ 25 model years old), WA |
   | **Lesser of 10% or $200** | OR |
   | **Greater of 10% or $50** | NM |
   | **Lesser of 20% or $100** (notice) | NV |
   | **20% if ≤ $300 / 10% if > $300** | DC |
   | **25%** | TN |
   | **Customer-set cap** | PA, plus WI option (2) |

   An overrun beyond the cushion with no recorded authorization is the single most common, objectively checkable violation.
4. **Oral/phone authorization record** — states that specify content usually want: date, time, name of the approver, phone number, what was approved and the added cost, and the employee who took it. Those states are NJ, NM, NY, OR, PA, RI, WA, WI, and DC. Oregon also lists fax/email approval. A DVI "customer approved via text link" entry should carry equivalent data: timestamp, identity, line items, dollar amount.
5. **Invoice content checklist:**
   - Each part named, with part number where required (WA, PA), and its price.
   - Labor hours and rate, or a disclosed flat rate. WI also wants actual time if flat-rate is shown.
   - Odometer reading in (NJ, NY, PA, RI, WA, DC).
   - **Each part labeled new, used, rebuilt, reconditioned, or aftermarket.** This is required in essentially every state with a repair law here.
   - **Who performed the work:** OH, UT, WI.
   - **The reason for each repair:** UT. This is a direct hook for checking that each DVI finding supports its line item.
6. **Parts return** — check whether the customer requested parts **at or before authorization**. Most states require the request before work (NH, NJ, NY in writing, NC, RI in writing, WA, WI). VA and OH require the shop to **offer** return. Warranty, core, and exchange parts are usually exempt from return but often still open to inspection (NV, VA). Failing to produce requested parts is a strong red flag for "replaced" parts that weren't.
7. **Unnecessary-repair hooks** — the strongest textual bases for flagging upsells:
   - **WA RCW 46.71.045:** charging for unnecessary repairs, meaning no reasonable basis in manufacturer specs, industry standards, or customer request. This is the best match for auditing DVI recommendations against OEM maintenance schedules and specs.
   - **WI ATCP 132.09:** falsely claiming a repair is needed for safety or operation; altering a vehicle to create a need.
   - **OH 109:4-3-13 / UT R152-11-5 / PA 301.5 / NC 20-354.8:** misrepresenting that a repair is necessary or that a vehicle is dangerous.
   - **OR 646A.490 / WA / PA:** billing for work not performed, even if it was on the authorized estimate.
   - **NV 487.6877** and **OR 646A.486:** repairs must follow manufacturer specs (NV) and authorized scope.
8. **Diagnostic/teardown fees** — NC, NJ, OR, VA, WI, and DC require the fee (or teardown and reassembly cost) to be disclosed and agreed before diagnosis. A diagnostic fee on the invoice with no prior disclosure is a finding.
9. **Records/lookback** — WA, NV, NH, and OR require 1-year retention; NY and WI require 2 years. A shop auditor can request the full repair order, estimate, authorization log, and parts invoices for that window. Parts *purchase* invoices (NY, WI) let you confirm a part was actually bought.
10. **Registration** — in NV, NY, and DC, check that the shop's registration or license is current. In NV, an unregistered garage cannot enforce a lien. DC estimate forms must be DLCP-approved and state the bond.

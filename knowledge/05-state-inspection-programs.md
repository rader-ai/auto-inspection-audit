# 05 — State-Mandated Vehicle Safety Inspection and Emissions (I/M) Programs

Division 5 of 9. Covers official government inspection programs, not shop courtesy inspections. Use this file as an **objective benchmark**: when a shop marks an item "fail/red/unsafe," compare it to the official reject criteria in the vehicle's state, where one exists.

---

## 1) Scope and as-of date

- **As of:** 2026-10-20. Research ran 2026-10-20. This area changed a lot in 2025-2026 (Texas, New Hampshire, Louisiana repeals; Missouri and West Virginia repeal bills; North Carolina emissions removal). Anything marked **[UNVERIFIED]** could not be confirmed from a primary or recent source during this session. **Tool limit:** the session's web-search budget ran out partway through, so several states (HI, WV, RI, DE, NJ details; Ontario; EU) rely on secondary sources or direct page fetches only.
- **In scope:** US periodic passenger-vehicle safety inspection programs, US emissions (I/M) programs including OBD-II, consumer protections inside those programs, and short international notes (Canada, UK, EU).
- **Out of scope:** Commercial/FMCSA inspections (see 06-commercial-fleet-specialty.md), detailed technical service limits (see 07-technical-inspection-criteria.md), and general repair-shop consumer law (see 04 and 09).
- **Key principle:** A state inspection standard is a **legal minimum for road use**. It is not a maintenance guideline. A part can pass inspection and still be close to worn out (several states require or encourage an "advisory" note for that). It also works the other way: a shop can't honestly call a part a "state inspection failure" if it meets the state's reject criteria.

## 3) Program details and reject-criteria thresholds

> These are **paraphrased** from official regulations and manuals. Always check the current text. "Reject" means the state's legal failure criterion. "Advisory" means the inspector should warn the owner, but it is **not** a failure.

### 3.1 Pennsylvania — 67 Pa. Code Ch. 175 (PennDOT) [S15][S24]
- **Brakes:** Reject bonded lining/pads under **2/32 in** at the thinnest point. Reject riveted linings under **1/32 in** above the rivet head. Reject drums over the stamped maximum diameter, or more than 0.090 in over original if unmarked. Reject rotors under the stamped or manufacturer minimum thickness. Reject drum or rotor scoring deeper than **0.015 in**. Reject pedal travel over 80% of available travel. The car must also meet the regulation's stopping-distance table.
- **Tires:** Reject under **2/32 in** in two adjacent grooves at any point, or if wear bars touch the road in two adjacent grooves. (The 4/32 front-tire rule applies only to vehicles over 10,000 lb.)
- **Steering/suspension:** Steering-wheel free play limit depends on wheel diameter: 2 in for wheels 16 in or smaller, up to 2¾ in for 22 in. Reject tire movement over **¼ in** measured at the front or rear of the tire. Reject ball-joint play beyond manufacturer spec. Reject shocks that keep rocking past 3 cycles. Reject a side-to-side wheelbase difference over 1 in.
- **Lights:** Turn signals must flash 60-120 times per minute. Headlamp aim may be off by no more than 4 in.
- **Glazing:** Reject damage that interferes with vision in the driver's critical area (8½ × 5½ in centered on the driver's line of sight). Rules also limit stickers and etching on rear glass.
- **Exhaust:** Reject **any** leak, hole, crack or loose joint. No numeric threshold.
- **Horn:** Must be audible at **200 ft**.
- **Wipers:** Must work at the manufacturer's rate, or 45 cycles/min if unspecified. Reject blades that streak after 5 cycles, and reject inoperative factory washers.
- **Consumer protections (§175.29(f)) [S24]:**
  - The station needs the owner's permission before repairs.
  - The owner may do the repairs or take the car to **any** shop.
  - Replaced parts must be kept for the customer to examine.
  - Parts that pass but may become unsafe before the next inspection must be noted in writing.
  - **Brake and tire measurements must be written on the receipt or work order.** This is a strong audit lever: a PA "failed brakes" claim with no recorded measurement is a documentation defect.
- **Emissions waiver:** An owner who still fails after spending at least a minimum amount on emissions repairs can get a waiver. The minimum was raised from $150 to **$450** (PennDOT bulletin, apparently 2023) and is adjusted every year under §177.282. The current figure is **[UNVERIFIED]** [S25][S26].

### 3.2 Virginia — 19VAC30-70 (Virginia State Police); 2026 Safety Inspection Manual [S21][S27][S28][S29]
- **Brakes (‑80):** Reject any lining or pad under **2/32 in** at any point. Don't measure in manufactured slots or over rivet heads. **A pad-wear warning light alone is not a reason to reject**; the inspector must measure. Reject rotors or drums worn or scored beyond the manufacturer's spec. Reject an external rotor or drum crack longer than half the width of the friction surface. Reject stopping distance over **25 ft from 20 mph** (4-wheel brakes). Hold, start and reserve tests also apply.
- **Tires (‑130):** Reject under **2/32 in**, measured in the two adjacent grooves where tread is thinnest. Reject only if both grooves are under 2/32 at the first point **and** at two more spots around the tire. Reject if wear bars touch the road in two adjacent grooves at 3 spots. Plugs and patches are allowed in the tread area only.
- **Steering/suspension (‑110):**
  - Wheel play at the top or bottom of the tire: ¼ in (wheels under 17 in), ⅜ in (17-18 in), ½ in (over 18 in).
  - Steering-linkage play at the front or rear of the tire: same scale.
  - Steering-wheel lash on passenger cars: 2 in power, 3 in manual, **0.4 in for rack-and-pinion**.
  - Wheel bearing: reject movement over ⅛ in at the tire's outer edge.
  - Wear-indicator ball joints: reject when the indicator surface is flush with or inside the cover.
- **Windshield (‑210):** Above the bottom 3 in, reject pits, chips or star cracks over **1½ in** in diameter. Reject multiple cracks running from one point when one is over 1½ in. Reject cracks that let pieces of glass move. Reject a scratch in the driver's wiper area over ¼ in wide and 6 in long.
- **Other items:** Reject **any** exhaust leak (built-in drain holes excepted) (‑300). Horn must be audible at 200 ft (‑240). The **airbag readiness light is advisory only** (‑290). Headlamp aim tolerance is 4 in.
- **Fees and reinspection:**
  - Maximum safety-inspection fee is **$20** (§46.2-1167). Reinspection at the same station within the sticker's validity costs **$1**.
  - The rejection sticker is valid for 15 days. The same station may reinspect only the failed items, at most twice.
  - The owner may take the car to **another station**, which then performs a full inspection.
  - Stations may not add a fee for drop-off inspections [S21][S22].

### 3.3 New York — 15 NYCRR Part 79 (CR-79), NY DMV [S8][S9][S31]
- **Brakes:** Bonded linings must be at least **1/16 in** (= 2/32). Riveted linings must be at least 1/32 in above the rivet. Pedal reserve must be ⅓, and the pedal must hold under pressure for 1 minute. Pads, rotors/drums, hoses, parking brake and side-to-side balance are also checked.
- **Tires:** Reject under **2/32 in** in two adjacent major grooves. Reject fabric cuts of 1 in or more, and bulges. The spare is not inspected. **Tire pressure is advisory only.**
- **Glass:** Reject a windshield crack **11 in or longer** that touches the wiper sweep area. Tint is also checked.
- **Advisory, not reject:** Airbag warning lamp; tire pressure [S8][S31].
- **Not inspected:** Side markers, parking lamps, side-mounted turn lamps [S8].
- **Emissions:** OBD-II applies to most 1996+ gas and 1997+ diesel vehicles of 8,500 lb GVWR or less. The test checks MIL (check-engine light) bulb function and command status plus OBD data. A Low Enhanced visual/gas-cap test applies to other vehicles up to 25 model years old [S8].
- **Consumer protections [S8][S9]:**
  - The station needs your permission before any repair.
  - You are **not required** to have repairs or re-inspection done at the inspecting station. You may use any registered repair shop or do the work yourself.
  - Any deposit may not exceed the inspection fee.
  - A station that can't inspect promptly must offer a written appointment within 8 working days.
  - The full inspection fee is owed whether the car passes or fails. A reinspection fee applies only if the car leaves and comes back. Within 30 days only the failed category is redone; after 30 days both are [S31].
  - Complaints go on DMV Form VS-35. Repair-shop complaints must be filed within 90 days or 3,000 miles.
- Fees (from memory, **[UNVERIFIED]**): about $10 safety, plus about $11 OBD upstate or $27 in the NYC metro area.

### 3.4 Massachusetts — 540 CMR 4.00 (RMV); Mass Vehicle Check [S10][S11]
- **Tires:** Reject under **2/32 in** in any original groove, or if a wear bar touches the road. Reject cuts over 1 in or visible cords. Studded tires are prohibited May 1 to Nov 1.
- **Brakes:** Service brake must stop from 20 mph within **30 ft**; parking brake within 80 ft. Reject if the pedal sinks or less than 20% of travel remains under about 125 lb of force for 10 s, or if the warning light is on. Reject leaks or damaged lines. No pad-thickness figure appears in the section fetched **[UNVERIFIED]**.
- **Steering:** Lash limit is 2 in for manual-steering passenger cars.
- **Windshield:**
  - Stone bruise or bullseye: reject over 1 in inside the critical viewing area, or over 2 in outside it.
  - Single crack: reject if it reaches more than 3 in into the critical area.
  - Wiper scrape: reject over ¼ in wide in the critical area.
  - Windshield tint: limited to above the AS-1 line or the top 6 in.
- **Exhaust:** Reject visible black or blue smoke when warm, leaks, or noise above original equipment.
- **Also rejected:** An airbag warning lamp showing a fault, a deployed or missing airbag (unlike NY and VA), and a missing or ill-fitting gas cap.
- **Retest:** A rejection certificate lets you drive for **60 days**, provided safety defects are fixed. It also gives **one free reinspection at the same station** within 60 days.
- Inspection fee ($35) and the cost cap on emissions waivers: from memory, **[UNVERIFIED]**.

### 3.5 Vermont — VN-113 Periodic Inspection Manual (rev. 3/2026), VT DMV [S19][S20]
Vermont is the best example of a **two-tier "Reject vs. Advise customer"** structure, which is the model a shop's DVI should follow.
- **Tires:** Reject under **2/32 in** in any two major grooves, exposed cord, bulges, or mismatched tires on an axle. **Advise only** for tread between 2/32 and 4/32, tires over 8 years old, wrong size, cuts, or weather cracking.
- **Brakes:**
  - Reject rotors under the manufacturer minimum, drums over the manufacturer maximum, and collapsed vent vanes.
  - Reject **more than ½ in of cumulative pitting** on a braking surface, and cracks reaching the open edge.
  - Reject **lining under the manufacturer minimum spec** (VT uses OEM spec, not a fixed 2/32), and cracked or delaminated lining.
  - Reject leaking calipers or wheel cylinders. Must stop within 30 ft from 20 mph.
  - **Rust buildup on rotor surfaces is advisory only.**
  - A non-working parking brake on an **automatic** is advisory only; on a manual it is a reject.
- **Warning lamps:** A lit brake-warning lamp is a reject. **TPMS light, airbag light, defroster and speedometer lamp are advisory.** ADAS faults (backup camera and similar) are advisory except on fully self-driving vehicles.
- **Windshield:** Reject intersecting cracks in the critical area, aftermarket windshield tint, and non-safety glass. **Advise** for damage outside the critical area, or 2+ star breaks over 1.5 in inside it. The critical area starts 2 in from the left pillar, runs 2 in past center, and is 10 in tall.
- **Body:** Holes or torn metal in **non-structural** areas are advisory. Metal tape is allowed for holes under 2 in. Structural rot (floor pan, rockers, frame) is a reject.
- **OBD-II (vehicles 16 model years old or newer, ≤8,500 lb):** Reject a missing or tampered connector, a MIL that doesn't light with key on and engine off, a MIL lit with the engine running, a MIL commanded on, or **too few readiness monitors set** (the DMV/DEC sets the number). A repair-assistance program exists for low-income owners [S20].
- **Fees:** The station must post a flat fee or an hourly rate and give an **itemized invoice and an AVIP Vehicle Inspection Report** whether the car passes or fails. Invoices are kept for 3 years [S19].

### 3.6 Maine — 16-222 CMR ch. 1 (Maine State Police) [S12][S32]
- **Windshield:** Reject a crack only if it is over **6 in** long **and** inside the critical vision area.
- **Exhaust:** Reject pipes extending more than 2 in beyond the body or bumper, unless factory.
- **Steering:** Reject bushings loose beyond manufacturer spec. Ball joints and tie rods are judged by the manufacturer's procedure.
- **Rust:** Reject brake or fuel lines at specified rust stages, and rust holes in unibody structure or rockers.
- **Tread:** By statute, a tire with under **2/32 in** of tread is not in safe condition [S32] (older code version; current text **[UNVERIFIED]**).
- **Pads:** A 2/32 bonded-lining minimum appears in the motorcycle section. The passenger-car figure is **[UNVERIFIED]**.

### 3.7 North Carolina — G.S. 20-183.2 et seq.; NCDMV [S13][S14]
- Annual safety inspection covers brakes, lights, horn, steering, wipers, directional signals, tires (**2/32 in**, per a secondary source [S33]) and mirrors.
- Window tint (32% limit) was removed from the safety inspection effective 12/1/2025.
- Emissions in 19 counties, done at the same visit as safety. Vehicles over 8,500 lb get safety only. Emissions waivers exist under G.S. 20-183.5.
- Fees (secondary source [S33]): about $13.60 safety-only, about $30 combined.

### 3.8 Texas — HB 3297 (2023) [S16][S17][S18]
- **No safety inspection** for non-commercial vehicles since 1/1/2025. A Texas shop that tells a non-commercial customer "you need this to pass state inspection" for brakes, tires or lights is **factually wrong**, unless the vehicle is commercial.
- **Emissions only** in Brazoria, Collin, Dallas, Denton, El Paso, Ellis, Fort Bend, Galveston, Harris, Johnson, Kaufman, Montgomery, Parker, Rockwall, Tarrant, Travis and Williamson counties, plus Bexar from 11/1/2026. The test is OBD-II for gas vehicles 2-24 years old.
- A free retest is available within 15 days at the original station (per DPS FAQ, via search summary).

### 3.9 Other safety states (brief)
- **Delaware:** State-run DMV lanes, free of charge. Checks safety items (tires, brakes, windows), a fuel-system pressure test, and OBD-II. Because the state does the inspecting, there is no conflict of interest between the inspector and a repair shop [S30].
- **Hawaii, Rhode Island, West Virginia:** Detailed reject criteria were not retrieved this session **[UNVERIFIED]**. WV moved to biennial inspections on 1/1/2024 [S1].
- **Missouri:** Inspections by MSHP-licensed stations. The MVI section investigates consumer complaints [S34]. Thresholds not retrieved **[UNVERIFIED]**.

### 3.10 Emissions programs and OBD-II basics [S35][S36][S20][S8]
- The Clean Air Act requires I/M programs in about **33 state/local areas** that don't meet air-quality standards. Most use **OBD-II plug-in tests** for 1996+ gas and roughly 1997-2002+ diesel light vehicles [S35].
- **A typical OBD test checks:**
  1. The MIL (check-engine light) lights at key-on, engine-off (bulb check).
  2. The MIL is **not** lit or commanded on with the engine running. A lit MIL with an emissions code = fail.
  3. **Readiness monitors** are set. Most programs allow 1 unset monitor for 2001+ and 2 for 1996-2000, set by state rule. Missouri's rule, for example, allows up to 2 unset non-continuous monitors on 1996-2000 gas cars [S36].
  4. The data link connector is present and communicating.
- **Readiness traps:** Monitors reset after a battery disconnect or after codes are cleared. Gas vehicles usually re-set all monitors within about 2 weeks of normal driving; diesels can take 8+ weeks. Programs increasingly read **permanent DTCs**, which clearing codes can't erase [S35]. An "unready" result **right after a repair or battery replacement is normal**. It is not, by itself, proof of a new fault.
- **Waivers:** Most programs grant a waiver after a minimum spend on qualifying emissions repairs plus a failed retest. Examples: PA $450 (adjusted yearly; current figure [UNVERIFIED]), NC per G.S. 20-183.5, VT repair-assistance program.
- **Repair-shop link:** An emissions fail gives a **diagnostic starting point** (DTCs). It does not dictate a parts list. Auditors should expect diagnosis notes such as freeze frame, DTC, test results and root cause before parts like catalytic converters or O2 sensors are replaced.

## 4) Audit implications

### 4.1 Using official reject criteria as a benchmark
1. **Find the jurisdiction first.** Is the vehicle registered in a state that requires periodic safety inspection (§2)? If not (for example a TX non-commercial car, NH, FL or CA), then **no shop finding is a "state inspection failure."** Any such wording is a factual error.
2. **Map each "red/fail/unsafe" DVI item to the state criterion.** Compare the shop's **measurement** to the state minimum:
   - Pads and linings: most states use 2/32 in (PA, VA, NY = 1/16). VT uses the OEM minimum.
   - Tread: 2/32 in nearly everywhere.
   - Rotors: below OEM minimum ("discard") thickness. PA adds 0.015 in scoring; VT adds ½ in cumulative pitting.
   - Steering play: VA uses ¼, ⅜ or ½ in by wheel size, and 0.4 in lash for rack-and-pinion.
   - Windshield: varies a lot. MA: 1 in bruise or 3 in crack in the critical area. NY: 11 in crack in the wiper area. VA: 1½ in chip or star. ME: crack over 6 in in the critical area. VT: intersecting cracks in the critical area.
3. **Classify each finding:**
   - **Meets a state reject criterion:** legally required before the car passes. That still doesn't mean *this* shop must do the repair.
   - **Fails the OEM service limit but not the state criterion:** a legitimate maintenance recommendation, not a legal requirement.
   - **Within limits but approaching:** advisory, the yellow tier. VT explicitly treats 2/32-4/32 tread as an advisory item.
   - **No measurement recorded:** unsupported. In PA the regulation requires brake and tire readings on the work order (§175.29(f)(4)).
4. **Check that wear is consistent:** For example, "pads 2 mm, rotors fine" versus "pads 7 mm, rotors replace." Cross-check with 07-technical-inspection-criteria.md.

### 4.2 Red flags
- The shop calls an item a "state inspection failure" in a state with no safety inspection, or on an exempt vehicle (MO under 10 model years and under 150k miles; DE newest 7 model years; LA personal vehicles after 6/30/2026; TX non-commercial).
- A finding is marked "fail" when the recorded measurement **meets** the state minimum, for example "pads 3/32 — FAIL STATE."
- Failing a car on items the state lists as **advisory only**: airbag light (NY, VA, VT; but MA rejects it), TPMS light (VT advisory), tire pressure (NY), rust on rotor faces (VT), non-structural body holes (VT), or a pad-wear light without measurement (VA).
- Rotors or pads "fail" with no micrometer reading or OEM minimum quoted. In PA or VA, a brake rejection with no wheels pulled or no readings recorded.
- **Required to repair here:** pressure to authorize repairs before releasing the rejection paperwork, or a claim that re-inspection must happen at the same station. NY, PA and VA expressly allow repair elsewhere. MA's free retest is tied to the same station, but repair location is the owner's choice.
- Inspection fee above the statutory cap (VA $20 maximum), or a fee not posted (VT and PA require posting).
- Emissions: catalytic converter or major parts sold without DTC/diagnostic documentation. "Not ready" treated as a failed component. Codes cleared right before the test (permanent DTCs reveal this).
- Repairs done without documented authorization (NY and PA require the owner's permission first).

### 4.3 Consumer view (vehicle owner)
- Ask: "Is this a **state inspection failure** or a **recommendation**?" A courtesy multi-point inspection (MPI/DVI) is **not** a legal requirement. Only items that meet your state's published reject criteria block the sticker.
- Ask for the **measurement** (pad mm or 32nds, tread 32nds, rotor thickness vs. the minimum stamped on the rotor) and compare it with the tables in §3.
- You may get repairs **elsewhere** or do them yourself (NY, PA, VA explicitly). Get a second opinion before costly "fail" repairs.
- Know your retest window: VA 15 days ($1 at the same station); MA 60 days (one free retest at the same station); NY 30 days (only the failed category retested).
- For a lit check-engine light, ask for the DTCs and freeze-frame data. Ask whether an emissions waiver or repair assistance applies.
- Complaints: NY DMV form VS-35; Missouri MSHP MVI; Virginia State Police Safety Division; PennDOT; VT DMV. This file only identifies channels; it does not file anything.

### 4.4 Shop-owner view (auditing your own technicians)
- Set up the DVI with three tiers that match the state: **Reject (state)**, **Recommend (OEM/maintenance)** and **Advise (monitor)**. Never label an OEM-limit item as a "state fail."
- Require **measurements** on every brake and tire finding, as PA law requires and as best practice everywhere. Spot-check techs who "fail" items without numbers.
- Watch for inspector conflict of interest. The inspection license is the station's legal privilege, and inflated rejections risk suspension or revocation (VT, PA, NY, VA all have administrative penalties).
- Post fees and record authorizations. Keep replaced parts available (PA). Give an itemized invoice and the VIR whether the car passes or fails (VT).
- Train on the advisory-only items in your state (airbag light, TPMS, tire pressure, rotor surface rust, etc.).
- Watch for program changes: TX (2025), NH and LA (2026), MO and WV bills, NC emissions. Outdated "state requires" scripts cause compliance and deceptive-practice exposure.

## 6) International (brief)

- **Canada — Ontario Safety Standards Certificate (SSC):** In general, an SSC is needed to put plates on a used vehicle being transferred to a new owner. It is issued by a licensed Motor Vehicle Inspection Station; Ontario says to look for "DriveON" signage [S37]. It certifies that the car met minimum standards **on the day it was inspected**. It is not a warranty of future condition. The 36-day validity and "as-is" sale rules are from memory, **[UNVERIFIED]**. Ontario's Drive Clean emissions program for light vehicles ended 4/1/2019. Nova Scotia and New Brunswick require periodic (2-year) inspections, PEI annual, and Quebec and BC generally none for passenger cars [S38]. The provincial periodic data is secondary.
- **UK — MOT:**
  - First test by the 3rd anniversary of registration, then every year. You can test up to a month minus a day early and keep your renewal date [S39].
  - Results are graded **dangerous, major, minor or advisory**. Dangerous or major = fail. Minor and advisory are notes for monitoring or future repair [S40].
  - You can drive away after a fail only if the current MOT is still valid **and** no dangerous defect was listed. Driving with a dangerous defect risks a fine of up to £2,500, a ban and 3 points [S40].
  - The MOT does not assess engine, clutch or gearbox condition (from general knowledge, **[UNVERIFIED]** this session). The minor/advisory split is a good model for separating "required" from "recommended."
- **EU — Directive 2014/45/EU (periodic roadworthiness):** First test for passenger cars within 4 years of first registration, then at least every 2 years [S38]. Member states may test more often. Defects are graded **minor, major, dangerous**, and the directive sets minimum test items and odometer recording (from general knowledge, **[UNVERIFIED]** against the EUR-Lex text this session).

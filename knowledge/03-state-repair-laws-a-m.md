### Colorado — Motor Vehicle Repair Act, C.R.S. 42-9-101 et seq.
- **Scope:** Applies to self-propelled highway vehicles. It excludes trucks over 8,500 lb GVW, farm tractors, and motorcycles/autocycles (42-9-102). Washing and adding gas or oil are not "repairs."
- **Consent and estimate (42-9-104):**
  - The shop generally needs the customer's written consent before repairs.
  - The customer must receive an estimate of total cost (written, or oral where allowed) that includes the expected completion date, plus a copy.
  - Towed or after-hours drop-offs: the deemed waiver is capped at **$100** of parts and labor.
  - The customer may waive the estimate by signing a prescribed bold statement. Signing the waiver does not itself authorize repairs.
  - If disassembly is needed to estimate, the estimate must show the reassembly cost.
- **Oral consent:** The work order must record the date, time, manner of consent, phone number called, and the names of the people giving and receiving consent.
- **Overage (42-9-106):** The bill may exceed the estimate by the **lesser of 10% or $25** without new consent. Paying an excess is not consent. The shop has no lien for unconsented excess and must release the vehicle once the estimate plus the allowed margin is paid.
- **Invoice (42-9-108):**
  - Parts are itemized by name and number, and each is flagged used, reconditioned, or rebuilt.
  - Labor charge, with the **name or employee number of each mechanic** and the stage of repair each one handled.
  - Subcontracted work is identified.
  - The shop keeps a copy for **3 years**. Flat-rate jobs have exceptions.
- **Parts (42-9-109):** Replaced parts are returned if requested at authorization. Exceptions: body-shop parts, warranty or exchange parts, and airbag components (airbags may not be returned).
- **Prohibited acts (42-9-111):**
  - Charging for unconsented repairs.
  - Saying repairs are **necessary when not a fact**.
  - Saying repairs were done when they were not.
  - Falsely calling a vehicle or part dangerous.
  - Doing emissions repairs the failure does not indicate.
  - No invoice, or not recording the odometer reading.
- **Enforcement:** Violations are deceptive trade practices under the Colorado Consumer Protection Act (per 42-9-112; summarized from secondary text **[UNVERIFIED wording]**).
- **Licensing:** No shop registration or technician licensing found **[UNVERIFIED]**.
- Sources: https://colorado.public.law/statutes/crs_42-9-104 , crs_42-9-106, crs_42-9-108, crs_42-9-109, crs_42-9-111, crs_42-9-102. These are an unofficial mirror of C.R.S., current through fall 2025.

### Connecticut — CGS 14-65e to 14-65j (DMV)
- **Licensing:** Nobody may repair motor vehicles as a business without a DMV **repairer's license** (or a new or used dealer license) (CGS 14-52; repairer fee $340, biennial).
- **Authorization (14-65f):**
  - Before **any** repair, the shop needs a customer-signed written authorization **on an invoice** that includes a written estimate of the **maximum** cost.
  - The shop may not charge above the estimate without oral or written consent. There is **no percentage tolerance**.
  - If the needed repairs are unknown at drop-off, the shop must call with a maximum estimate before starting and record the authorization on the invoice.
  - The same applies when additional repairs are found or the estimate will be exceeded.
  - After-hours drop-off: authorization may be oral but must be recorded.
  - Advance written estimate not required when parts and labor total **less than $50**, unless the customer asks (14-65f(f)). At exactly $50 it is required.
  - The shop also needs a written acknowledgment of the customer's right to choose a licensed shop. This is aimed at insurance claims and may be sent by fax or email.
- **Waiver (14-65g):** Must be written, in prescribed form, and authorize "reasonable and necessary" repairs up to a fixed dollar cap. The shop must keep a written record of oral consents. Before work, the invoice must show customer contact details, drop-off date and time, vehicle year, make, and registration, odometer reading, and the specific repairs requested or the problem described. A diagnosis charge requires prior notice and consent, which must be written if the customer asks or if the charge is $50 or more (14-65g(f)).
- **Invoice and parts (14-65h):** Separately itemized parts and labor, including sublet and warranty work. Used parts must be stated, as must mixed new/used component systems. Warranties must be in writing. Replaced parts are made available if requested **at authorization**; warranty or rebuild parts are for inspection only. The customer gets one copy and the shop keeps one. The statute sets no retention period. DMV regulations (RCSA 14-63 series, which include a records section for dealers and repairers) may, but they were not read because the eRegulations site did not render **[UNVERIFIED]**.
- **Prohibitions (14-65j):** No statement the shop knows or should know is false or misleading, including about the **necessity of repairs** or vehicle condition. No charging for repairs not performed. Violations of (a) or (b) are a class B misdemeanor. Repairs are due the same business day unless the customer is told otherwise or consents.
- **Signs (14-65i):** Customer-rights sign (24"x36"); posted labor rate, storage, and diagnosis charges.
- **Enforcement:** The DMV Commissioner investigates and holds hearings (14-65k). Other violations are infractions (added by P.A. 13-271).
- Source: https://www.cga.ct.gov/current/pub/chap_246.htm

### Florida — Motor Vehicle Repair Act, F.S. 559.901 to 559.9221 (FDACS)
- **Registration (559.904):** Every repair shop must register with **FDACS** (559.903 defines "department" as FDACS) before doing business.
  - Renewal is biennial. Annual fee per location is based on repair employees: $50 / $150 / $300.
  - Exempt: individuals with no employees and no place of business. Local licensees under an equivalent ordinance and ch. 320 dealers skip the fee.
  - Most recent amendment: ch. 2024-137.
- **Estimate (559.905):** For repairs costing **more than $150** (raised from $100 by **ch. 2024-137**), a written estimate is required before diagnostic or repair work.
  - Required contents: shop, customer, and vehicle info including odometer and tag; problem described; flat-rate versus hourly; estimated cost including shop-supply and hazardous-waste fees (any legally mandated fee identified by law); the estimate charge; any guarantee; whether the customer wants replaced parts returned; and the daily storage charge.
  - A separate signed disclosure lets the customer choose a written estimate, no estimate, or a dollar limit.
  - Waivers: written; partial implied waiver for after-hours or third-party drop-off.
- **Overage (559.909):** The shop must notify the customer before exceeding the estimate. The customer may authorize, modify, or cancel, orally or in writing.
  - It is unlawful to charge more than the estimate plus the **greater of $10 or 10%, not to exceed $50**, without authorization.
  - On cancellation, the shop must reassemble the vehicle. It may charge teardown and reassembly costs only if they were disclosed in the estimate.
  - The shop cannot hold the vehicle hostage for unauthorized charges.
  - Parts: inspection or return if requested at authorization, unless a warranty or exchange program applies.
- **Invoice (559.911):** Each labor charge and part is itemized with cost. Free or warranty items are marked. Each replacement part is flagged used, rebuilt, or reconditioned. Also required: odometer reading, description of work, guarantee terms, and the FDACS registration number.
- **Records (559.915):** Estimates and invoices are kept and available to the customer for **12 months**.
- **Unlawful acts (559.920):**
  - Charging for unauthorized repairs.
  - Misrepresenting that repairs were made.
  - Misrepresenting that **parts or repairs are necessary**.
  - Misrepresenting that the vehicle is dangerous.
  - False or misleading statements; false promises to induce authorization.
  - Willful departure from accepted practices and professional standards.
  - Substituting used, rebuilt, salvaged, or straightened parts for new without notice.
  - Work orders without the requested repairs or odometer reading.
  - Unconsented subcontracting.
  - Insurance-glass rebates; failing to give the required notice on whether **ADAS calibration** is needed after glass work.
  - Amended by **ch. 2023-136** (glass/ADAS items) and 2019-73.
- **2023 to 2026 changes:**
  - ch. 2023-136 amended 559.920.
  - ch. 2024-137 raised the estimate threshold to $150 and amended 559.904. Effective date [UNVERIFIED].
  - 2025 SB 92 (crash report/estimate database, front plates) **died** 6/16/2025.
  - 2025 H 807 (motor vehicle repair work) reportedly died in subcommittee [secondary source].
- **Local:** Some counties have historically run their own repair-shop licensing, and 559.904 exempts shops licensed under an equivalent local ordinance from the state fee. Confirmed on county sites:
  - **Miami-Dade** requires shops (mechanical, paint and body) and their mechanics, technicians and apprentices to register with the county (County Code ch. 8A, art. VII). The county page cites a written estimate when charges exceed $150 and authorization before exceeding the estimate by more than $10 or 10%. — https://www.miamidade.gov/global/economy/consumer-protection/motor-vehicle-repairs.page
  - **Broward** requires a county license for repair work done for pay, with at least one certified technician per shop (County Code §20-176.77 et seq.). — https://www.broward.org/Consumer/ConsumerProtection/AutoRepair/Pages/default.aspx
  - **Palm Beach:** the county consumer affairs FAQ says shops register with FDACS; no county license found. — https://discover.pbcgov.org/publicsafety/consumeraffairs/pages/faq.aspx
  - **Pinellas:** the county page points to FDACS and does not mention county registration; it still cites the old $100 threshold.
  - **Hillsborough:** not checked **[UNVERIFIED]**.
- Sources: https://www.flsenate.gov/Laws/Statutes/2025/559.905 , /559.909 , /559.911 , /559.915 , /559.920 , /559.904 , /559.903 ; https://www.flsenate.gov/Session/Bill/2025/92 ; https://pinellas.gov/motor-vehicle-repair-101/

### Hawaii — HRS ch. 437B; HAR ch. 16-87 (Motor Vehicle Repair Industry Board, DCCA)
- **Licensing:**
  - Motor vehicle repair dealers must register with the Board.
  - **Mechanics must pass certification tests** in specialty areas to be registered. Automotive specialties: engine, tune-up, automatic transmission, brakes, electrical, suspension/steering, HVAC, manual drivetrain. There are also truck and motorcycle categories.
  - Registration is limited to the certified specialties. A mechanic may work for pay only as or for a registered dealer (HAR 16-87-25, -26). Helpers must be supervised.
  - Advertising must include the dealer registration number. Advertising specialties the dealer is not certified for is prohibited (HAR 16-87-33).
  - Exempt: government employees, and in-house fleet employees (HRS 437B-2).
- **Estimate (437B-15):** A written estimate for parts and labor is required **before any job**, unless the customer waives it in writing.
  - Overage without prior written or oral consent: **15% if the estimate is under $100; 10% if over $100**. Consent must come after the shortfall is found and before the extra work.
  - A reasonable estimate fee is allowed if disclosed first (HAR 16-87-23).
  - Non-OEM crash parts must be identified on the estimate and accepted by the owner's signature.
  - Consent-to-increase and waiver forms must be on board-prescribed forms and may not be combined with other forms (HAR 16-87-22).
- **Invoice (437B-13):** All work, including warranty work, goes on an invoice. Service and parts are listed separately with separate subtotals and tax. Crash, used, rebuilt, or reconditioned parts must be stated.
- **Parts (437B-14; HAR 16-87-24):** Returned if requested **when the work order is taken**. Warranty-return parts are shown on request. The dealer may decline to return a transmission, differential, or engine block.
- **Records (437B-16; HAR 16-87-17):** **2 years**. Covers invoices, estimates, consent forms, and work orders.
- **Prohibited (437B-11):**
  - Untrue or misleading statements.
  - Work orders without the requested repairs or odometer reading.
  - Fraud; gross negligence.
  - **Willful departure from accepted practices or professional standards**.
  - False promises to induce authorization.
  - Undisclosed subcontracting.
  - Rebuilt-vehicle work not to OEM procedures.
  - Using unlicensed shops.
- Sources: https://data.capitol.hawaii.gov/hrscurrent/Vol10_Ch0436-0474/HRS0437B/HRS_0437B-0015.htm (also -0011, -0013, -0014, -0016, -0017, -0002); HAR 16-87 PDF at https://cca.hawaii.gov/wp-content/uploads/2026/02/har_87-c1.pdf ; board page https://cca.hawaii.gov/pvl/boards/motor-vehicle-repair/
- 2023 to 2026 changes: none found [limited search].

### Idaho — general AG repair rules (not vehicle-specific)
- IDAPA 04.02.01 (Idaho Rules of Consumer Protection, under the Consumer Protection Act, Idaho Code 48-601 et seq.) applies to repairs of any goods, including vehicles:
  - Rule 120: unreasonably understating an estimate is unlawful. No estimate is required.
  - Rule 121: if an estimate was given, the shop needs oral or written authorization before unforeseen but necessary work that would **unreasonably** increase the price.
  - Rule 122: an express price limit cannot be exceeded without consent.
  - Rule 130: no claiming repairs are **necessary when not**, no performing and charging for **unnecessary repairs**, no false claims that repairs were done or that the vehicle is dangerous.
  - Rule 131: an itemized bill **on request** (hours times rate or flat rate; parts flagged used or rebuilt).
  - Rule 132: return or inspection of old parts on request.
- No shop or technician licensing.
- Source: https://adminrules.idaho.gov/rules/current/04/040201.pdf

### Illinois — Automotive Repair Act, 815 ILCS 306 (AG)
- **Scope:** Consumer auto repair. Excludes commercial fleet repair (2 or more vehicles) and business service contracts (306/10). Collision and body shops are covered instead by the Automotive Collision Repair Act, 815 ILCS 308 (306/83). Firm-price install jobs (tires, batteries, oil, lube) are excluded.
- **Estimate (306/15, /20):**
  - Work over **$100** needs specific authorization after disclosures.
  - The shop gives either a written parts-and-labor estimate (cannot exceed it by **more than 10%** without oral or written consent) or a written price limit (cannot exceed without consent).
  - The estimate must include **all charges, including estimate and diagnostic fees**; the major parts, flagged **new or used**; the labor basis (flat-rate manual, actual time, or vehicle condition); whether each repair is **"required or suggested"**; any disassembly or reassembly cost if the customer declines; and the date, odometer, and promised delivery date.
  - Statutory waiver form choices: written estimate; call me above $X; or no estimate.
- **Oral consent (306/25, /35):** Note on the work order or estimate **and** invoice: date, time, name of the authorizer, phone number called, the additional parts and labor, and the total added cost.
- **Invoice (306/50):** Business name and address, date, odometer, customer, vehicle, guarantee terms. All work described, each major part identified, parts and labor listed separately with subtotals and tax, and extra charges itemized.
- **Records:** Copies of estimates and invoices kept **2 years** and available to the AG; electronic format is allowed (306/50(d), 306/65).
- **Unlawful acts (306/80):**
  - Charging for parts not installed or labor not done.
  - **Charging for unnecessary repairs.** These are repairs with **no reasonable basis**. A reasonable basis means the repair is consistent with manufacturer or legal specifications, or follows usual and customary practice, or was requested by the consumer (including after the technician explained why a non-spec recommendation was made).
  - Altering a vehicle to create a repair need.
  - Collecting from both the consumer and a warranty provider.
  - Charging for unauthorized repairs.
- **Remedies:** Lien barred for unauthorized amounts (306/75). A knowing, persistent pattern at one location is a Consumer Fraud Act violation enforceable by the AG and State's Attorneys (306/85).
- **No statutory return-of-parts right** found in the Act.
- Required sign (306/60) summarizes the $100 and 10% rights.
- 2023 to 2026 changes: none. All sections show P.A. 90-426 (eff. 1-1-98); 306/83 is from P.A. 93-565.
- Source (official public file repository): https://ftp.ilga.gov/ILCS/Ch%200815/Act%200306/ (files 081503060K15.html, K20, K25, K35, K50, K65, K80, K83, K85)

### Iowa — Motor Vehicle Service Trade Practices Act, Iowa Code ch. 537B (AG)
- **Scope:** Consumer vehicles used primarily for farm or personal use. Excludes vehicles over 12,000 lb GVWR.
- **Estimate disclosure (537B.3):** On a written authorization, a prescribed conspicuous notice says the consumer is entitled to a written or oral estimate if expected cost exceeds **$50**, and the bill cannot exceed the estimate by more than **10%** without approval. The consumer initials one choice: written estimate, oral estimate, no estimate, or call me above $X. The form must show the date, supplier, consumer name and phone number, and anticipated completion date. For oral authorization, the shop must tell the consumer about the estimate right and note the response.
- **Deceptive acts (537B.6):**
  - Not getting oral or written authorization for unforeseen work that pushes cost **more than 10% (excluding tax)** over the estimate.
  - For jobs under $50 with no estimate, not getting authorization before the total exceeds $50.
  - Undisclosed teardown or reassembly charges.
  - Charging unauthorized work.
  - **Representing that repairs are necessary when not**; that repairs were done when not; or that the vehicle is dangerous when not.
  - Materially understating the estimate.
  - Not providing an **itemized list of repairs and parts, flagged used, remanufactured, or rebuilt, with costs, labor charge, and identity of the individual who did the work**.
  - Not tendering replaced parts, unless the reuse or return was disclosed beforehand.
  - Not providing an itemized drop-off receipt on request.
  - Not disclosing subcontracting on request.
  - Aftermarket crash parts must be disclosed on a written estimate (537B.4).
- **Records:** Written estimates and disclosure forms kept **9 months** (61 IAC 29.1).
- Enforcer: AG under the Consumer Fraud Act, Iowa Code 714.16.
- 2023 to 2026 changes: none (last amended 2012).

### Maine — 29-A M.R.S. §§1801–1811 (Title 29-A, ch. 15, subch. 2: Repair)
- **Scope (§1801):** "repair facility" means a motor vehicle repair shop serving the public for pay. "Repair" includes body work, painting, storage and towing; selling fuel is excluded. Enacted by PL 1993 c. 683; amended by PL 2005 c. 189 (shop supplies) and the 2023 citizen initiative (telematics and mechanical-data definitions).

### Maryland — Com. Law 14-1001 to 14-1011 (AG Consumer Protection Division)
- **Estimate (14-1002):** For work billed **more than $50**, on the customer's request the shop gives a written statement before work. ### Michigan — Motor Vehicle Service and Repair Act, MCL 257.1301 et seq. (Michigan Dept. of State)
- **Licensing:**
  - Repair facilities must register with the Department of State. The registration number must appear on every estimate, invoice, waiver, and other customer document (257.1332(6)).
  - **Mechanics:** No one may work as a specialty or master mechanic without a Department of State certificate, renewed annually. Some specialties (engine tune-up and performance, electrical, brakes) require periodic retesting, approved training, or recognized certification such as ASE (257.1311).
- **Estimate (257.1332):**
  - A written estimate itemizing parts and labor "as closely as possible" is required before repairs.
  - **Not required when the total for services and parts is less than $50** unless the customer asks (257.1334a, as amended by 2016 PA 430). At exactly $50 it is required.
  - Overage needs "knowing" written or oral consent, obtained after the shortfall is found and before the extra work. Without a waiver, consent is not required for an overage of no more than the **lesser of 10% or $50** unless the customer asked for it.
  - Diagnosis cost must be in the estimate.
  - If the customer declines, reasonable costs to restore the vehicle must be itemized in writing.
  - Waiver: 14-point bold capitals in statutory wording, with a stated dollar cap.
  - The statute does not mention electronic estimates or signatures.
- **Final repair statement (257.1334; 257.1307(m)):**
  - Repairs **needed** (per the facility), **requested**, and **authorized**.
  - Estimated and actual cost.
  - Work performed, with replaced parts flagged new OEM, new, used, rebuilt, OEM surplus, or reconditioned.
  - Certification that repairs were properly completed, or an explanation.
  - **Name of the mechanic(s) who diagnosed and repaired**.
  - Signed by the owner or a designee.
- **Parts (257.1333):** Replaced parts **must be returned** (exceptions: size or weight exemptions, warranty or exchange, fuel tanks). Warranty or exchange parts must be offered for inspection. Unreturned parts are held at least 2 business days, longer if disputed. On request, the shop must explain why a part was defective. The customer is notified of these rights in 12-point bold or by a sign.
- **Prohibited (257.1307a):**
  - Charging for repairs not performed.
  - **Performing unnecessary repairs**, unless the customer insists after being told they are unnecessary.

  - Unauthorized repairs.
  - Passing off used or rebuilt parts as new, and failing to give prior written disclosure of used parts.
  - After a paid diagnosis, failing to disclose the malfunction, the recommended remedy, and the **tests or procedures used**.
  - Also 257.1307: false or misleading statements, waivers used to evade the Act, blank documents, and similar.

### Minnesota — Truth in Repairs Act, Minn. Stat. 325F.56 to 325F.66 (AG)
- **Scope:** Covers "repairs" over **$100** to consumer motor vehicles, appliances, and dwellings (325F.56 subd. 2; amended 2024 c 114). The invoice section uses a **$50** threshold and also covers warranty, service-contract, or insurance work of any value (325F.60).
- **Estimate (325F.58):** On request, a written estimate before repairs. It lists parts flagged new, used, rebuilt, reconditioned, or replated, and **OEM or non-OEM** for new parts (other than glass); labor; tax; storage; and total. An estimate fee requires prior disclosure and authorization.
- **Overage:** The shop may not charge more than **110%** of the estimate. A narrow exception applies when additional necessary work is found that the shop did not unreasonably fail to foresee and disclose. The 110% limit also governs vehicle release (325F.61).
- **Unnecessary repairs (325F.59):** No charges for unauthorized repairs. No performing repairs the shop **knows or has reason to know are unnecessary**, unless the customer authorizes them after being told they are unnecessary.
- **Invoice (325F.60):** Date; shop; all repairs; itemized parts, labor, tax, and other charges; parts flagged new, used, rebuilt, or reconditioned and OEM or not; odometer; and **the symptoms as described by the customer**. Mailed within 2 business days if there is no face-to-face pickup.
- **Parts and records (325F.62):** Replaced parts are returned if requested **before repairs start**; warranty or exchange parts may be examined for 5 business days. Name, address, estimates, and invoices are kept **1 year**. Required sign states the $100, written-estimate, and 10% rules, and notes that **oral estimates are not subject to the cap**.
- **Remedies (325F.63):** Return of the vehicle without paying excess charges; damages; attorney fees; punitive damages up to 3 times charges. Violations are also consumer-fraud violations enforceable under 8.31.
- Exempt: insurer or service contract pays 90% or more, or free warranty repairs (325F.64). The Act supersedes less-strict local ordinances (325F.65).

## 4) Audit implications: common patterns an auditor can check

### 4.1 Decide which state's rules apply
1. **The shop's physical location usually controls.** Every statute here regulates the repair facility or shop operating in the state, not the customer's home state. A Florida resident who gets a repair in Georgia falls under Georgia law (UDAP only).
2. **Mobile or remote work:** Use the state where the work was performed. Florida covers mobile shops, and Hawaii covers mobile repair facilities under the dealer's registration.
3. **Check the scope exclusions before applying a rule.** Examples:
   - Colorado excludes trucks over 8,500 lb and motorcycles.
   - Iowa excludes vehicles over 12,000 lb GVWR and covers only farm or personal-use vehicles.
   - Illinois excludes collision shops, fleet work, and firm-price tire, battery, or oil installs.
   - Minnesota excludes insurer- or warranty-paid jobs and applies only above $100 (estimates) or $50 (invoices).
   - Massachusetts c. 100A registration covers collision and glass shops only.
4. **Fallback:** If a state has no repair statute, apply the UDAP act. The common UDAP themes are misrepresenting necessity, charging for work not done, and concealing material facts. The FTC Act baseline is in file 01. Many state UDAP acts treat "repairs represented as necessary when not" as deceptive, even without a repair-specific list. Alaska and Idaho say so explicitly.

### 4.2 Checklist (map each document field to a rule)
| Check | What to look for | States with an explicit rule |
|---|---|---|
| **Estimate existed before work** | Estimate date or time earlier than repair start or tech clock-in; dollar threshold met | CO, CT (> $50), FL (> $150), HI (all jobs), IL (> $100), IA (> $50, if chosen), MD (> $50 on request), MA (all), MI (≥ $50), MN (> $100 on request) |
| **Waiver / disclosure form** | Signed waiver or choice form (written estimate / no estimate / call me above $X), with the cap honored | CO, CT, FL, HI, IL, IA, MI, MA |
| **Final ≤ estimate + allowed overage** | Compute (final − estimate) ÷ estimate, pre-tax where specified | CO lesser of 10%/$25; CT $0; FL greater of $10/10%, max $50; HI 15% (< $100) / 10%; IL 10%; IA 10% excl. tax; MD 10%; MA $10; MI lesser of 10%/$50; MN 10% |
| **Re-authorization documented** | For any overage or added repair: date, time, who authorized, phone number, what was added, new total | CO, CT, IL, MA (exact statement), MD (towed or after-hours), HI (board form) |
| **"Required vs. suggested" labeling** | Each recommendation labeled | IL (estimate must say required or suggested) |
| **Necessity basis** | Each recommendation tied to manufacturer spec, measured condition, or customer request | IL's "reasonable basis" definition is the best audit yardstick. FL, CT, CO, IA, MI, MA, ID, AK, and HI all ban misrepresenting necessity. MI and MN ban performing known-unnecessary repairs unless the customer insists after being told |
| **Diagnosis disclosure** | After a paid diagnosis: malfunction found, remedy recommended, **tests used** | MI (257.1307a(g)) |
| **Dangerous-condition claims** | Safety-critical flags backed by measurements | FL, CO, IA, MA, ID ban false "dangerous" claims |
| **Invoice itemization** | Parts listed separately (name, number, price); labor (hours × rate, or flat-rate flagged); fees; tax | All statute states. MA requires actual vs. flat-rate hours. IL and HI require separate parts and labor subtotals |
| **Used / rebuilt / non-OEM flag** | Each non-new part flagged; new parts OEM or not | All statute states flag used or rebuilt. MN flags OEM vs. non-OEM. MI requires prior written disclosure. HI requires signed acceptance of non-OEM crash parts. IA requires aftermarket crash-part disclosure |
| **Who did the work** | Technician name or ID on the invoice | CO, IA, MI |
| **Odometer recorded** | On the work order or invoice | CO, CT, FL, IL, MA, MN; HI (work order) |
| **Customer complaint recorded** | Symptoms or requested repairs on the work order | CT, FL, MA, MN (on invoice), HI, MI |
| **Replaced parts** | Return or inspection offered; request timing | Must tender: IA, MD, MI. On request: CO, CT, FL, HI, MN (before start), MA, ID |
| **Shop / tech credentials** | Registration number on documents; tech certification | FL (FDACS registration number on invoice), MI (registration number on all documents; certified mechanics), HI (registered dealer and certified mechanics), CT (DMV repairer license) |
| **Record retention** | Ask for records within the period | CO 3 yr; MI 3 yr; HI 2 yr; IL 2 yr; FL 12 mo; MN 1 yr; IA 9 mo |

### 4.3 How to tell a consumer which rules apply (suggested skill wording)
- Ask for **the shop's state (and city or county)**, the **date of service**, and whether the shop is a **dealer, independent, collision/glass, or fleet** shop.
- If the state has a repair statute, cite the specific rights: estimate threshold, overage tolerance, parts return, and itemized invoice. If not, explain that general consumer-protection (UDAP) law still prohibits misrepresenting that work is needed or charging for work not performed.
- Point the consumer to the **regulator**:
  - FL: FDACS.
  - MI: Michigan Dept. of State.
  - CT: DMV Dealers & Repairers.
  - HI: Motor Vehicle Repair Industry Board / DCCA.
  - Everywhere else: the state AG's consumer protection division.
- The skill should only point the consumer there. It should never file a complaint for them.
- Flag findings as **"possible noncompliance"**, not legal conclusions. Thresholds change. FL changed in 2024, and MI and MN were amended in 2024 to 2026.
